Experimental design, paper campaign

CapEvolve Experimental Design

This page is the design for the CapEvolve paper campaign. It lists what is frozen, what is still pending and what decides each pending item. Every number comes from a source record in the campaign repository, cited by path. Forecasts are arithmetic on stated inputs and are labelled as such.

Summary and decision table

The campaign asks whether reported capability-optimization gains survive scorer correction and fresh tasks, then compares search methods at matched budget on two suites. The paper route (systems, method or empirical) is chosen after the evidence. A simpler method winning is a valid outcome.

Feasibility check

The frozen design was run through the measured costs. Three results need a decision before launch. Inputs: one Gemma SpreadsheetBench rollout is about 41k tokens and about 3 minutes (decisions.md, task 58687, 41.5k tokens, 177 s); RITS concurrency is 4 per deployment (campaign.yaml).

Pending: wall time

At 3 minutes per rollout, one deployment completes 5,760 rollouts in 72 h at concurrency 4 and 46,080 at concurrency 32 (72 x 60 / 3 x slots), if latency holds under load. Suite A now has 19 search or best-of-K arms: CapEvolve hill-climb and agent-optimize, 16 non-CapEvolve competitors (including the WikiSkill reimplementation and four harness/context methods) and best-of-K. At 3 seeds x 600 rollouts that is 34,200 search rollouts, about 53 h at concurrency 32 (427 h at 4). The Suite A final adds up to 18,600 rollouts and the ablations 16,200 to 18,000. Together that is 69,000 to 70,800, about 1.5 times the 46,080 capacity of the Gemma deployment, before E03 and E17. Suite B runs on a different deployment and can run in parallel.

What decides it: a latency measurement at concurrency 32, how many competitor adaptations succeed, and which arms and ablations are cut. The 3-minute figure comes from one 6-turn task at low concurrency. GEPA and SkillOpt are already running, so they are the first rows to consume the budget.

Pending: non-RITS token cap

The optimizer ceiling is 4M tokens per search. About 15 methods x 2 suites x 3 seeds x 4M is about 360M, more than three times the 100M cap. Counting the arms on this page (Suite A 19 arms, Suite B 6 to 12 arms, 3 seeds each) gives 75 to 93 searches and 300M to 372M at the ceiling. If the 25% reserve also applies to tokens, 75M is available before final evaluation, which fits at most 18 full-ceiling searches. GPT-5.5 transfer rollouts and the GPT-5.5 judge for Suite B also count against the cap: at a similar 41k tokens per rollout (unmeasured for GPT-5.5), 100M is about 2,400 rollouts.

Handling: the gateway token guard monitors spend. Suite A has priority over Suite B when the cap tightens. What decides the rest: measured optimizer tokens per search in the pilots (many methods spend well under the ceiling), and whether the reserve applies to tokens.

Pending: how far 600 rollouts go

Rule P screens each candidate on val-40. With the simulation's 5 trials per task a single paired screen is about 200 candidate rollouts, so 600 rollouts fund roughly three screens before any fresh-trial confirmation. Trials per screen are not yet frozen. The pilot decides it and decides whether 600 is enough to separate methods.

Research questions

RQ1

How much of reported capability-optimization gain survives scorer correction and fresh held-out tasks?

Re-score history under both scorers (done, zero LLM cost). E03 compliance diagnostic. Re-measure seed and champions on a fresh final set. Experiments: E00, E03, E10.

RQ2

Under matched budgets, how do hill-climb, agent-optimize and official competitors compare?

Same surface, models, feedback, evaluator and budget (matched track) plus native designs at equal total budget (practical track). Controls: seed, compliance instruction, one-shot repair, best-of-K. Experiment: E10.

RQ3

Which mechanisms explain any gain: gate, footprint masking, DAG and merge, active evaluation, surface width?

Development-data ablations at equal budgets. Experiments: E11 to E16.

RQ4

Do frozen artifacts transfer across models and benchmarks?

No re-optimization. Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, GPT-5.5, SpreadsheetBench 2 if available. Smaller-optimized vs larger-unoptimized at measured deployment cost. Experiments: E20 to E22.

RQ5

Do the measurement controls prevent false improvements: null edits, injected faults, gold isolation?

Null-edit candidates through each gate, injected fault cases, gold-isolation negative test, footprint false-exclusion audit. Experiments: E04 to E07 (IDs proposed).

Paper route. Chosen after the evidence: systems (A), method (B) or empirical study (C). The novelty screen leaves only narrow claims (S1 to S6 in literature/novelty_matrix.md section 3). Route B needs S1 or S2 to show a gain under matched budget. Otherwise route C is built on S3 to S5. S5 (hill-climb vs agent-optimize at matched total budget) does not require a win: a simpler method winning is reported as such.

Reward

  • Primary: LibreOffice 25.8 recalculated hard score (hard_restriction). Optimization and selection use this reward.
  • Secondary: as-saved hard score (hard_no_recalc), reported for comparability with the literature. Both are always reported.
  • Exclusions: tasks the official comparator cannot pass even when the gold file is the output are excluded and listed. Verified-400: 4 tasks are unscorable on any path (130-9, 283-32, 45944, 49300, all in the SkillOpt test split). With LibreOffice 25.8.7.3, 19 of 400 fail gold-as-output (15 beyond those 4). The per-split breakdown for 25.8 is pending; the 7.1.8.1 breakdown (8 train, 2 val, 18 test) is not the one used.
  • Why recalculation: 282 of 400 gold answers contain formula cells. The as-saved scorer gives 0 of 30 on correct formula outputs with no cached values; recalculation gives 25 to 30 of 30 depending on engine. As-saved rewards writing literals instead of formulas.

  • Target: Gemma-4-31B-it on RITS, maximum 30 turns.
  • Gold-free agent sandbox (issue #782). Historically the gold workbooks sat in the directory mounted read-write into the agent container. The patch (patches/adapter_gold_dir.patch by bench-steward) gives the agent a gold-free copy and keeps the scorer on the pristine tree. A negative test (E06) must fail to read gold before any search rollout. Patch landed: pending. Historical optimizer sessions also read gold workbooks and one read a prior sealed-test evaluation (issue #784), so the optimizer side is isolated too.

Work the audit says retail needs

  • The adapter hard-codes DOMAIN = "airline". It needs a domain parameter and a retail seed capability (policy and tools). pending
  • Evaluator coupling. adapter.apply() replaces the tau2 domain constructor with one built from the candidate tools.py. tau2's evaluator uses that constructor for both the predicted and the gold environment, so a candidate that weakens state-changing tools moves both sides. The audit's proposed fix keeps the pristine constructor for evaluate_simulation and uses the candidate constructor only for the agent's environment. The design requires this fix, tested by an injected-fault case (E05). Patch status: pending
  • Candidate tools.py executes inside the evaluator process. Isolation is needed so edits cannot alter evaluation state (defect D08).
  • Adapter settings differ from tau2 defaults (max_steps=100 vs 200, and rollouts with a leaked ###STOP### are dropped as infra noise). The design either aligns these with tau2 defaults or discloses them in every table; which one is pending. Dropped rollouts must follow the missingness rules in the statistics section.
  • The user simulator is an LLM, so noise is high. The audit expects retail to be unable to certify +0.02 effects, as for airline.

Two tracks

Matched algorithm track.

Same editable surface, models, feedback, evaluator and budget. Isolates the search algorithm.

Practical track.

Each method in its native design, same total budget. Shows what a user gets from the shipped method.

Every table and figure is marked with its track. Matching across tracks is never implied.

Per-search budget and promotion rule P

Each search gets 600 target rollouts on dev data, counting every evaluation including screens and confirmations, plus at most 4M non-cache-read optimizer tokens. The same budget applies to every method, including best-of-K.

Rule P (CapEvolve arms)

protocol/analysis_plan.md section 8; operating characteristics from analysis/out/sim_promotion.md.

  1. Screen on val with the paired gate at k_se=1 on the full unmasked delta vector (footprint_gate=False).
  2. Confirm: re-evaluate candidate and incumbent with fresh trials (new seeds, at least 10 per task) on the same val tasks. Promote only if mean delta is at least 0.02 and delta / paired SE is at least 2.0.
  3. The incumbent's confirmation measurement is the baseline for the next iteration.
  4. Budgets count confirmation calls.
  5. The deployed artifact is the last promoted candidate, not the best-looking dev score.

Competitors keep their native acceptance rules. Their results are labelled "native acceptance" and the gate strictness difference is reported, because gate strictness is a controlled factor (methodology rule R12).

Pending: confirmation cost

10 confirmation trials per task on val-40 is 400 rollouts per side. The simulation charges about 0.58 to 0.63 of one screening evaluation per iteration, which assumes confirmation only for candidates that pass the screen. Whether 600 rollouts leave room for the confirmation count in rule P is settled by the pilot.

Final campaign

  • Scored once. All frozen artifacts and seeds are scored once, interleaved in randomized order, in one short window. This controls provider drift. Model snapshot ids and timestamps are recorded per rollout.
  • Trials. Suite A: 2 per task. Suite B: 4 per task. Every confirmation and final trial uses new seeds. Whether the providers honour seeds is verified first.
  • One shared baseline measurement per suite serves every contrast. This removes the 76.5 / 76.8 and 38.5 / 38.6 discrepancies seen in the history.
  • Test-event ledger. A campaign-level ledger records every scoring of a final set (artifact hash, evaluator hash, time). The per-run test seal is not a campaign barrier (defect D07), and reuse_baseline resets it, so the ledger is outside the run directory.
  • Evaluator isolation. The final evaluator and labels are not readable from optimizer workspaces. Actual read permissions are audited and recorded.
  • Reporting. All registered arms and seeds are reported, whether or not they look good. A crashed or aborted search stays in the denominator and its champion is the seed artifact unless a candidate had already been promoted.
  • Gain. Reported only from fresh re-measurement of the seed and the final champion. Dev-val gains and gate deltas are never results (they run about 4x the new-task gain under the one-stage gate).

Rules

  • Unit of inference: the task, clustered by family; trials averaged only within a task.
  • Seeds are a separate level. S=3 gives 2 degrees of freedom, so pipeline intervals use t(2) = 4.30. Seed-level percentile bootstraps are not the headline for S below 8.
  • Intervals: below 30 families the headline CI is the cluster-robust t interval (CR1), not the percentile bootstrap. Sign-flip randomization is a declared robustness check.
  • Multiplicity: Holm step-down at FWER 0.05 within a suite. Each suite is its own family. A cross-suite claim needs both to pass. Unlisted contrasts are exploratory.
  • "Win" language only if the Holm-adjusted p is below 0.05 and the CI excludes 0 under the pessimistic missingness bound. Otherwise "no detectable difference at the MDE of X".
  • Claims are artifact-level (about the S champions) unless S is at least 5; pipeline-level claims need S of at least 5.
  • Never reported: SplitResult.stderr / combined_stderr (double counts within-task noise; overstates new-task variance by 12 to 33% at the assumed kappa and fixed-task variance by 1.2 to 8.6x).
  • SD labels its unit; SE names its sampling process; every table cell states n tasks, n families, n seeds, n trials per task.

Missingness

  • Failures are classified by a frozen deterministic classifier, blind to arm and reward. Unclassified counts as failure.
  • Exogenous failures (429, 5xx, gateway timeout, container pull, host OOM): up to 3 further attempts, same policy on every arm, triggered by error class only, cost charged to the arm. Exhausted retries make a missing cell.
  • Artifact-induced failures count as a trial with reward 0. They are never dropped.
  • Complete-case analysis only if the missing fraction is at most 5% per arm and missing counts are not associated with arm (Fisher exact p at least 0.10). Otherwise bounds only.
  • Pessimistic and optimistic bounds always sit next to the estimate. A contrast is "robust" only if the pessimistic-bound CI excludes 0.
  • Confirmatory evaluation needs at least 95% valid (arm, task) cells after retries, else the suite is re-executed or declared invalid for the headline. Coverage is computed on the paired intersection.

Cost accounting

Source: analysis_plan.md section 9. Money is metered from provider usage logs.

  • Per role: optimizer, target, simulator, judge, diagnostics. Optimizer-model cost and deployed-target cost are separate columns. A combined column appears only when both are fully priced.
  • Optimization cost C_opt(method, seed): proposer plus target rollouts on dev data (screening and confirmation included) plus judges and diagnostics. Tokens, USD, rollouts and wall hours, mean and SD over seeds. Failed and aborted searches are included.
  • Cost per usable champion for "run S searches, deploy the best on dev" is S x mean C_opt. Its accuracy has a winner's-curse bias, so it gets its own final-set row.
  • Deployment cost: metered target cost of one deployed task attempt on the final tasks, including failures and retries; also cost per success.
  • Net value per task v(a) = R x p_a - c_a, with the task reward R declared in advance. R=0 gives the cost-only view.
  • Break-even volume V* = C_opt / (v(champion) - v(baseline)) tasks. Defined only when the lower CI bound of the value difference is above 0; otherwise "no break-even established" with the denominator's interval. Never a delta-method interval when the denominator CI includes 0.
  • Unknown prices: RITS targets and GPT-5.5 are "unknown". Sums that include them are flagged "lower bound". Token counts and wall time are always reported. Optimizer cost is shown at proxy price, labelled as proxy.

Bound on optimizer spend (arithmetic on the proxy price). One search at the 4M-token ceiling costs between $8 (all input at $2/MTok) and $40 (all output at $10/MTok). The measured input/output mix from the pilot decides where inside this range a search falls. Thirty searches at the ceiling would be between $240 and $1,200, so the $1000 cap can bind. pending pilot mix.
