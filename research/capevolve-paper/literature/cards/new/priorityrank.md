# priorityrank (SHORT)
- Title: Towards Direct Evaluation of Harness Optimizers via Priority Ranking (benchmark SHOR: 182 human-verified optimization scenarios)
- Authors: Kai Tzu-iunn Ong, Minseok Kang, Dongwook Choi, et al. (14; Yonsei LangAGI Lab / Samsung). arXiv 2605.22505 read v3; first 2026-05-21, latest 2026-10-06. Venue: arXiv comment "Accepted to NeurIPS 2026 (Main)"; PDF header "40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026)". Code/data: [CODE & DATA] link on p.1 (URL not extracted).
- Surface: harness = prompt, memory, workflow, tool (4 component classes, as in Hao et al.).
- Method: evaluation design, not optimizer. Optimizers rank components by their potential to improve/hinder performance when updated; labels derived from actual delta-SR from component updates plus annotators (Kendall's W>0.5 filter retains 238 of 595 candidate harnesses -> 182 SHOR scenarios); optimizer agents are Claude Code, Gemini-CLI, etc.
- Held-out: optimization runs of 10 iterations with 4 validation tasks per iteration and held-out test of 40 instances (App. p.16); Meta-Harness-style runs 20 iterations, 4-12 tasks per iteration, 20 held-out test instances, J=595 candidate harnesses retained (App. p.17).
- Headline: ranking performance correlates with real multi-step optimization ability (rho=0.602 in-domain; ~0.63 OOD, abstract/contributions p.2). Notes optimizers often make erroneous intermediate updates (hardcoding, redundant duplication, SR-degrading), invisible to end-improvement evaluation (p.1-2).
- Budget: cheap non-iterative evaluation; correlation experiments limited by budget (App. p.26).
- RELEVANCE: LOW-MED. Orthogonal (evaluating the optimizer's judgement). Relevant to (f): supports analyzing the controller agent's step-level decisions; may be cited for the observation that optimizer updates are frequently erroneous (motivates verified gating/merging, b). Dataset is a possible probe for CapEvolve's controller quality, not a competitor.
