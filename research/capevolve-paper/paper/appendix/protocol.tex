\section{Campaign Protocol: Gate Simulation, Power and Analysis Plan}
\label{app:sim}

We chose rule P before any main \capevolve{} seed, from a budget-exact simulation on the real promotion and gate code. We then re-ran it at each suite's observed difficulty: the seed artifact's mean reward and trial-level variance on the search split (Suite A: 40 validation tasks, 600 rollouts; Suite B: 49 optimization tasks, 550 rollouts; Suite C: 20 tasks), one screening trial per task, about ten candidates per search and 22{,}400 searches per cell. The pre-declared criterion was to maximise the expected true new-task gain of the final artifact subject to at most 0.15 probability of a false promotion in an all-null search. A search screens 6.7 to 9.4 candidates on average. Table~\ref{tab:sim} gives rule P and the one-stage gate. The false-promotion probability is per search, about one percent per candidate. The gain column is the expected true new-task gain of the final artifact under a stress mix of edits (below). The one-stage gate has the larger expected gain and the higher power, and it falsely promotes in about half of all-null searches; rule P gives up power to avoid that. Five confirmation trials at $z_{\mathrm{conf}}=1.645$ would cost $2\cdot 40\cdot 5=400$ of the 600 rollouts per confirmation on Suite A instead of 240, so fewer candidates are screened and the expected gain halves; on Suite B the 490 rollouts exceed the 452 left after the baseline and one screen, so that setting could never promote.

The simulated edit mix is a stress assumption, not a measured rate. Of the proposed edits, 60\% are null (true population effect 0), 25\% are positive (uniform $+1$ to $+6$ points) and 15\% are negative (uniform $-1$ to $-6$ points). Half of the edits are diffuse (all tasks affected, per-task SD 0.10) and half are sparse (30\% of tasks affected, per-task SD 0.25), with independent per-task realisations on the selection tasks and on new tasks. Expected gains in Table~\ref{tab:sim} are conditional on this mix.

Empirical check (E22). We evaluated the unmodified Suite D seed 40 times on the 49 optimization tasks (one trial per task per evaluation) and replayed the real gate code on null candidates drawn from that pool, using distinct evaluations for the screen and each confirmation. The one-stage unmasked rule (screen at one standard error) passes a null candidate \nullOneStagePer{}\% of the time and promotes at least one of ten null candidates in \nullOneStageTen{}\% of searches (bootstrap interval \nullOneStageTenLo{} to \nullOneStageTenHi{}\%). Rule P as run promotes in \nullRulePTen{}\% (\nullRulePTenLo{} to \nullRulePTenHi{}\%), so it lowers false acceptance about \nullRatio{} times. The simulation above gave \simKoneFalseB{} and \simPFalseB{} for the same quantities on Suite B, so it understated both rates, rule P's by about \nullSimRatio{} times. The intervals are bootstrap intervals over the 40 evaluations. The check covers unmasked rules and null edits on a target other than Suite B's; it does not test the masked stock gate, search dynamics or false rejection of true effects. The operating characteristics are therefore simulated and empirically checked, and the empirical check is the stricter of the two.

Seed-artifact variance replaces the earlier assumptions (mean 0.55, within-task variance 0.149). The observed within-task variance is 0.088, 0.148 and 0.124 on Suites A, B and C, and the standard deviation of the true pass rate across tasks is 0.40, 0.31 and 0.37. With three search seeds, 80\% power, $\alpha=0.05$ and a task-level effect spread of 0.15, the minimum detectable effect for contrasts between the three frozen artifacts is \mdeACondOne{} on Suite A (\mdeATasks{} tasks, one trial), \mdeBCondOne{}, \mdeBCondTwo{} and \mdeBCondThree{} on Suite B (40 tasks, one to three trials) and \mdeCCondOne{}, \mdeCCondTwo{} and \mdeCCondThree{} on Suite C (20 tasks). For claims about the optimizer, which include seed variance and use $t$ with two degrees of freedom, it is \mdeAPipeOne{} on A, \mdeBPipeOne{} to \mdeBPipeThree{} on B and \mdeCPipeOne{} to \mdeCPipeThree{} on C. With Holm correction over the primary family the first-step values rise to \mdeAHolmOne{} on A, \mdeBHolmOne{} on B and \mdeCHolmOne{} on C with one trial. The task-level effect spread, seed-by-task interaction and seed variance cannot be estimated from seed-artifact data and remain assumptions. With the task-level effect spread set to zero the values are noise-only floors, lower bounds on any MDE: \mdeFloorAOne{} on A, \mdeFloorBOne{}, \mdeFloorBTwo{} and \mdeFloorBThree{} on B, and \mdeFloorCOne{}, \mdeFloorCTwo{} and \mdeFloorCThree{} on C.

The shared standard-error helper adds a within-task term to a variance of task means that already contains it. In simulation (mean reward 0.55, dispersion 1.5, 40 to 280 tasks) its variance is 1.00 times the correct value at one trial per task, 1.33 times at three trials, 1.24 at five and 1.13 at ten for the new-task estimand, and 1.6 to 8.6 times too large for the fixed-task estimand. Its coverage of a new-task target is 0.94 to 0.97. At one trial it equals the between-task term alone.


The analysis plan fixed the following before the search cutoff of 2026-10-13 06:00 UTC. A second family scores each arm's native end-of-run artifact at the cutoff, with unmatched budget and the same contrast rows as the primary family (12, 8 and 6 per suite), under its own Holm correction. Arms with budget-coupled schedules are SkillOpt (cosine schedule), GEPA (metric-call cap) and the iteration-capped arms. All arms are scored interleaved in one evaluation window, with the seed artifact re-scored inside it. A one-hour block sensitivity analysis applies a two-standard-error drift rule. The empty-output rate per arm is reported as a descriptive outcome. These items are specified in the analysis plan (commit \texttt{029335d8c}).


The primary contrast family pairs hill-climb and agent-optimize with the following. Suite A: the seed artifact, GEPA, SkillOpt, Meta-Harness, WikiSkill and EvoSkill (12 contrasts). Suite B: the seed artifact, the one-shot control, GEPA and HarnessLens (8). Suite C: the seed artifact, the one-shot control and GEPA (6). Secondary families, each with its own Holm correction, compare every competitor with the seed artifact, hill-climb with agent-optimize, and \capevolve{} with the cheap arms. Equivalence uses a two one-sided test on the artifact-level contrast (cluster-robust, $t$ with $T-1$ degrees of freedom) with margin $0.10$ absolute, about the planned conditional MDE of 0.106 on Suite A and 0.110 on B; Suite C is descriptive. Its larger one-sided $p$ has its own Holm family per suite. The pipeline-level 90\% half-width at three search seeds is about 0.2, above the margin, so equivalence of the optimizer is never claimed. The two interval types are as in Section~\ref{sec:v2:stats}; the pipeline-level variance adds the seed variance divided by the number of seeds to the task variance, which counts seed-by-task variance twice and is conservative.

The stock-gate arms (hill-climb with one-standard-error gate, footprint masking on, candidate-side coverage 0.6, no rule P; three search seeds, Suites A and C) are compared at the smaller of $N^\star$ and their own rollout count, with rule-P hill-climb taken at the same count; the count is disclosed per seed. Amendment 6 of the analysis plan was written before any final-set data but after we had seen search-time selection-split results, which showed rule P nearly inert and the stock gate promoting. We therefore label the following post-search-time. A secondary family S-stock, with its own Holm correction per suite, compares stock-gate hill-climb with the seed artifact and GEPA, SkillOpt, Meta-Harness, WikiSkill and EvoSkill on Suite A (6 contrasts), and with the seed artifact and GEPA on Suite C (2 contrasts, development-exposed, never the sole basis of a generalization claim); Suite B has no stock-gate arm. It uses the artifact-level test, the two intervals and the equivalence rule above. Stock-gate against rule-P hill-climb stays descriptive: artifact-level mean differences with 95\% intervals and per-seed values, no Holm correction, no $p$-values in the abstract.

Selection-to-final shrinkage is reported for every arm. Per search seed it is the arm's gain over the seed artifact on its own selection split, taken from the search record at the comparison point, minus its paired gain over the in-window seed artifact on the final set. We give per-seed values, the mean, a 95\% interval with variance equal to the sum of the two task-clustered variances ($t$, conservative) and the seed standard deviation. Seeds that return the seed artifact contribute 0 and are marked. Positive shrinkage is selection optimism, which includes regression to the mean of the single search-time seed measurement. Each arm is tested once against zero shrinkage (two-sided, own Holm family per suite); differences between arms are descriptive. A gain survives only if its final-set artifact-level interval excludes 0, not because shrinkage is small.

For each arm and suite we report the cells touched by each rule: first-attempt infrastructure errors, cells recovered at retry one, two or three, cells scored 0 after three retries, and hangs retried or missing. Per artifact we also give the infrastructure-error rate (first attempt and after retries, Wilson 95\% interval) next to the seed artifact's rate in the same window, descriptively and without a test, and flag clearly higher rates. A Meta-Harness candidate's verification step failed with a sandbox error on 17\% of rollouts against 5\% for other candidates, so infrastructure-labelled errors can be caused by the artifact.

The Suite D primary family has ten contrasts (prompt only, tools only and prompt+tools each against the seed; prompt+tools against each single surface; rule P and the one-stage gate each against the stock gate; prompt+tools against the one-shot repair; prompt-only against GEPA on the sealed test only). Its secondary family has four contrasts: GEPA, rule P, the one-stage gate and the one-shot repair, each against the seed. The interaction PT - P - T + seed is descriptive, reported with an interval and no $p$-value. Holm correction applies within each family.


\section{What the History Ablation Removes}
\label{app:history}

In the no-history cells (E26 on Suite D retail, E36 on Suite E airline) the proposer receives no cross-iteration history. Three kinds are removed. First, the staged history files: the ledger of per-candidate outcomes with the tasks each edit fixed and broke, the proposer's own handover journal, the map to earlier iterations with copies of their explanations and diffs, the insight files, and the block listing the currently passing tasks to protect. None is written, none is mentioned in any text the proposer sees, and the passing-task block is empty; a configuration switch controls this. Second, run-directory reachability: in the other cells the proposer's sandbox mounts the whole run directory, so earlier candidates' raw rollouts, snapshots, events, earlier work directories, earlier proposer transcripts and metrics files are one directory listing away, while in the no-history cells it mounts only the proposer's working directory, the project directory read-only, a copy of the rejected-candidates record and a per-call configuration directory. Third, inherited notes: a child of an accepted candidate starts from that candidate's snapshot, which holds the parent's filled process note and its diagnosis file, and both are reset before each proposer call. What is kept is the current best capability's files, its trajectories and failure summary, the process note for the current iteration, and the gate's reject record: for each rejected candidate its identifier, a one-line summary with the validation score and delta, and the gate's reason. The reference cell receives the same record, and proposers read it about once per iteration. The ablation therefore removes the rich history (per-task outcomes, handover notes, diffs, raw traces and inherited notes), not the reject record. In the reference cell history is both staged and reachable.

After each no-history search a registered scan counts the proposer tool calls in the saved transcripts that name the history files, run-directory internals or another candidate's work directory. Through iteration 10 it reports two numbers per seed: designed reads of the reject record, and attempts at hidden paths. There was one attempt per seed, a guessed history file that was not mounted, so nothing was returned. Table~\ref{tab:e26check} gives the counts; the registered pattern counts are beside them, and no forbidden history file was found in any work directory (\noHistForbiddenFiles{} files across the three seeds). All three attempts were a guessed history file next to the reject record, which is not mounted in the no-history sandbox, so the output was empty.

\section{Matched Checkpoint, Acceptance Rules and Inert Seeds}
\label{app:matching}

The matching is on target rollouts, not on optimizer compute, which differs by arm; we report optimizer tokens and dollars per arm and claim no compute parity. It is also not on acceptance rules: stock \capevolve{} and the competitors keep their native rules, and rule P is a separate, deliberately conservative configuration. We therefore compare each method together with its own rule, and the stock-gate and rule-P hill-climb arms, plus the Suite D gate cells, measure what the rule costs and buys. A stock-gate secondary family and a selection-to-final shrinkage analysis were added after we saw search-time results and are labelled post-search-time. For every arm and seed we report candidates screened, candidates promoted under the native rule, and whether the returned artifact equals the seed artifact. A secondary analysis scores each arm's native end-of-run artifact at the cutoff, labelled unmatched, because truncation at $N^\star$ can penalise schedules tied to the full budget (SkillOpt's cosine schedule, EvoSkill's iteration cap). The one-shot repair is one \capevolve{} iteration with one proposal followed by the arm's own gate on Suites B and C, and by no selection on Suite D.

The best-of-$k$ one-shot control on Suite C draws $k$ independent single proposals per seed and keeps the best. It was paused at 140, 140 and 180 counted rollouts per seed, which is $k=6$, 6 and 8 proposals, to fund the stock-gate arm on Suite B, so it is exploratory and descriptive only, truncated at those counts, and it has no test. It is also the only compute-matched control, and it sits on the development-exposed Suite C.

A seed whose returned artifact equals the seed artifact (same content hash) is not rerun for the primary test: its delta is exactly 0 and it is marked inert. It is rescored as a labelled measurement check. Shrinkage and cost per point are reported over non-inert seeds with the inert fraction beside them, and cost per point is total cost divided by total gain with a bootstrap interval, not a mean over positive seeds.

\section{Infrastructure Incidents}
\label{app:incidents}

Times are UTC on 2026-10-11 unless stated. Each incident lists its effect on the data.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \emph{EvoSkill priority starvation.} The gateway's low-priority tag list still contained EvoSkill after its promotion to a main arm, so under reserved capacity its calls never got a slot between 13:05 and 18:40. Those rollouts show as 1800-second timeouts with zero model calls. They are invalid and kept as \texttt{INVALID} directories; EvoSkill restarted from scratch at 18:25 to 18:55 and is about 5.5 hours behind the other arms.
\item \emph{Suite B relaunch.} The first Suite B runs (09:40) used the wrong judge, a pre-merge worktree without rule P, and untagged candidate-tool errors. They were stopped at 12:38, labelled superseded and not used. A second correction restored the agent-optimize seed capability and archived two sessions that ran the archived legacy engine.
\item \emph{Content-filter rename for ACE.} Through the gateway, Claude Sonnet 5.5 returns an empty content-filter response when a prompt asks for a chain-of-thought or reasoning field in the output; prose mentions pass. ACE's prompts requested such a field. Its output field was renamed to ``analysis'' with the diff and affected-call count recorded. No other arm was affected.
\item \emph{HarnessLens retry policy.} Upstream aborts a whole baseline stage on one worker error. One of six trial rollouts ended with \texttt{opencode\_exit\_1} after a streamed gpt-oss response ended with only reasoning content and no finish reason. HarnessLens is the only arm that streams the target (1 of 98 streamed calls lacked usage). The campaign policy retries a worker error once inside the rollout budget, scores a second error as zero for HarnessLens's internal selection only, and logs every occurrence.
\item \emph{HarnessLens environment defect.} HarnessLens's isolated editor calls require bubblewrap, which the host lacked, so every loop iteration before the fix ended in a materialization failure and no candidate reached a rollout (41 iterations in the extended exploratory run, final output written 2026-10-12 09:33 UTC). Bubblewrap 0.6.3 was installed at 09:42 UTC, the stopped seeds were cut and restarted at 09:43 to 09:44 UTC, and the failed iterations of the one seed resumed earlier were refunded in the token ledger. All earlier HarnessLens runs are therefore recorded as not evaluated, environment defect, and never as a method result. The earlier statement that no candidate fitted within the token guard is withdrawn.
\item \emph{HarnessLens search-time judge.} Inside HarnessLens the $\tau^2$ natural-language judge during search was gpt-oss-120b, while every other Suite B arm used GPT-5.5. The final evaluation scores every Suite B cell with the same GPT-5.5 judge and simulator, and the ripgrep deviation is identical in the search and the final evaluation.
\item \emph{Search-split gold visibility on the $\tau^2$ suites.} On Suites B, C and D the \capevolve{} proposer's run directory holds the native simulation result files for the optimization split, and they include each task's evaluation criteria, which are the gold for those search tasks only. No selection-task gold is reachable before finalization, and no test ids or gold are reachable at all (checked for Suite D on 12 October and by a negative test on 11 October for Suites B and C). Selection-task ids are visible, and agent-optimize's filtered optimization and selection gold is disclosed separately. This is separate from the final-set seal, which concerns the test tasks.
\item \emph{The gold-derived signal visible to every optimizer.} On Suite A the scorer feedback that optimizers see carries no gold cell values, and a unit test asserts it. It does disclose categorical facts about the gold: the expected fill count per range, whether the expected cells are empty, the numeric direction of an error, text versus number, a matching prefix, whether the expected value equals the cell's original input, and Excel error markers. The signal is the same for every arm, so feedback is not gold-blind.
\item \emph{Budget checked once per iteration.} \capevolve{} checks its rollout budget at the start of each iteration and charges rollouts after each evaluation completes. A rule-P confirmation of a candidate and its parent on 49 tasks with three trials each takes 147 rollouts per side, so one iteration can overrun the cap by up to 294 rollouts. This affects every hill-climb cell. On Suite B two hill-climb seeds reached 637 counted rollouts against a limit of 550, and in both of them the third candidate straddled the cap during confirmation, was excluded as truncated, and the champion is the seed artifact.
\item \emph{Optimizer sandbox gaps.} The optimizer sandbox on Suites B, C and D lacks the \texttt{patch} and \texttt{rg} tools, and its Python cannot import the tau2 package or \texttt{loguru}. This holds identically for every arm, and the optimizers worked around it, but they spent turns on it and could not run local checks that import tau2.
\item \emph{Agent-optimize and its skill file.} Claude Code's path permission denied agent-optimize reading its own skill file, one to seven times per seed on Suites B and C and once per seed on Suite A. The driver prompt carried the loop instructions, so candidates were still produced, but this deviates from stock agent-optimize.
\item \emph{Hill-climb seed 2 on Suite A.} After a hot-swap of the evaluation daemon at 03:02 UTC, two evaluations collided on the agent-view directory and three candidates never ran. The run was stopped from 03:26 to 08:26 UTC. The graph labelled these candidates rejected, but they are infrastructure losses and every count treats them as missing, not rejected. The same hot-swap invalidated the seed train-split scores of the stock-gate arm on Suite A for all three seeds.
\item \emph{SkillOpt template edits.} Three of about twenty SkillOpt edit steps wrote an unknown placeholder into the task template, so the whole validation batch scored zero without a model call. That is candidate-induced and scored by its native feedback.
\item \emph{Stale target name in the GEPA setting string.} On Suite D the reflective-dataset setting string that GEPA gives its reflection model names the target as gpt-oss-120b, inherited from the Suite B script, while the target is gpt-4.1-mini. We kept it unchanged for parity with the running cells. Its effect has no known direction, and it could disadvantage GEPA in the prompt-only contrast with GEPA, so that contrast carries this caveat. The \capevolve{} cells name the right target in their instructions and guidance.
\item \emph{Coverage veto under rule P.} On Suite A one hill-climb seed (seed \stVetoHcASeed{}) had \stVetoHcAIndecisive{} of \stVetoHcACands{} candidates vetoed because the share of valid scores was \stVetoHcACoverage{}\%, below the 0.95 floor. This is search-time gate behaviour on the selection split at a mid-search snapshot, and it is not held-out.
\item \emph{Candidate-caused failures labelled as infrastructure errors.} During search on the $\tau^2$ suites, the benchmark labels some candidate-caused failures as infrastructure errors. In Suite D every such termination follows a write-tool call, the seed has none, and they cluster in candidates that edited a write tool's error or return behaviour. \capevolve{}'s search treats infrastructure-errored trials as missing, so such a candidate's screen scores are computed without those trials. In a mid-search snapshot at about 15:15 UTC on 12 October, no accepted Suite D champion had an elevated infrastructure rate (the highest was 2 of 74 trials), and every candidate with a rate of 6 to 25\% was rejected even though its failing trials were dropped, so the bias had not put any current champion through the gate. The final check is the list of flagged artifacts from the final evaluation. At final scoring a cell that still errors after three retries counts as a failure, so this does not flatter any artifact's held-out score. A registered no-retry-credit sensitivity analysis scores 0 for any cell whose first attempt was an infrastructure error. It is reported beside the primary result for every contrast that involves an artifact flagged for an infrastructure-error rate of at least 5\% whose Wilson lower bound exceeds the seed artifact's upper bound in the same window. If the two analyses disagree, both are shown and the primary stands. \needs{The list of flagged artifacts, after the final evaluation.}
\item \emph{Infrastructure errors during search, CapEvolve against GEPA.} In \capevolve{}, an infrastructure-errored trial (after the model-client and benchmark retries) is missing data and is excluded from that task's mean; only faults the adapter attributes to the candidate, such as a tool import or construction failure, score 0. In GEPA, the campaign's counted evaluator retries an errored rollout once, and a rollout still errored after that scores 0 in GEPA's internal selection. The two handle exogenous errors differently during search, which affects selection only; the final evaluation applies one rule to every arm. During the Azure rate-limit period on 12 October about 1\% of Suite D rollouts ended in an infrastructure error, and GEPA's log showed none so far.
\item \emph{Gateway call failures.} At 13:11 we added a transparent retry (three retries, exponential backoff) for connection resets, read timeouts and 502 to 504 responses, and raised the upstream timeout from 900 to 1800 s. Before that, 1 to 2.5\% of Gemma calls failed, and the shared SpreadsheetBench adapter scored such failures as zero for competitor arms while \capevolve{} dropped them. Pre-13:11 zeros remain in competitor search histories as search-time feedback, handled natively, while \capevolve{} dropped errored trials; this asymmetry may favour \capevolve{} in search feedback and is labelled.
\item \emph{Gold and split-file readability.} On Suite A the shared proposer user could read split-id files in world-readable checkouts until 10:00; gold and credentials were already denied. A Suite B agent-optimize transcript scan found no read of task or split files, and the arm was moved into a mount-namespace shim at 15:00. Meta-Harness first launched with a gold-readable proposer, stopped before any proposer iteration, and was relaunched container-isolated at 09:38.
\item \emph{Failed reproductions.} VERSE needs SWE-rebench or Terminal-Bench docker sessions; CoEvoSkills needs a Harbor per-task loop; HarnessLens on Suite A evolves only OpenCode, Codex and Pi runtimes. Blocker files are in the campaign scratch store.
\end{itemize}
