\section{Gate Simulation and Statistical Power}
\label{app:sim}

We chose rule P before any main \capevolve{} seed, from a budget-exact simulation on the real promotion and gate code. We then re-ran it at each suite's observed difficulty: the seed artifact's mean reward and trial-level variance on the search split (Suite A: 40 validation tasks, 600 rollouts; Suite B: 49 optimization tasks, 550 rollouts; Suite C: 20 tasks), one screening trial per task, about ten candidates per search and 22{,}400 searches per cell. The pre-declared criterion was to maximise the expected true new-task gain of the final artifact subject to at most 0.15 probability of a false promotion in an all-null search. A search screens 6.7 to 9.4 candidates on average. Table~\ref{tab:sim} gives rule P and the one-stage gate. The false-promotion probability is per search, about one percent per candidate. The gain column is the expected true new-task gain of the final artifact under a stress mix of edits (below). The one-stage gate has the larger expected gain and the higher power, and it falsely promotes in about half of all-null searches; rule P gives up power to avoid that. Five confirmation trials at $z_{\mathrm{conf}}=1.645$ would cost $2\cdot 40\cdot 5=400$ of the 600 rollouts per confirmation on Suite A instead of 240, so fewer candidates are screened and the expected gain halves; on Suite B the 490 rollouts exceed the 452 left after the baseline and one screen, so that setting could never promote.

The simulated edit mix is a stress assumption, not a measured rate. Of the proposed edits, 60\% are null (true population effect 0), 25\% are positive (uniform $+1$ to $+6$ points) and 15\% are negative (uniform $-1$ to $-6$ points). Half of the edits are diffuse (all tasks affected, per-task SD 0.10) and half are sparse (30\% of tasks affected, per-task SD 0.25), with independent per-task realisations on the selection tasks and on new tasks. Expected gains in Table~\ref{tab:sim} are conditional on this mix.

Seed-artifact variance replaces the earlier assumptions (mean 0.55, within-task variance 0.149). The observed within-task variance is 0.088, 0.148 and 0.124 on Suites A, B and C, and the standard deviation of the true pass rate across tasks is 0.40, 0.31 and 0.37. With three search seeds, 80\% power, $\alpha=0.05$ and a task-level effect spread of 0.15, the minimum detectable effect for contrasts between the three frozen artifacts is \mdeACondOne{} on Suite A (\mdeATasks{} tasks, one trial), \mdeBCondOne{}, \mdeBCondTwo{} and \mdeBCondThree{} on Suite B (40 tasks, one to three trials) and \mdeCCondOne{}, \mdeCCondTwo{} and \mdeCCondThree{} on Suite C (20 tasks). For claims about the optimizer, which include seed variance and use $t$ with two degrees of freedom, it is \mdeAPipeOne{} on A, \mdeBPipeOne{} to \mdeBPipeThree{} on B and \mdeCPipeOne{} to \mdeCPipeThree{} on C. With Holm correction over the primary family the first-step values rise to \mdeAHolmOne{} on A, \mdeBHolmOne{} on B and \mdeCHolmOne{} on C with one trial. The task-level effect spread, seed-by-task interaction and seed variance cannot be estimated from seed-artifact data and remain assumptions. With the task-level effect spread set to zero the values are noise-only floors, lower bounds on any MDE: \mdeFloorAOne{} on A, \mdeFloorBOne{}, \mdeFloorBTwo{} and \mdeFloorBThree{} on B, and \mdeFloorCOne{}, \mdeFloorCTwo{} and \mdeFloorCThree{} on C.

The shared standard-error helper adds a within-task term to a variance of task means that already contains it. In simulation (mean reward 0.55, dispersion 1.5, 40 to 280 tasks) its variance is 1.00 times the correct value at one trial per task, 1.33 times at three trials, 1.24 at five and 1.13 at ten for the new-task estimand, and 1.6 to 8.6 times too large for the fixed-task estimand. Its coverage of a new-task target is 0.94 to 0.97. At one trial it equals the between-task term alone.


The analysis plan fixed the following before the search cutoff of 2026-10-13 06:00 UTC. A second family scores each arm's native end-of-run artifact at the cutoff, with unmatched budget and the same contrast rows as the primary family (12, 8 and 6 per suite), under its own Holm correction. Arms with budget-coupled schedules are SkillOpt (cosine schedule), GEPA (metric-call cap) and the iteration-capped arms. All arms are scored interleaved in one evaluation window, with the seed artifact re-scored inside it. A one-hour block sensitivity analysis applies a two-standard-error drift rule. The empty-output rate per arm is reported as a descriptive outcome. These items are specified in the analysis plan (commit \texttt{029335d8c}).


The primary contrast family pairs hill-climb and agent-optimize with the following. Suite A: the seed artifact, GEPA, SkillOpt, Meta-Harness, WikiSkill and EvoSkill (12 contrasts). Suite B: the seed artifact, the one-shot control, GEPA and HarnessLens (8). Suite C: the seed artifact, the one-shot control and GEPA (6). Secondary families, each with its own Holm correction, compare every competitor with the seed artifact, hill-climb with agent-optimize, and \capevolve{} with the cheap arms. Equivalence uses a two one-sided test on the artifact-level contrast (cluster-robust, $t$ with $T-1$ degrees of freedom) with margin $0.10$ absolute, about the planned conditional MDE of 0.106 on Suite A and 0.110 on B; Suite C is descriptive. Its larger one-sided $p$ has its own Holm family per suite. The pipeline-level 90\% half-width at three search seeds is about 0.2, above the margin, so equivalence of the optimizer is never claimed. The two interval types are as in Section~\ref{sec:setup:stats}; the pipeline-level variance adds the seed variance divided by the number of seeds to the task variance, which counts seed-by-task variance twice and is conservative.

The exploratory stock-gate arms (hill-climb with one-standard-error gate, footprint masking on, candidate-side coverage 0.6, no rule P; three search seeds, Suites A and C) are compared at the smaller of $N^\star$ and their own rollout count, with rule-P hill-climb taken at the same count. Contrasts are artifact-level mean differences with 95\% intervals and per-seed values, with no Holm correction and no $p$-values in the abstract; a count below $N^\star$ is stated beside the numbers.

For each arm and suite we report the cells touched by each rule: first-attempt infrastructure errors, cells recovered at retry one, two or three, cells scored 0 after three retries, and hangs retried or missing. Per artifact we also give the infrastructure-error rate (first attempt and after retries, Wilson 95\% interval) next to the seed artifact's rate in the same window, descriptively and without a test, and flag clearly higher rates. A Meta-Harness candidate's verification step failed with a sandbox error on 17\% of rollouts against 5\% for other candidates, so infrastructure-labelled errors can be caused by the artifact.

\section{Scorer Audit}
\label{app:scorer}

The default SpreadsheetBench reward is \texttt{hard\_no\_recalc}: cached values as saved. Correct formula outputs without cached values passed 0 of 30 checks under it and 25 of 30 (LibreOffice 7.1.8.1) to 29 or 30 of 30 (LibreOffice 25.8.7.3) under recalculation. The recalculation path recalculates the candidate output only and uses gold as shipped. With LibreOffice 7.1.8.1 the official gold file used as output fails on 32 of 400 verified tasks, and with 25.8.7.3 on 19. Four verified tasks (130-9, 283-32, 45944, 49300) are unscorable on every path because of malformed answer-position strings, all four in the SkillOpt test split. Scoring was deterministic: 333 records were scored twice with fresh profiles and none differed. The gold workbooks sat in the directory bind-mounted read-write into the agent container; the campaign runs agents on a gold-free copy and scores on the pristine tree.

\section{Infrastructure Incidents}
\label{app:incidents}

Times are UTC on 2026-10-11 unless stated. Each incident lists its effect on the data.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \emph{EvoSkill priority starvation.} The gateway's low-priority tag list still contained EvoSkill after its promotion to a main arm, so under reserved capacity its calls never got a slot between 13:05 and 18:40. Those rollouts show as 1800-second timeouts with zero model calls. They are invalid and kept as \texttt{INVALID} directories; EvoSkill restarted from scratch at 18:25 to 18:55 and is about 5.5 hours behind the other arms.
\item \emph{Suite B relaunch.} The first Suite B runs (09:40) used the wrong judge, a pre-merge worktree without rule P, and untagged candidate-tool errors. They were stopped at 12:38, labelled superseded and not used. A second correction restored the agent-optimize seed capability and archived two sessions that ran the archived legacy engine.
\item \emph{Content-filter rename for ACE.} Through the gateway, Claude Sonnet 5.5 returns an empty content-filter response when a prompt asks for a chain-of-thought or reasoning field in the output; prose mentions pass. ACE's prompts requested such a field. Its output field was renamed to ``analysis'' with the diff and affected-call count recorded. No other arm was affected.
\item \emph{HarnessLens retry policy.} Upstream aborts a whole baseline stage on one worker error. One of six trial rollouts ended with \texttt{opencode\_exit\_1} after a streamed gpt-oss response ended with only reasoning content and no finish reason. HarnessLens is the only arm that streams the target (1 of 98 streamed calls lacked usage). The campaign policy retries a worker error once inside the rollout budget, scores a second error as zero for HarnessLens's internal selection only, and logs every occurrence.
\item \emph{Gateway call failures.} At 13:11 we added a transparent retry (three retries, exponential backoff) for connection resets, read timeouts and 502 to 504 responses, and raised the upstream timeout from 900 to 1800 s. Before that, 1 to 2.5\% of Gemma calls failed, and the shared SpreadsheetBench adapter scored such failures as zero for competitor arms while \capevolve{} dropped them. Pre-13:11 zeros remain in competitor search histories as search-time feedback, handled natively, while \capevolve{} dropped errored trials; this asymmetry may favour \capevolve{} in search feedback and is labelled.
\item \emph{Gold and split-file readability.} On Suite A the shared proposer user could read split-id files in world-readable checkouts until 10:00; gold and credentials were already denied. A Suite B agent-optimize transcript scan found no read of task or split files, and the arm was moved into a mount-namespace shim at 15:00. Meta-Harness first launched with a gold-readable proposer, stopped before any proposer iteration, and was relaunched container-isolated at 09:38.
\item \emph{Failed reproductions.} VERSE needs SWE-rebench or Terminal-Bench docker sessions; CoEvoSkills needs a Harbor per-task loop; HarnessLens on Suite A evolves only OpenCode, Codex and Pi runtimes. Blocker files are in the campaign scratch store.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Rollouts scored zero after an infrastructure error.}
Before the 3-hour hang rule, ACE and MIPROv2 first ran with a 40-minute watchdog that scored a timeout as reward zero.
The coordinator rejected it as asymmetric before it produced results: the six 40-minute runs (three seeds per arm) were killed after one counted rollout each, and their event logs hold no watchdog zero.
Zeros that did reach the search come from other adapter paths.
An adapter returns reward 0 with an error string when an LLM call times out at the client or the sandbox returns HTTP 500.
Each such rollout counts against the budget, so the search saw a score and not a missing cell.
We did not check whether the ACE reflector or the MIPROv2 bootstrap showed the error text.

Adapters label these errors differently, so we classify every error row with one rule and keep the adapter label beside it (Table~\ref{tab:zeros}).
The classification is reporting-only: each arm's search used its own native handling of these errors, and no search decision depends on the uniform label.
In the analysis, a cell that is still an infrastructure error after three retries with new trial seeds counts as a failure in the primary analysis and as missing in the sensitivity analysis (amendment 5O).
A hang past three hours gets one retry and is then missing in both.
MIPROv2 and Meta-Harness ran with handling from before this rule, as did the GEPA, EvoSkill and SkillOpt seeds until their boundary restarts, so their search-time zeros stay in their histories.
ACE was relaunched at 00:59 UTC on 12 October on the fixed library and is not affected.
The rule changes the class of Trace2Skill and WikiSkill rows, which their adapters label as candidate faults.
Of the \errTraceTotal{} Trace2Skill errors, \errTraceInfra{} carry a timeout or sandbox string and move to infrastructure, and \errTraceCand{} keep the candidate class because no string was stored.
All \errWikiTotal{} WikiSkill errors are infrastructure.
Every Meta-Harness error is infrastructure, \errMetaTotal{} in all, with per-seed counts of \errMetaSeedZero{}, \errMetaSeedOne{} and \errMetaSeedTwo{} (sandbox 500 errors or timeouts).
MIPROv2 has \errMiproTotal{} in all, with \errMiproSeedZero{}, \errMiproSeedOne{} and \errMiproSeedTwo{} per seed.
SkillOpt has \errSkillInfra{} infrastructure errors and \errSkillCand{} candidate errors.
An audit of the candidate rows found all but one to be genuine template breaks, where an edit appended literal Python braces such as \texttt{\{c.coordinate\}} to the template and broke the placeholder contract, and one row caused by a race in the shared evaluator.
The race was fixed at about 00:55 UTC on 12 October, and no search decision was affected.
One row remains unverifiable: GEPA on Suite C, seed 2, whose run stores no string although the rollout made 20 target calls.
It counts as a failure in the primary analysis and as missing in the sensitivity analysis, like any cell whose class we cannot establish.
Eight $\tau^2$ rows are infrastructure only because the user simulator emitted a stop token next to leaked reasoning.
We count a simulated-user fault as infrastructure, in its own subclass, because the simulator is a frozen environment component shared by all arms.
A pre-specified sensitivity analysis flips those 8 rows to candidate errors, and we report it beside the main result.
GEPA persists outputs only for its best program, so its counts are incomplete.
Gateway retries from 13:11 UTC on 11 October removed most client-side failures, and the uniform search-time infrastructure retry was added from each arm's restart time (decisions.md).
