\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item \emph{Power.} Three search seeds give two degrees of freedom for pipeline-level claims, and the artifact-level minimum detectable effect is \mdeFortyArtifact{} at 40 final tasks (three trials, variance inputs fixed before any pilot). We make artifact-level claims, show all per-seed values, and call smaller gaps undetected.
\item \emph{Exposure.} The Suite A final set samples an older 912-question release whose ids were scored at most twice before, score only. We exclude 55 ids by family prefix. Suite C's test split is development-exposed, and we cannot see off-repository use of the retail test split.
\item \emph{Native-design differences.} The stock \capevolve{} arms see 40 validation tasks and competitors 120. Competitors keep their native gates. On Suite B, agent-optimize runs its evaluator inside its shell and can read gold actions of its own 74 optimization and selection tasks, never the sealed test 40, so its selection-time comparisons are flagged.
\item \emph{Matched checkpoint.} Capacity, not the cap, bound the search. $B^\star$ falls below 600, so results say little about larger budgets, and the slowest arm sets $B^\star$ for all.
\item \emph{Scorer and judge.} LibreOffice 25.8 fails 19 of 400 official gold files as outputs, so some tasks are unwinnable on the recalculated scale. GPT-5.5 judges the $\tau^2$ assertions; a stored verdict sample will test judge sensitivity. \needs{Judge sensitivity result.}
\item \emph{Coverage.} One optimizer model, two target models, a community port in place of official WikiSkill, and five methods stopped at one seed. RITS token counts have no prices.
\end{itemize}
