\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

A gain in this paper is the paired difference between an artifact and the shared seed artifact on tasks that search did not use for design, on the recalculated scale. It is not comparable to the as-saved gains of earlier work, which carry \histGemmaCompliancePts{} to \histGptCompliancePts{} compliance points in our own history (Appendix~\ref{app:hist}). When an optimizer is within the minimum detectable effect of the seed artifact or of the one-shot control, we report it as not distinguishable there. \needs{Which arms are distinguishable from the controls, per suite, after the final evaluation.}

Rule P trades power for few false promotions. A true gain of $+0.08$ is promoted with probability \simPPowerEight{} under rule P and \simKonePowerEight{} under the one-stage gate. Per-search gains under 600 rollouts should therefore be small, and a competitor with a looser native gate may promote more edits, real and spurious. Per-arm accepted-candidate counts and null-edit false-accept rates separate search quality from gate strictness. \needs{Per-arm accepted-candidate counts and null-edit false-accept rates.}

Infrastructure choices also changed outcomes for individual arms. A gateway priority list starved EvoSkill for 5.5 hours, and failed gateway calls scored as zero penalised long rollouts. Admission control, retry policy and timeouts are part of the experimental condition, and we audit them per arm (Appendix~\ref{app:incidents}).
