\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:v2:setup}

\subsection{Suites, models and splits}

Table~\ref{tab:v2:suites} lists the four suites. Suite A edits spreadsheets with a code-execution agent: the target is Gemma-4-31B-it, served by an internal inference service, on the 400-task verified subset of the 912 SpreadsheetBench questions \citep{spreadsheetbench}\footnote{File \texttt{spreadsheetbench\_verified\_400.tar.gz} (sha256 \texttt{10ef893d}\ldots) in \texttt{github.com/RUCKBReasoning/SpreadsheetBench}, \texttt{data/} directory.}. Suites B and C are customer-service dialogue in $\tau^2$-bench retail and airline \citep{tau2-bench,tau_bench}, pinned at commit 8ebb7499, with gpt-oss-120b as target and user simulator. Suite D is a separate ablation family on retail with an Azure-hosted target (gpt-4.1-mini), which matches Suite B in seed accuracy (0.61 against 0.56 to 0.65 on 49 tasks) and trial noise (null paired SD 0.57 against 0.55), and the standard gpt-4.1 user simulator and judge. It reuses Suite B's splits and is never pooled with Suites A to C. On Suites B and C GPT-5.5 grades natural-language assertions, because the default judge is not served by our gateway, so absolute scores are not comparable to published $\tau^2$ numbers. Claude Sonnet 5.5 is the optimizer or reflection model for every matched arm. One metering gateway tags every model call with suite, arm, search seed and role.

On Suite A the reward is the LibreOffice-recalculated hard score, and the as-saved score is logged for every rollout. Competitors read train traces and select on validation, while stock \capevolve{} reads and gates on the 40 validation tasks, so it sees 40 tasks where competitors see 120. This differs in exposure, in a direction we cannot sign, and we keep it as the native design. Search-time selection scores are not comparable across methods, so only final sets are compared. Every arm receives the same scorer feedback, which carries gold-derived facts such as fill counts and error direction but no gold values. Proposers are isolated by user id or mount namespace with a negative test before launch, except where Appendix~\ref{app:incidents} records a gap: on Suites B and C agent-optimize can read gold actions of its own optimization and selection tasks, never the final tasks, so its rows carry a dagger and a sensitivity row excludes it. The Suite A final ids come from the 512 ids of the 912 release that are not in the verified subset. An id is eligible only if it is scorable with its gold file as output under the official comparator and after LibreOffice 25.8 recalculation, was scored at most twice before and never used for design, and has no development-exposed sibling in its task family (294 eligible, 239 after the family rule). The 70 are drawn at the search cutoff with a seeded stratified sample (seed 20261013) and the sample is hashed before any final rollout is scored.

\subsection{Arms and the surface each edits}

Table~\ref{tab:v2:arms} lists the arms. We compare \capevolve{} in three configurations (hill-climb with the stock gate, hill-climb with rule P, agent-optimize with rule P) against nine competitors and two controls, an unoptimized seed artifact and a one-shot repair. Eight of the nine competitors run from adapted official code with the deviations of Table~\ref{tab:deviations} (direction judged by us); WikiSkill has no official code, so we run a community port. Meta-Harness, EvoSkill, Trace2Skill and WikiSkill are edited down to the same two prompt files on Suite A, so Suite A measures the loop of an adapted method, not the method in its native form. VERSE, CoEvoSkills, and HarnessLens on Suite A failed to reproduce (Appendix~\ref{app:incidents}). Five more methods (AHE, MCE, RHO, Memento-Skills, GraphSkillEvo) are one-seed exploratory runs outside the headline tables. Eight competitors run on Suite A, GEPA and HarnessLens on Suite B, GEPA on Suite C and GEPA on Suite D. The surface asymmetry matters: on Suites B and C the \capevolve{} arms edit prompt and tools, while GEPA edits the prompt only. Suite A (prompt files for every arm) and the prompt-only cells of Suite D separate loop quality from surface width.

\subsection{Matched checkpoint and acceptance rules}

Each search may count a budget of $N$ target rollouts, every evaluation included: 600 on Suite A and for GEPA and HarnessLens, and 550 plus finalization for \capevolve{} and the one-shot repair on Suites B, C and D. \capevolve{} also stops at an optimizer-token guard (soft 3.5M, hard 3.9M non-cache-read tokens); competitors are checked after the fact, and SkillOpt exceeded 4M in one seed (4.78M). One Gemma deployment gives about 12 rollouts per hour per search, so slow arms miss 600. We therefore compare at a matched checkpoint $N^\star$: the largest rollout count that every seed of every primary-family arm reaches by the search cutoff (2026-10-13 06:00 UTC), rounded down to a multiple of 50 and floored at 200, computed from rollout counters only. Other arms are compared at the smaller of $N^\star$ and their own count, and the count is printed beside each number. A candidate whose evaluation straddles $N^\star$ is excluded. Each arm is scored on what its own selection rule would have deployed at $N^\star$. The placeholder values are $N^\star=$ \mockBstarA{}, \mockBstarB{} and \mockBstarC{} for Suites A, B and C. \needs{Final $N^\star$ and the list of incomplete seeds, after the search cutoff.} Suite D cells use a fixed budget and are not checkpoint-matched.

The matching is on target rollouts, not on optimizer compute or acceptance rules; every arm keeps its native rule and we claim no compute parity (Appendix~\ref{app:matching}). The stock-gate arms and the Suite D gate cells measure what the rule costs and buys, and the stock-gate arm on Suite B was launched after search-time results, and promoting the arms on A and C into a secondary family, with the shrinkage analysis, came after them; both are labelled post-search-time.

\subsection{Ablation family and statistics}
\label{sec:v2:stats}

Suite D changes one factor against a reference cell: hill-climb with the stock gate (footprint mask on, candidate-side coverage 0.6, no confirmation), prompt and tools, full history. The cells are prompt only, tools only, one-shot repair, rule P, the one-stage gate (mask off, joint coverage 0.95, no confirmation), no history (E26), and GEPA on the prompt. Candidates are locked to their surface by reading files of a locked surface from the pristine seed, and violations are counted. The family is specified in a time-stamped repository commit before launch. Its primary Holm family has eleven contrasts: prompt only, tools only and prompt+tools each against the seed; prompt+tools against each single surface; prompt only against tools only; rule P and the one-stage gate each against the stock reference; prompt+tools against the one-shot repair; no history against the reference; and prompt only against GEPA on the test tasks only. A secondary family has four contrasts (GEPA, rule P, the one-stage gate and the one-shot repair, each against the seed), and the prompt-tools interaction is a descriptive estimate with an interval and no $p$-value. Appendix~\ref{app:changelog} lists the analysis-plan amendments.

Intervals cluster on task family on Suite A, where families are nearly singletons, and on task on B, C and D. The headline is the paired contrast with the seed artifact, with cluster-robust standard errors and a $t$ interval, and Holm's step-down adjustment inside each family and suite; a claim that spans suites covers only the suites where the configuration ran and needs its row to pass in every suite named; Suite C never carries a claim alone. The primary family on Suite A pairs hill-climb and agent-optimize with the seed artifact and five competitors (12 contrasts); on B it uses the seed, the one-shot control, GEPA and HarnessLens (8), and on C the seed, the one-shot control and GEPA (6). Each row carries two intervals. The artifact-level interval, used for tests, supports statements about the three frozen artifacts; any statement about the method needs the pipeline-level interval ($t$ with two degrees of freedom, adding seed variance), and if it includes zero we restrict the statement to the frozen artifacts. Per-seed values accompany every mean. At final scoring every infrastructure error is retried up to three times with new trial seeds, and a cell that still fails scores 0 for every arm and the seed artifact; hangs past three hours get one retry and then count as missing. Search-time handling differed by arm (Appendix~\ref{app:incidents}); HarnessLens used a different search-time judge, but all Suite B final cells share one judge and simulator. A win needs a Holm-adjusted $p<0.05$ and an interval that excludes zero; a sensitivity analysis treats failed cells as missing, and if it disagrees the primary result stands and both are reported. A seed whose artifact is identical to the seed artifact has a delta of exactly 0 and is marked inert; with two or more inert seeds out of three an equivalence verdict is reported as not testable (Appendix~\ref{app:matching}).

Two artifacts are equivalent only if two one-sided tests at $\alpha=0.05$ both reject for a margin of 10 points, on Suites A and B at the artifact level; Suite C is descriptive. The margin is about the planning MDE, so equivalence is often out of reach, and the expected verdict is inconclusive; we print the test's design power at a true difference of 0 and of 5 points with every verdict, and inert seeds are excluded from equivalence statements. We do not write ``no difference''.

Variance comes from the seed artifact's observed evaluations (Appendix~\ref{app:sim}). The minimum detectable effect (MDE) for the mean of the three frozen champions against the seed, at 80\% power and $\alpha=0.05$ before Holm, is \mdeACondOne{} points on Suite A (70 tasks, one trial), \mdeBCondThree{} on B and \mdeCCondThree{} on C (three trials) and \mdeDSeed{} on D (40 tasks, five trials), or \mdeDSeedHolm{} on D with Holm over eleven contrasts; claims about the optimizer itself need \mdeAPipeOne{}, \mdeBPipeThree{}, \mdeCPipeThree{} and \mdeDPipe{} points. A single champion against the seed needs about 15 points on Suite A. If no contrast clears Holm, the contribution is the protocol and the audit. Suite A's seed-artifact mean moved by 8.8 points across six batches, so all arms are scored interleaved in one window with a pre-specified drift check.
