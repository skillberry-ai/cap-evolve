\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Agent optimizers edit the files around a frozen model, such as the system prompt, tool code and skill packages \citep{agent_skills_docs}, and report large gains \citep{skillopt,meta-harness}. Published methods span prompt and pipeline optimization \citep{dspy,miprov2-dspy,gepa}, skills \citep{skillopt,evoskill,trace2skill,wikiskill}, context playbooks \citep{ace} and harness search \citep{meta-harness,xu2026harnesslens}. Each paper uses its own benchmark, split, scorer, budget and optimizer model, so the numbers cannot be compared. Concurrent work holds out six benchmarks for five skill optimizers and finds that no method is best everywhere \citep{piao2026skillsgeneralize}, and matched budgets are a known weakness of harness-evolution evaluations \citep{rethinkharness}. We ask a measurement question: measured on tasks the search never used for design, at a matched rollout budget, with gold isolated and one error classifier, how much do frozen-weight agent optimizers gain, and what does the acceptance gate cost or buy?

The question is not idle. We audited the history of an earlier version of our own optimizer, \capevolve{}, which we also run here, and its reported gains were inflated. An earlier draft reported a Gemma-4-31B gain of \histGemmaDeltaSaved{} points on SpreadsheetBench. Recomputed from the run artifacts, the same outputs gain \histGemmaDeltaRecalc{} points once formulas are recalculated, because the original scorer reads cached cell values and so rewards literals over formulas. Scorer compliance accounts for \histGemmaComplianceShare{}\% of the Gemma gain and \histGptComplianceShare{}\% of the GPT-5.5 gain (Section~\ref{sec:problem}). The test split had also been scored in development runs, and optimizer sessions had read gold workbooks.

We make four contributions, in the order the paper presents them.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
\item To our knowledge, the first audit of one deployed optimizer's own history for scorer compliance, development reuse, gold reads and gate strictness (Section~\ref{sec:problem}).
\item A measurement protocol (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}, Section~\ref{sec:setup}): gold isolation with negative tests, one uniform error classifier, a matched rollout checkpoint across prompt, skill and harness optimizers, pre-specified paired contrasts with an equivalence rule, and final sets whose exposure we state exactly.
\item A matched comparison of \capevolve{} with nine other optimizers and two controls on three suites, run from official code where it exists, with configurations, costs and incidents reported (Section~\ref{sec:results}, Appendices~\ref{app:config} and~\ref{app:incidents}). \needs{Headline findings after the final evaluation.}
\item The gate trade-off as a finding. Our promotion rule is deliberately conservative: in a null search of about ten candidates it falsely promotes in \simPFalseB{} to \simPFalseC{} of searches, against \simKoneFalseB{} to \simKoneFalseA{} for a one-standard-error gate, and it promotes a true 8-point gain in \simPPowerEightB{} (Suite B) of searches. Earlier work argues that stock gates are lenient \citep{shawn2026pace}; we measure what the gate buys and costs in final-set score with stock-gate runs on Suites A and C (Sections~\ref{sec:method:P} and~\ref{sec:discussion}).
\end{enumerate}
Section~\ref{sec:method} describes \capevolve{}, whose editable surface is a parameter of one loop with two search algorithms, and Section~\ref{sec:related} places the study against prior work.
