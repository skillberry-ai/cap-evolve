\subsection{What promotion rule P changes}
\label{sec:results:p}

Rule P (Section~\ref{sec:method:P}) confirms a candidate on fresh tasks before promotion.
We measured its operating characteristic by simulation on the real gate code (\texttt{analysis/out/sim\_budget.md}).
With the campaign setting (three confirmation trials, $z=1.282$), the chance of at least one false promotion in a search of six all-null candidates is 0.050.
The one-stage gate with $k=1$ promotes a null candidate in 0.434 of such searches, and with $k=1.645$ in 0.199.
The price is power: for a single candidate with a true effect of ten points, rule P promotes it with probability 0.41 and the one-stage $k=1$ gate with probability 0.54.
Figure~\ref{fig:rulep} in the appendix plots both curves.
We do not claim rule P improves final scores.
Whether it does is the comparison of CapEvolve against its own one-stage variant, reported in Appendix~\ref{app:results}.

\subsection{Historical audit}
\label{sec:results:audit}

Earlier CapEvolve results on SpreadsheetBench reported large gains.
We recomputed \realAuditClaims{} numbers from the archived run artifacts (Table~\ref{tab:audit}).
\realAuditReproduced{} reproduce to the decimal, \realAuditContradicted{} are contradicted in framing and \realAuditNoArtifact{} have no artifact that produces them.
The arithmetic was mostly right.
The interpretation was not.

The largest problem is the scorer.
The historical scorer reads the workbook as saved, with no recalculation, so a model that writes a value in place of a formula passes.
Under LibreOffice recalculation, the Gemma-4-31B gain falls from +\realAuditGemmaReported{} to +\realAuditGemmaRecalc{} points.
The GPT-5.5 gain falls from +\realAuditGptReported{} to +\realAuditGptRecalc{} points.
Both scorers were applied to the same output files, so the comparison inside each scorer is fair, but most of the as-saved gain is compliance with a scoring convention and not task skill (Figure~\ref{fig:audit}).
Two further findings change how the old numbers can be used.
A hill-climb control from the same empty seed reached 85.6 on the same test tasks where the headline agent-optimize run reached 76.4, and the earlier write-up omitted it.
The test sets had also been seen by earlier optimizer runs, so no clean split remained, which is why Suites A and B use fresh final sets.
Nothing in the audit is evidence about the current campaign, and no historical number appears in the result tables.

\subsection{Operations}
\label{sec:results:ops}

The campaign ran on shared infrastructure with a rate-limited gateway, and several incidents changed what the data can support.
We list each one with the handling, because several of them acted like treatment variables.

Suite B was launched once with the wrong judge model and without rule P in the CapEvolve arms.
Those runs were stopped, labelled superseded and rebuilt from the campaign head with GPT-5.5 as judge.
None of their results is used.

Between 13:05 and 18:40 UTC on the first day, EvoSkill rollouts waited in the gateway queue until a client timeout.
The gateway still listed EvoSkill in its low-priority tags after the arm had been promoted, so its calls never obtained a slot on saturated deployments.
All EvoSkill rollouts after 13:05 are invalid and its three seeds restarted from scratch.
Priority and admission control behaves like a treatment variable, so we audit it per arm.
EvoSkill finished roughly five and a half hours behind the other Tier 1 arms and was expected to bind $B^\ast$.

About one to two and a half percent of Gemma calls failed with gateway 502 errors before a transparent retry was added at 13:11 UTC.
Because one failed call ends a rollout of about twelve calls, and the shared adapter scored such rollouts as zero for competitor arms but dropped them for CapEvolve, the bias fell on the competitor arms and grew with rollout length.
The gateway retry is identical for every arm.
Rollouts that failed before the fix are counted per arm, and we report a sensitivity analysis that treats them as missing.
A hung-rollout policy followed after ACE and MIPROv2 stalled for 84 to 114 minutes on a single rollout: a timeout counts as an infrastructure error with one retry, never as reward zero, and abandoned attempts are counted outside the rollout budget.

The ACE reflection prompts asked for a chain-of-thought field in the output.
The gateway returned an empty body with finish reason \texttt{content\_filter} for such prompts on Sonnet 5.5, which we reproduced with a minimal script.
ACE prompts were patched with a phrase substitution and the diff is kept as a deviation.
For HarnessLens a worker error is retried once and the retry counts inside the budget.
A streamed response that ended after HTTP 200 with no content, no finish reason and no usage is a truncated stream, and the gateway logged it as a success.
The metrics script counts these as truncated streams.

On Suite B the CapEvolve optimizer, which has a shell, could initially read the task file with gold actions, including the sealed test tasks.
We stopped both arms and relaunched them inside a mount-namespace shim with a credential limited to the campaign tag.
A scan of every earlier optimizer transcript found no read of the task files.
One residual remains and is disclosed: the agent-optimize arm runs the evaluator inside its shell, so its sandbox holds gold actions for its own 74 optimization and selection tasks, never for the sealed test set.
Selection-time comparisons for that arm on Suite B are flagged for that reason.
