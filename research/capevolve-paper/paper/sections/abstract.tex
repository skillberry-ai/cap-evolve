Published gains from agent-capability optimizers are hard to compare. They are measured on tasks the optimizer searched on, scored by checkers that reward output format, and produced by optimizers that could sometimes read the answers.
We audited our own earlier SpreadsheetBench results and found that \histGemmaCompliancePts{} of \histGemmaDeltaSaved{} points (Gemma-4-31B) and \histGptCompliancePts{} of \histGptDeltaSaved{} points (GPT-5.5) came from a scorer that rewards literal values over formulas, that the test split had been reused during development, and that at least \goldOptimizerRuns{} optimizer sessions had read gold files.
We built a measurement protocol that removes each problem: a functional scorer, gold isolation by process and mount, one shared promotion rule, matched search checkpoints, and fresh final sets.
Under it we compare \capevolve{}, a system whose editable surface is a parameter of one search loop, with the official implementations of GEPA, SkillOpt, Meta-Harness, EvoSkill, Trace2Skill, ACE, MIPROv2 and HarnessLens, a community port of WikiSkill, and unoptimized and one-shot controls, on SpreadsheetBench and two $\tau^2$-bench domains, with three search seeds per arm.
The promotion rule keeps the chance of a false promotion in a null search at \simPFalse{}, against \simKoneFalse{} for a one-standard-error gate, at the price of low power on small gains.
