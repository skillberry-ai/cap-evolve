Reported gains from agent-capability optimizers can be measurement error. In one widely used optimizer's own SpreadsheetBench history, \histGemmaCompliancePts{} of \histGemmaDeltaSaved{} points (Gemma-4-31B) and \histGptCompliancePts{} of \histGptDeltaSaved{} points (GPT-5.5) came from a scorer that rewards literal values over formulas. The test split had been reused during development, and at least \goldOptimizerRuns{} optimizer sessions had read gold files.
We build a protocol that removes each problem: a functional scorer, gold isolation by process and mount, one shared promotion rule, matched search checkpoints, and final sets that search never uses.
In a null search of about ten candidates, that rule falsely promotes with probability \simPFalseB{} to \simPFalseC{} depending on the suite, against \simKoneFalseB{} to \simKoneFalseA{} for a one-standard-error gate, and it pays with low power on small gains.
Under this protocol we compare \capevolve{}, whose editable surface is a parameter of one search loop, with nine other optimizers (official code for GEPA, SkillOpt, Meta-Harness, EvoSkill, Trace2Skill, ACE, MIPROv2 and HarnessLens, and a community port of WikiSkill) and with unoptimized and one-shot controls. The suites are SpreadsheetBench and two $\tau^2$-bench domains, with three search seeds per arm; the $\tau^2$ suites are descriptive.
