Agents run on frozen models, and files around the model set their behavior: the prompt, tool code, the tool list exposed over MCP, and skill packages. Optimizers edit these files, but each fixes what it may edit, usually the prompt, and trusts its own scores to decide what to keep. A fixed surface cannot repair a failure that lives elsewhere, and a self-scored accept rule promotes noise. An audit of an earlier version of our own optimizer shows the cost: scorer compliance explains \histGemmaComplianceShare{}\% of its reported Gemma gain and \histGptComplianceShare{}\% of its GPT-5.5 gain.
\capevolve{} makes the editable surface a parameter of one loop, keeps every decision an optimizer could game in core code it cannot edit (gold isolation, a gate, a final set scored once), and gives the optimizer a fixed/broke history of its earlier edits. It implements four surfaces and evaluates two, prompt and tool code, as a deterministic hill-climb or as a coding agent driving the same gate. The shipped configuration uses the stock gate.
We run it at a matched rollout checkpoint against adapted versions of nine published optimizers on SpreadsheetBench and two $\tau^2$-bench domains, and ablate the surface, the gate, the orchestration mode and the history on a retail ablation family. The design resolves differences of about 11 to 15 points between frozen artifacts, so we report paired estimates with intervals per suite and call smaller gaps inconclusive.
