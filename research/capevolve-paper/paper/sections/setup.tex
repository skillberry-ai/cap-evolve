\subsection{Suites, models and splits}

Table~\ref{tab:suites} lists the three suites. Suite A is SpreadsheetBench verified-400 \citep{spreadsheetbench} with Gemma-4-31B-it as the target, served by one RITS deployment. Suites B and C are $\tau^2$-bench retail and airline \citep{tau2-bench}, pinned at commit 8ebb7499, with gpt-oss-120b as target and user simulator. Natural-language assertions are graded by GPT-5.5 for every arm, because the benchmark default judge is not served by our gateway and the target model should not grade itself. Claude Sonnet 5.5 is the optimizer or reflection model for every matched arm. All model calls pass through one metering gateway, which tags each call with suite, arm, seed and role.

\subsection{Budget and matched checkpoint}

Each search may count 600 target rollouts, every evaluation included, and up to 4M non-cache optimizer tokens, with seeds 0, 1 and 2. Throughput on the single Gemma deployment (about 12 rollouts per hour per search) means slow arms will not reach 600. The user-approved protocol therefore compares at a matched checkpoint $B^\star$: the largest rollout count reached by every Tier 1 seed of every Tier 1 arm at the search cutoff (2026-10-13 06:00 UTC), rounded down to a multiple of 50 and floored at 200. $B^\star$ is computed from rollout counters only. The artifact scored for each arm is what that method would have deployed at $B^\star$ under its own native selection rule, using only information produced inside its first $B^\star$ counted rollouts. A seed below 200 rollouts is reported as incomplete and the arm is dropped from the matched table with the reason. The placeholder values are $B^\star=$ \mockBstarA{}, \mockBstarB{} and \mockBstarC{} for Suites A, B and C. \needs{Final $B^\star$ and the list of incomplete seeds, after the search cutoff.}

\subsection{Statistics}
\label{sec:setup:stats}

The unit of inference is the task, clustered by task family. We report three estimands. E1 is the score on the fixed final set, with execution noise only. E2 is the score on the population the final set was drawn from, with a family-clustered interval. E3 is the spread of champions across search seeds. The headline is the paired E2 contrast of each arm's artifact with the shared seed measurement, computed with cluster-robust (CR1) standard errors and a $t$ interval, and Holm-adjusted within each suite. With three seeds the pipeline-level interval has two degrees of freedom, so we report the three raw per-seed values next to every mean and make artifact-level claims only. We never report the standard error of the shared helper. A win requires a Holm-adjusted $p<0.05$ and an interval that excludes zero under the pessimistic missingness bound, where missing cells of the evaluated arm score 0 and those of the comparator score 1. Otherwise we write ``no detectable difference'' and give the minimum detectable effect, which is \mdeTwoEightyArtifact{} at $T=280$ and \mdeFortyArtifact{} at $T=40$ for artifact-level contrasts with three trials and three seeds. These values use variance inputs fixed before any pilot (Appendix~\ref{app:sim}). \needs{MDE recomputed from the no-op repeated-evaluation pilot (E02) and the final trial count.}

\subsection{Cost accounting}

Every call is priced from the gateway log. Claude models use list prices as a proxy; RITS and Azure-served models have no visible price, so their tokens are reported and never counted as zero, and totals that include them are marked lower bounds. So far the campaign has metered \costCalls{} calls and \costTokensM{}M non-cache tokens (Appendix~\ref{app:cost}).
