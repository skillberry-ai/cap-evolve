## Reviewer 3: Early-Career Researcher, Same Subfield (PhD Student)

### Summary
The paper argues that reported gains from agent-capability optimizers are often measurement artifacts. It audits the authors' own earlier CapEvolve results (scorer compliance, split reuse, gold visibility, gate strictness) and then builds a protocol: functional scorer, gold isolation, a shared promotion rule P, a matched rollout checkpoint B*, and fresh final sets with paired, family-clustered statistics. Under this protocol it compares two CapEvolve search algorithms with nine other optimizers and two controls on SpreadsheetBench and two tau2-bench domains, with three seeds per arm. Result tables are still mock.

### Strengths
- The motivating audit is concrete and checkable: 33.0 -> 18.7 and 49.1 -> 9.9, with Table 6 and Figure 3 showing that 31 of 40 historical claims reproduce, 2 are contradicted and 7 have no artifact. Reporting against one's own earlier draft is unusual and credible.
- Rule P's operating characteristics are given as numbers (Table 12, Fig. 7), and the paper says plainly that it is underpowered by design. The threat-model section (3.6) states what is not guaranteed.
- Section 8 pairs each limitation with what was done about it. Incidents (5.6, App. E) are disclosed per arm, including ones that hurt competitors.
- Table 4/5 columns (three token classes, wall time, missing cells) fit the cost and reproducibility expectations of the track. "No detectable difference plus MDE" is the right reporting convention.

### Weaknesses
- The text is very hard to follow for someone who has not lived inside the repository. Many terms are used before they are defined or never defined: RITS, Tier 1 (first appears in 4.3; Table 3's caption is the only gloss), E1/E2/E3, CR1, "pessimistic missingness bound", MDE, T, footprint, pregate, digest, ledger, "reward_gated", "Beta-posterior rule", "shared seed measurement".
- Raw code identifiers, flag names, file paths and issue numbers (`promotion_rule: P`, `confirm_fresh_only`, "issues #785/#786", `audit/exposure_ledger.csv`) are in the body prose of Sections 2, 3 and 5. Issue numbers also risk breaking anonymity.
- The data-exposure story is confusing. Table 2 lists opt/sel as 80/40 (A) and 49/25 (B), yet Section 3.1 and 4.1 say the stock CapEvolve arms use validation only (Dopt = Dsel = val, 40 tasks) while competitors use 120. Table 2 does not show this. The "74 optimization and selection tasks" in Sec 5.6/8 also needs a pointer to 49+25.
- The matched-checkpoint construction (4.3) is hard to follow and its mock output contradicts the setup. Table 3 says Trace2Skill, ACE and MIPROv2 are "cheap" arms with about 200 rollouts, but Table 4 shows 532 to 536 rollouts for all three. The one-shot control shows 10 rollouts, while Appendix A.4 configures max_metric_calls=200. Nearly every arm sits at 528 to 538 against B* = 540. If B* is the matching device, the table should show how each arm's artifact was reconstructed at B* and why counts differ. Also, "about 12 rollouts per hour per search" implies about 45 h to reach 540, yet the wall column shows 11 to 29 h.
- Several numbers disagree across sections. The metered totals are 113 059 calls, 584M tokens, $69 (4.5, App. F) versus 118 556 calls, 616M tokens, $82 (5.3). Appendix F's own footing differs by definition (non-cache vs "ledger" tokens). Suite A trials are 2† (5.1, Table 4's 720 cells) but the MDE statements assume three trials (4.4, App. B). Section 2.1 gives the recalculated Gemma end score as "75.4 to 78.6", which does not obviously equal 58.3 + 18.7 = 77.0.
- The 98.6% ceiling (2.1) accounts for the 4 unscorable tasks only. The 19 gold files that fail under LibreOffice 25.8 are mentioned in the same paragraph with no statement of how they affect the ceiling.
- Two different search lengths are used for the false-promotion numbers with no signal to the reader. The abstract, 3.3, 3.6, Table 12 and App. B use 12 candidates (0.055 / 0.565). Section 5.4 and Fig. 7 use six candidates (0.050 / 0.434). Fig. 7 "stricter" is 1.3% vs Table 12's 0.014, and "z=1.645" 2.6% vs 0.030.
- Section 3.3 has a broken passage: "...rather than as absent. (gate_footprint: false) and joint coverage (gate_coverage: joint), so the two algorithms share one acceptance rule." A sentence has lost its head. Table 1's right column is visibly cut off ("(budge", "pregate wa", "enfo", "incumbent wh").

### Detailed Comments
**Clarity and flow.** The paper packs an audit, a protocol, a statistics design and a benchmark into 10 pages and it shows. The Introduction lists five protocol parts, Section 3 describes the method as two algorithms plus rule P, and Section 4 re-describes the budget. A reader meets "rule P" (abstract), "B*" (abstract/intro) and "Suite A/B/C" before any definition. The author conventions allow these terms, but each should be glossed in one clause at first use (the abstract says only "one shared promotion rule"). Section 3 is the weakest for a non-insider. 3.1 formalizes (a, theta, D_opt/D_sel/D_fin, Eq. 1), then the next pages switch to a description of shipped code. I suggest moving Table 1 and the code-level description of default gates into an appendix, keeping in the body only rule P, the contract and the threat model, and returning the freed space to the page budget. Section 3.5 ("Optional mechanisms") says they are unused in the main comparison and are "not new statistics". It could be one sentence pointing to Appendix G.

**Notation.** Several symbol-level problems. (i) The SE gate threshold is k_se in 3.1 and k in Algorithm 1 and Section 3.3. (ii) The confirmation trial count is n_conf but Algorithm 2 uses an unexplained n_0 for the baseline measurement. (iii) The final-set number T (MDE "T = 280") is not defined, and the paper uses |D_sel|, "40 tasks" and T interchangeably. (iv) Delta-bar with and without tilde/hat/subscript c appears in 3.1, Algorithm 1 and Appendix G (hat Delta, Eq. 3) without a unified definition; Eq. 2 uses Delta_Y as per-task and r-bar_b for "the parent with the larger block mean" while Section 3.1 defines r-bar_n with a trial index. (v) E1/E2/E3 are called "estimands" but E3 ("the spread of champions across seeds") is a dispersion, not an estimand of the same kind; and the abstract's gains are in "points" while 3.3/Table 12 use fractions (+0.08) and Fig. 7 uses points. State one convention. (vi) The prime on a' and m' is lost in several places in the rendering (Algorithm 2 line 13 uses a_0 and a* with different roles); check that the seed and the candidate are visually distinct everywhere.

**Tables and figures.**
- Figure 1: text is about 5 pt at print size. The caption says teal shading means "fixed before any outcome and identical across arms, except competitors keep native acceptance rule", but the "promotion" box (which says "rule P or native rule per arm") is teal, which contradicts the caption. A figure that shows data flow per arm (what is held equal, what varies) would be more useful.
- Table 3: the "Notes" column wraps one word per line, and the tier distinction ("Tier 1" vs "cheap") is only in the caption, not in a column. Add a tier column and the native budget. "Suites" cells mix "A B C" and single letters with inconsistent centering.
- Tables 4/5: the design is mostly good, but ten numeric columns at print size are dense. Rollouts, Uncached, Cache rd, Cache wr, USD and Wall could move to a separate cost table so the effect table has room for per-seed values (4.4 promises "three raw per-seed values next to every mean", which these tables do not provide). "Missing" is shown as k/N but the pessimistic-missingness bound that defines a "win" (4.4) is not shown anywhere; add a column or a second CI. Table 5 pools two suites with different arm sets; HarnessLens appears on B/C but the text calls it "native tau2 fit".
- Figure 2: the y-axis labels appear only on the Suite A panel; the B and C panels have no labels and fewer arms, so the reader must infer row alignment from position (and HarnessLens is not in the label list at all, yet it is the leading arm in Suite B). In the mock, HarnessLens is drawn on the "ACE" row. Either label each panel, or use a shared arm order with blanks. Also, x-axis ranges differ across panels, which is fine, but say so. With 11 arms, marker shape and color together carry identity; this will be hard in greyscale. Filled vs hollow markers (Holm p < 0.05) is a small cue; mark significant arms with something that survives greyscale. In the mock, WikiSkill and Trace2Skill look partly filled although neither has p < 0.05 in Table 4, so check the encoding.
- Figure 3: clear. The right panel (counts of reproduced claims) answers a different question from the left and could be a one-line sentence. The left panel lacks intervals though Table 6 quotes them.
- Figure 4: legend order does not match drawing order, "mean over seeds" hides the seed spread (the bands are min to max of CapEvolve arms only). That is the opposite of 4.4, which says raw per-seed values are shown. Y-axes differ in range per suite.
- Figure 5: with 11 overlapping error bars at log scale the plot is nearly unreadable in Suite A. The frontier is drawn with a dashed step line without a legend entry. Cost for CapEvolve arms is "n/m" in Table 15, so this plot cannot be produced unless the dagger-recovered numbers are used; say which.
- Figure 6: fonts are about twice as large as in every other figure, only five arms are shown ("main arms" is undefined), and families are anonymous labels F1 to F6. Say what a family is (e.g., task-id prefix) in the caption.
- Figure 7: good concept. Left uses six candidates and right uses a single candidate; say so in the caption. The y-axis in the right panel is "%" while the x-axis is "points", while the text uses fractions.
- Table 15: the dagger here means "recovered from transcripts", but the paper's dagger macro means "mock value" everywhere else; use a different symbol. Column "Target tok." has no unit, and "Errors" is undefined. Rollouts here (e.g., ACE 68) are in conflict with Table 4's mock but that is expected; still, a table of in-progress numbers cannot be cited from the body (5.3 says "Table 15 gives the per-arm breakdown").

**Placeholder-prose and claims.** Following the stated convention, I do not critique mock numbers. But several body sentences state outcomes: "so it sets the floor that search must clear" is fine, but 5.1's "2 of the ten optimizer arms beat the seed" and 5.2's "Suite B gives the cleaner second test" are written as findings with daggers on the numbers only; the framing ("cleaner", "even at 40 tasks ... Suite C has half") is an outcome-flavored statement that should be conditional until real data exist. "If a control or a simpler method wins, that is the main result" (Intro) is a good pre-commitment but is not echoed in the Abstract. Section 6 says "Per-search gains under a 600-rollout budget should be small" which presupposes the answer. The abstract's final sentence ends at "three search seeds per arm" with a stray footnote mark and no result sentence; it reads as a methods note, not an abstract. The conclusion's "Whichever arm leads" is fine.

**Over-claims and logic.** (a) "We found no gold read in 2,049 retained agent rollouts" is correctly caveated in the next sentence, but the abstract's "gold isolation by process and mount" reads as stronger than Section 3.4(ii), which says proposer read isolation "is a deployment requirement" and that "the library does not sandbox it". (b) The audit says "the footprint concern rests on code and simulation" and the replay "is descriptive", yet the Introduction counts "a stricter gate would change 10 of 96 recorded decisions" as a contribution. (c) The claim that rule P "removes the selection bias from the reference" is argued but never measured. (d) Section 5.4's closing sentence ("they show the trade, not an improvement in final scores") is honest; but Fig. 7's title ("promotes false candidates rarely and real ones less often") is the headline of the section while Table 12 shows that nc=5, z=1.645 has both a lower false-promotion rate (0.019) and higher power at +0.06 to +0.10 than the chosen setting. The reader needs the rollout-cost reason (400 vs 240 confirmation rollouts per candidate) stated beside the table. (e) The paper compares competitors under their native gate but CapEvolve under rule P, and CapEvolve sees 40 tasks where competitors see 120. That is disclosed, but it means the headline cannot separate "search algorithm" from "gate" or "data", and the E12 ablation that would address it is still an open footnote. I would call the comparison "protocol-matched" rather than "like for like" until that ablation is in.

**Statistics.** The inference plan (CR1 with t intervals, Holm within suite, three seeds so two degrees of freedom) is a reasonable idea, but I could not tell from 4.4 whether the seed is a random effect in the headline CI or only in the "pipeline" interval, which the paper then declines to use ("artifact-level claims only"). If seeds are not in the interval, a "win" says the champion artifact beat the seed on this task population, not that the optimizer pipeline is better; the abstract and Section 6 should be explicit. The "pessimistic missingness bound" is mentioned in 4.4 and then not used visibly. Suite B/C MDEs (0.10 to 0.13) exceed every mock delta, so as designed the B/C tables can only produce "no detectable difference"; the paper should say what question those suites answer.

**Related work.** Prose is dense but accurate in outline. Citation formatting is uneven: "Anthropic" has no year in the intro, "Shawn, 2026" is cited as a surname (check that the author entry is not a given name), and "Wang et al., 2026a/b/c" are three different papers cited as "Wang et al." in one paragraph, which is hard to follow. The paragraph "Evaluation hygiene" cites many 2026 reports; consider trimming to the three most relevant.

### Minor Issues
- Abstract: "14 of 33.0 points" reads oddly; "14 of the 33.0 points" is clearer. "at least 6 optimizer sessions" should say "of how many".
- Abstract and Intro say "official code of nine other optimizers", but only five of them appear on Suites B/C and HarnessLens is absent from Suite A (and Table 4). Say which arms run where in the Intro, not only in Table 3.
- "RITS", "SkillOpt split", "capopt sandbox", "field renamed" (Table 3) are unexplained.
- Section 1: "Section 3.3 ... Section 4 ..." references are fine, but "Section 2" is cited for the exposure ledger and the ledger is a repo file; the Section 2 reader cannot see it.
- Section 2.2: three numbers (85.6, 76.4, 62.9) are given for runs of different type; add a small table or rewrite. Section 5.5 calls the 85.6 run "a hill-climb control from the same empty seed", Section 2.2 calls it "a one-iteration run"; use one description.
- Section 2.3: "dropping tasks inside a candidate's edit footprint" vs Table 1 "footprint-masked": a task in the footprint is set to zero, not dropped (Sec 3.2). Use one verb.
- Section 3.6: "Pr(Z > 1) = 0.16" fine; but "the Beta-posterior rule ... its sequential-validity wording in the code comments is not an established bound" is an internal remark, not for a reader.
- Section 4.3: "The user-approved protocol" is an odd phrase in a paper; drop.
- Section 4.3: B* = 540, 480, 420 are neither multiples of the same step nor obviously "rounded down to a multiple of 50" (540 is not a multiple of 50). Check the placeholder rule.
- Section 5.2 counts "40 test tasks" with a dagger, but Table 2 gives 40 sealed without a dagger.

- Appendix A.4: the per-arm settings dump (SkillOpt especially, with empty azure/minimax keys) is about 1.5 pages of unreadable run-record text. Summarize and ship the full dump as an artifact. Check the empty `*_api_key=` fields are blank by design.
- Appendix D: "Opus 5 optimizer" vs main text "Claude Sonnet 5.5"; clarify that the historical runs used a different optimizer.
- Table 6: first column header "Claim" with brackets "[29.1, 36.8]" has no unit; text uses "points".
- Table 12 caption: add n_c and z definitions and say that the "False prom." column is for 12 candidates and the other columns for one candidate.
- Eq. numbering: (1) in Section 3, then (2) and (3) only in the appendix; Eq. 1 is never referred to by number.
- Tables 4 and 5 use "pHolm" in the header and "p_Holm" in captions; font style for "HC", "AO" is not defined in the text (HC/AO appear in tables and figure legends but "hill-climb" and "agent-optimize" are spelled out in the text). Add the abbreviations at first use.
