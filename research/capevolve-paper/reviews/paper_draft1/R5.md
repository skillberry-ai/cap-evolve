## Reviewer 5: Writing Editor

### Summary
The paper argues that reported gains from agent-capability optimizers are partly measurement error. It audits the authors' earlier CapEvolve results (scorer compliance, split reuse, gold visibility, gate strictness), proposes a protocol (functional scorer, gold isolation, a shared promotion rule P, a matched rollout checkpoint B*, fresh final sets), and compares CapEvolve with nine official or ported competitors and two controls on SpreadsheetBench and two tau2-bench domains. The results sections are still mock, so I review the prose, structure, tables and figures as they stand.

### Strengths
- The honest-framing voice is real and unusual. Sentences such as "which is an absence of evidence" (2.2), "The headline is not [fair]" (2.1), "They show the trade, not an improvement in final scores" (5.4) and "If a control or a simpler method wins, that is the main result" (1) state limits without hedging mush. Keep this register.
- The hard bans are largely respected. I found no em or en dashes, none of the listed banned words, no "not only X but also Y", no rhetorical questions and no colon-titled section headings in the .tex. Short sentences are used well ("It is that nobody could tell from the draft.").
- Most captions of Figure 3 and the section headings of 2, 5.4, 5.5 follow the guide's "give the answer" rule ("Rule P cuts false promotion and costs power"; "Recalculation removes most of the historical gain").
- Limitations are written as "what we did and what remains" per item, which is the format a skeptical PC wants.
- Placeholders and open evidence are visibly marked, so over-claiming risk is concentrated in a few sentences rather than spread across the text.

### Weaknesses
- **Broken text in the compiled PDF.** In 3.3 the sentence that should begin "Both CapEvolve arms judge through rule P with the footprint mask off" is swallowed by a LaTeX `%` comment (the `% src (library defaults): ...promotion.py:28-35` line also contains the sentence's first half). The PDF reads "...undetected rather than as absent. (gate_footprint: false) and joint coverage (gate_coverage: joint), so the two algorithms share one acceptance rule." That is a fragment, and it carries the claim that both arms share one rule. Table 1 overflows into the right margin and is clipped ("(budge", "pregate wa", "never view a prose instruction, not enfo"). In Section 8 the italic item labels hang into the left margin and overprint the line numbers and text ("ign differences", "hed checkpoint", "orer and judge", and "Coverage." collides with body text). Section 8 also loses the end of two items in my text extraction, so check that nothing else is truncated.
- **The story is split across three papers.** The title says "comparison", the contributions list an audit, a promotion rule and a comparison, and the declared main result (which arm wins) is an empty footnote in the introduction, abstract and conclusion. The abstract ends on setup and contains no finding about any competitor. Section 2 (audit) plus Section 5.5, Table 6, Figure 3 and Appendices C and D take more space than the comparison that the title promises. The guide's "what, how, why, then the crux" is only satisfied for the audit.
- **Heavy repetition, which is also the page-budget problem.** The same numbers appear many times: 0.565 vs 0.055 (abstract, intro, 3.3, 3.6, 5.4, 6), +33.0 to +18.7 and +49.1 to +9.9 (abstract, intro, 2.1, 5.5, Table 6, Figure 3, 6, App. D), 85.6 vs 76.4 (2.2, 5.5, Table 6), the agent-optimize gold-access gap (3.4, 5.6, 8, Appendix), the 5.5-hour EvoSkill starvation (5.6, 6, App. E). Section 5.5 restates Section 2.1 almost sentence by sentence.
- **Overloaded, ambiguous vocabulary.** "Seed" means the unoptimized artifact ("paired with the seed", "shared seed measurement", "scores the best artifact and the seed"), the search random seed ("three search seeds") and the rollout seed in the same paragraphs. "E1/E2/E3" (estimands, 4.4) collide with "E02/E04/E10/E12" in the author notes and with Appendix E. "Tier 1" is used in 4.3 and Table 3's caption but no table marks tiers. "shared helper" (2.3, 4.4), "same-task luck" (3.6), "HC", "AO", "T", "CR1", "MDE", "RITS", "pregate", "probes", "footprint" are used before or without definition.
- **Over-claims and internal contradictions in the prose.**
  - "fixed before any outcome is seen" (intro, Figure 1) conflicts with the Suite B relaunch, the retry policy introduced at 13:11, the ACE/HarnessLens "deviations", and B* itself, which is computed from run outcomes (rollout counters).
  - "fresh final sets" and "untouched final tasks" (intro, Table 2, conclusion) conflict with Section 8, where Suite A residual ids were "scored in at most two earlier runs" and Suite C is exposed.
  - 3.1 says the method "returns the last promoted candidate, never the best-looking", but 3.4 says finalize "scores the best artifact".
  - 5.5 says the as-saved gain is "compliance with a scoring convention and not task skill". The recalculated scale also measures a convention, and the paper itself reports a 98.6% ceiling and 19 unscorable gold files.
  - "Evaluation hygiene is the oldest problem here" (Related Work) is a flourish with no citation.
- **Internal-process text leaks into a double-blind paper body**: "implemented as the shared module of issues #785/#786" (3.3), "The user-approved protocol" (4.3), "So far the campaign has metered..." (4.5), code identifiers and file paths as prose (`promotion_rule: P`, `reward_gated`, `-parent`, `audit/exposure_ledger.csv`, `analysis/out/sim_budget.md`, `scripts/derive_numbers.py`). The guide says not to use variable names from code in labels or prose.
- **Tables and figures do not yet answer the question** (details below): Tables 4 and 5 bury the one number that matters among 11 columns, Figure 2 mis-aligns rows across panels, and Figure 2 and Tables 4 and 5 show the same delta and interval twice.

### Detailed Comments

**Story level.** Decide what the paper is. The abstract's last sentence list ("a functional scorer, gold isolation by process and mount, one shared promotion rule, matched search checkpoints, fresh final sets") reads as an inventory. The guide's advice is to name one finding and cut the rest. The strongest single story is the one the brief gives: gains in this field are fragile, and under a fixed protocol the simpler method or a control may win. Then Section 2 should be a short motivating section (about one page: scorer, split reuse, gold access, with Table 6 and Figure 3 moved to the appendix), Section 5.5 should be deleted (it duplicates Section 2), and Section 3.3 and 5.4 should be the only places that give rule P's operating characteristics (abstract and intro may cite two numbers once). That recovers well over a page, which is the budget problem the authors raised. The SWE-bench pilot, SkillsBench and "tau2 held-out 30.0 to 47.5" rows of Table 6 appear nowhere else in the body, so they are darlings.

**Abstract and introduction.** The abstract opens well ("Much of the gain ... may be measurement error") but "14 of 33.0 points" is awkward: say "14 of the 33.0 points". "pays with low power" is a metaphor where "has low power" is exact. The abstract needs the finding in the last sentences, even if worded conditionally until the numbers exist; at present it stops at "three search seeds per arm". The introduction names no result (footnote 2). The paragraph "We therefore ask which optimizer finds the best artifact under a protocol fixed before any outcome is seen. The protocol has five parts." introduces "The protocol" before it has been defined, then lists five parts in one run of short sentences that mirror each other in rhythm. Contribution 3 ("a matched-checkpoint comparison with full configurations, costs and incidents") states what is done, not what is found; contribution 1 and 2 are findings. The roadmap sentence "Section 6 discusses the results and Section 7 places them against prior work" costs two lines and gives no information; the guide wants the intro to point to each section with its main finding.

**Section and paragraph level (skim test).** The skim test works for Section 2 and 5.4 to 5.6. It fails in four places. (1) 4.3: the first sentence gives the budget numbers, and the actual point (arms are compared at a matched checkpoint because slow arms cannot reach 600) arrives in sentence 4. Lead with the point. (2) 5 opening: "This section compares every arm... and then reports two measurements" is a roadmap, not a gist. (3) 5.1, 5.2: headings are topical ("Suite A, SpreadsheetBench"), and the paragraphs open with placeholders. When the numbers exist, the headings should state the result. The same holds for 3.2 "Two search algorithms in one notation" and 4.x. (4) The "Discussion" paragraphs 1 and 2 restate Section 5 and Section 3.3; the real discussion content (what a reader should do differently) is one sentence ("old leaderboards with the scorer in mind"). The Discussion's third paragraph repeats 5.6. Consider folding Discussion into a shorter Conclusion.

Related Work puts its closest competitors last. The final paragraph ("Evaluation hygiene") is the real positioning of the paper (Wang et al. 2026c, Nguyen et al. 2026, Dwork et al.) and should lead. The "Acceptance rules" paragraph lists CAPO, TRIPLE, BudgetAPO, SAGE, PACE and a "further paper" with half a clause each and differentiates none of them from rule P except in the last sentence. The guide asks for works organized by how each relates to or differs from yours. SAGE (paired one-sided test) and PACE (anytime-valid) are the closest and deserve a sentence on why they are not baselines.

**Sentence and word level.** Most issues are not AI-sounding vocabulary but over-long sentences with several clauses and weak endings. Examples. 3.6: "Rule P lowers the false-promotion rate but does not make it zero: the confirmation controls trial noise (seed-level luck), including the selection of a candidate on its own screen draws, and the unmasked vector removes the footprint mask as a source of inflated deltas" has three claims and the stress falls on "deltas"; split it. "It does not control same-task luck: an edit that is genuinely better on these tasks passes both stages" is cryptic; say that a real improvement on these tasks cannot be told apart from overfitting to them. 3.4: "Detection is host-independent but it is detection after the fact and does not stop reads" repeats "detection". "The statistics above are only as good as the measurements they consume" and "Two design points matter for the measurement contract" are transition filler that says nothing. 5.5: "The arithmetic was mostly right" is vague (whose arithmetic, and what does "mostly" mean?); the numbers follow in the same paragraph, so lead with them. Nominalizations: "adaptive reuse is a property of the implementation", "the confirmation of the promoted candidate, not its screen measurement, is the next baseline", "enforcement that the proposer does not read test outputs left on disk by earlier runs" (say "we do not prevent the proposer from reading test outputs..."). Heading wording such as "Infrastructure incidents shaped what the data supports" and "The protocol changes what a number means" is slogan-like; prefer the concrete claim ("Five infrastructure incidents invalidated or biased some arms"; "Gains are only comparable inside one scorer, split and checkpoint").

Style-rule tics: "rather than" appears 7 times and "disclose rather than hide it" twice (3.1 and Ethics); "Whichever arm leads" closes the conclusion in the same shape as the intro's "If a control or a simpler method wins". Run-in lead-ins in the Method and Limitations (Frozen model, editable artifact.; Tasks and splits.; Hill-climb.; Agent-optimize.; Evaluator isolation.; Test seal...; Power.; Exposure.) are the "bolded lead-ins inside paragraphs" the authors' own rules ban, even when set in italics. In Limitations the labels also break the layout (see Weaknesses); convert to plain topic sentences ("Three seeds limit power.") which also satisfies the paragraph-opens-with-its-point rule. The lists of exactly three recur for rhythm: "scorer compliance, split reuse and gold access" (stated three times), "screening, confirmation, merge verification, probes", "admission control, retry policy and timeouts", "pipeline, skills, context playbooks and harness search" (the intro citation sentence). Keep them only where the count carries content.

Tense: the guide asks for present for the paper and past for earlier work. 3.4 and 5.6 mix "we found", "was expected", "went in at 13:11" with present; acceptable, but pick one convention for incidents.

**Tables.** Table 1 is cut off on the right (it exceeds the column) and its cells are code strings ("reward_gated test with coded SE floor; Beta-poster...", "(-parent)", "screen.py"). Use words, define the one or two terms a reader needs, and move the rest to Appendix I. Table 2 is fine, but "fresh" in the Final column contradicts the Exposure limitation; use "unseen by search" or state the exposure count. Table 3 carries unexplained jargon in its Notes column ("capopt sandbox", "native tau2 fit", "field renamed (App. E)", "cheap") and its caption mentions "Tier 1" without a tier column; the justified narrow column wraps to four lines and leaves wide gaps. It is a candidate for the appendix, replaced by the single sentence list in 4.2. Tables 4 and 5 have 11 columns; the claim of the section (is any arm detectably better than the seed or the one-shot control?) needs Score, Delta with interval, p and perhaps USD. Move the three token classes, wall time and missing counts to the appendix cost table the paper already has (Table 15). Sort rows by delta (or put CapEvolve last, per the guide's "bar you care about on the right") instead of the arm-list order, and add the one-shot control directly under the best arm. The Table 5 caption repeats about 70 words of the Table 4 caption; write "As in Table 4." The captions start with "Suite A final-set results." rather than the claim they support; once the results exist, lead with it. The "0/720" missing denominators are unexplained (120 tasks x 2 trials x 3 seeds, but the reader must derive it). Table 6 packs eight claims in two columns at a font size that is smaller than the text; most rows are not discussed in the body.

**Figures.** Figure 1: the font in the boxes is about 5 pt at print size, the box text is jargon ("agent view, uid, mount", "from counters only"), the shading distinction (teal vs white) depends on colour, and the caption does not open with the point. The caption's "Search sees only the optimization and selection splits" is inaccurate for the stock CapEvolve arms, which read validation only (3.1). Figure 2: in panels B and C the five arms are plotted at row positions that match Suite A labels (the HarnessLens markers sit on the "ACE" row, One-shot on the last row), so a reader will misread them; give each panel its own labels or use a shared sorted axis with blank rows. Eleven arms are encoded by both colour and marker shape, plus thickness for CapEvolve and fill for significance; the guide asks for one salient channel. Use grey for competitors, one accent for CapEvolve, and mark significance in the table. The point (a gain only counts if the interval clears zero) is hard to see because x ranges differ by panel and the zero line is thin. The watermark crosses the data, which is fine for a mock, but the final plot must not rely on the watermark region. The caption begins "Paired delta to the seed" (a description) and ends with a useful sentence that should come first. Figure 2 and Tables 4 and 5 show the same delta and interval, so one of them belongs in the appendix. Figure 3's left panel is clear; the right panel is three numbers (31, 2, 7) and is better as a sentence. Colours (green, orange, grey) are acceptable for colour-blind readers but only because the bars are labelled; add direct labels in Figure 2 as well. Appendix Figures 4 to 8 (not reviewed in detail here) should be checked against the graph checklist (capitalized labels, no code names in axes such as "CapEvolve AO").

**Anonymity and internal references.** The body names GitHub issue numbers, internal commit hashes (Table 8) and file paths from the campaign repository. Hash pins belong in an appendix table, and the body should say "released with the artifact". A citation "Shawn, 2026" for "Shawn, Z." looks like a given name used as a surname; verify the bibliography entry.

**Over-claim sweep.** Beyond the items listed under Weaknesses: "Both scorers were applied to the same output files, so each comparison is fair inside its scorer" is true for the paired comparison but the abstract says "came from a scorer that rewards literal values over formulas" as fact about causes; the 14-point and 39-point attributions are differences between two scorers, so write "differ by" rather than "came from" unless a per-task attribution exists. "Rule P ... falsely promotes a null edit with probability 0.055" is a simulation result under the script's generative assumptions (the .tex comment says so); the abstract and intro should say "in simulation". "Only the sealed final split gives a number that is not selected on" (3.6) is too strong given Suite B and C exposure and the shared scorer feedback channel that carries gold-derived facts (Section 8). The 5.1 and 5.2 sentences "2 of the ten optimizer arms beat the seed" and "Suite B gives the cleaner second test" are template text with mock numbers; they must be rewritten rather than only re-filled, because "cleaner" is a judgment that the final data may contradict.

**Numbers that disagree within the text.** 4.5 reports 113 059 calls and 584M tokens, 5.3 reports 118 556 calls and 616M tokens (orange, in progress, but both appear in the submitted draft as current). 3.3 and 5.4 give false-promotion rates for different search sizes (12 vs 6 candidates) and different effect sizes (+0.08 in 3.3 and Discussion vs ten points in 5.4); a reader will think a number is wrong. Pick one search size and one effect for the body and move the grid to Appendix B.

- Appendix A.4 prints entire run-record dictionaries, including many empty or irrelevant credential-named fields (for example `minimax_api_key=`, `optimizer_azure_openai_api_key=`); trim to settings that differ from defaults and drop credential-named keys even when empty.
