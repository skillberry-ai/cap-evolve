## Reviewer 4: Adversarial Methodologist and Statistician

### Summary
The paper audits an earlier CapEvolve result: a scorer that rewards literal values, a reused test split, and optimizer sessions that read gold files. It then builds a measurement protocol (functional scorer, gold isolation, a two-stage promotion rule P tuned by simulation, a matched rollout checkpoint B*, fresh final sets, paired family-clustered Holm statistics). Under that protocol it compares two CapEvolve search algorithms against nine other optimizers and two controls on SpreadsheetBench and two tau2-bench domains, with three seeds per arm. Outcome numbers are mocked, so what can be judged now is the design, the audit, the simulations and the framing.

### Strengths
- The scorer audit (Sec. 2.1, App. B, Fig. 3, Table 6) is concrete and self-incriminating, and it recomputes both scorers on the same output files. The 14 of 33.0 and 39 of 49.1 decompositions are arithmetically consistent with the macros.
- The paper states what the protocol does not guarantee (Sec. 3.5, 8): no proposer sandbox in the library, per-run seals only, no control of same-task luck, and the shared SE helper not reported.
- Rule P is simulated on the real gate code with a pre-declared criterion, and the paper reports the power it gives up (0.27 at +0.08). Admitting "underpowered by design" is unusually honest.
- The incident log (Sec. 5.7, App. E) treats gateway priority, retries and timeouts as treatment variables, which is the right framing.
- B* is defined from rollout counters only and floored, with a rule for incomplete seeds. This is a defensible idea in principle (see W1 for what it does not fix).

### Weaknesses
- **W1. The central question cannot be answered by the statistics as designed.** The headline contrast is each arm versus the shared seed (Sec. 4.4). The paper's question is "which optimizer finds the best artifact" (Sec. 1), and the Intro says "if a control or a simpler method wins, that is the main result." Arm-versus-arm contrasts (CapEvolve vs best baseline, search vs one-shot) are not in the pre-specified family, the "win" definition (Sec. 4.4) or the tables. Tables 4-5 and Fig. 2 invite the reader to compare overlapping intervals, which is not a test. With S=3 the paper concedes only artifact-level claims (2 df at pipeline level). Those are claims about 3 frozen artifacts per arm, not about methods. The title, abstract and conclusion still speak of comparing optimizers.
- **W2. Matched checkpoint B* is a counterfactual that is not a run at budget B*.** "What the method would have deployed at B*" is reconstructed by truncating a run that was configured for another budget. The configs show budget-coupled schedules: SkillOpt `lr_scheduler=cosine`, `epochs=3`, `edit_budget`; GEPA `max-metric-calls=555` with Pareto and merge scheduling; WikiSkill `iterations=8`; EvoSkill `max-iterations=12`. For these, a truncated trajectory is not the artifact of a B*-budget run. The same configs show native iteration caps (CapEvolve `max_iterations=10` on Suites B/C versus 50 on A, `max_metric_calls=550` on B/C versus the stated 600) that may bind before rollouts do, so "equal budget" is not equal. Limitations says "Capacity, not the cap, bound the search", which makes B* a function of the slowest arm and of gateway luck (Sec. 5.7). The incident that moved the slowest arm (EvoSkill, 5.5 h) is exactly what sets B* for everyone. The paper needs a sensitivity analysis of arm ordering across several checkpoints, not one.
- **W3. Comparison fairness is asserted, not demonstrated.** "Official code" (abstract, Sec. 4.2) coexists with a community port (WikiSkill, counted in "nine"), EvoSkill with its task interface replaced, SkillOpt with its native gold-to-agent reports removed, ACE with a renamed output field, HarnessLens with a new retry policy, and Meta-Harness restarted several times. None of these deviations is quantified. No competitor is validated on its native benchmark under the adapter (a reproduction sanity check), so a low score cannot be separated from a broken port. Competitors keep native gates while CapEvolve's gate was tuned by simulation on its own search setting (Sec. 3.3), which is a design advantage for CapEvolve, not a neutral one. Conversely CapEvolve sees 40 tasks where competitors see 120 and spends 80 train rollouts on a baseline (the decision log says so; the paper does not), so the direction of net bias is unknown. HarnessLens is run only on Suite B, chosen because its paper evaluates tau2 retail natively, which means its defaults were developed on that domain.
- **W4. Optimizer compute is not matched, and the stated cap hides it.** The cap is "4M non-cache-read optimizer tokens". The mock tables (and the real cost table, Table 7) show cache-read volumes of tens to over a hundred million tokens for Claude Code arms against about zero for API-reflection arms. The comparison is therefore matched on target rollouts but not on optimizer work, agentic tool use, or wall-clock. Arms with an 80-300-turn Claude Code proposer versus a single reflection call are different amounts of intelligence per rollout. Cost is reported but never enters the claim.
- **W5. Isolation leaves a privileged-information asymmetry that is understated.** On Suite B, agent-optimize can read gold actions of its 74 optimization/selection tasks (Sec. 5.7, Limitations). The paper says only "selection-time comparisons are flagged." But gold actions for training tasks are demonstrations, and the resulting artifact is scored on the final set, so the advantage propagates to the headline number, not just to selection. Likewise the shared scorer feedback carries gold-derived facts (fill counts, error direction) to all Suite A arms, while SkillOpt's native gold reports were removed. Suite A gold isolation for agents rests on a negative test plus "2,049 rollouts with no gold read," which the paper itself calls absence of evidence (retained rollouts are validation-only).
- **W6. Final-set provenance and exposure are weaker than the labels.** Table 1 calls the Suite A final set "fresh", but the 239-id pool is "scored in at most two earlier runs" (Limitations), and exposure is judged by family-prefix matching (55 ids removed), which does not exclude near-duplicates across task families. Suite B's retail test-40 is called "sealed", yet the decision log records the retail exposure check as pending, the audit table lists earlier CapEvolve tau2 retail results, and Limitations concedes off-repository use cannot be seen. Retail test is the public benchmark split that HarnessLens, gpt-oss reports and others used. "Sealed" is about this campaign's runs only. Suite C is development-exposed yet appears in the Holm-adjusted table with p-values.
- **W7. Operating characteristics of rule P do not transfer to the experiments they are quoted for.** The 0.055 / 0.565 / power numbers come from val-40, 1 trial, 12 candidates, under generative assumptions not given in the paper body. Suite A has val-40, but Suite B selection uses 25 or 49 tasks with at most 10 iterations, and Suite C uses 10 selection tasks, where a z-test on paired deltas with coverage 0.95 is very coarse. The abstract and Intro state the false-promotion rates as properties of "the promotion rule" without the search length and task count. See also Minor issues for a unit error.
- **W8. Power and MDE rest on unverified variance inputs.** The MDE (0.13 at T=40, 0.05 at T=280) uses a per-task advantage SD of 0.15 and was "fixed before any pilot". For tau2 tasks with binary or near-binary rewards and a simulated user, paired per-task differences plausibly have SD well above 0.15, which would roughly double the MDE. The quoted MDEs are for three trials, but Suite A's actual T and trials are decided by capacity (the decision log targets T of 60-80 with n=1; Table 1 mocks 120), so neither quoted T (280, 40) is the Suite A design. The E02 pilot is pending; until then Sec. 4.4 and Limitations quote numbers a reviewer will treat as optimistic.
- **W9. Missingness and retry asymmetries contaminate search, not just scoring.** The sensitivity analysis (Sec. 5.7) treats pre-13:11 failures as missing, but those zeros affected competitor candidate scores during search, so the selected competitor artifacts may be worse for reasons a final-set sensitivity analysis cannot undo. HarnessLens gets a different retry policy (retry once inside budget, then 0 for internal selection) from the CapEvolve `fail` policy and the global hang rule. The "pessimistic bound" (missing evaluated cells 0, comparator cells 1) is extreme and will make intervals uninformative unless missing counts stay near zero; it is unclear which interval Tables 4-5 print, the primary or the bound.
- **W10. Forking paths in the campaign are real and only partly disclosed.** The roster changed after launch (EvoSkill promoted to Tier 1 at 09:55, HarnessLens placed on Suite B and chosen "a priori" by its authors' benchmark, Suite C added at 17:40, ACE/MIPRO/HarnessLens dropped or not run on B), AO was re-configured twice (engine correction, active_eval flipped on), rule P's operating point was re-chosen after a first choice, and E12 (stock gate vs rule P) was dropped for capacity. The paper claims a protocol "fixed before any outcome" (Fig. 1, Sec. 1). A dated changelog of every change and the information the authors had at the time belongs in the paper, not only in the decision log.
- **W11. No information about whether searches changed anything.** Rule P is deliberately conservative, so many CapEvolve seeds may return the seed artifact (delta exactly 0). The tables do not report the fraction of seeds whose B* artifact differs from the seed, the number of accepted candidates, or the per-seed deltas, although Sec. 4.4 promises raw per-seed values next to every mean.

### Detailed Comments

**Claims versus design.** The abstract's first sentence ("Much of the gain that agent-capability optimizers report may be measurement error") generalizes from one lab's own earlier draft to the field. The evidence is a single self-audit plus a literature survey of other papers' split reuse, with no re-measurement of any other optimizer's published claim. Scope it to what was shown. The abstract also says the protocol "removes each problem". Sec. 3.5 and App. E list gold-read gaps (Suite B AO), unenforced proposer isolation, and per-run only seals, so "mitigates and records" is what the evidence supports. "A win requires Holm p<0.05 and an interval excluding zero under the pessimistic bound" is a sound, strict rule, but it is a rule against the seed only (W1). Add a pre-specified contrast of each CapEvolve arm against the best non-CapEvolve arm and against the one-shot control, in the Holm family, using the same cluster-robust machinery. State that with S=3 any between-method statement is conditional on the drawn seeds.

**Prose that will read as a finding once placeholders fill.** Sec. 5.2: "Suite B gives the cleaner second test, because its test tasks are sealed" is a pre-written judgment of the result's credibility, and given W6 it should be hedged until the exposure check is done. Sec. 5.1: "The one-shot control ... sets the floor that search must clear to justify its rollouts" is fine as a design statement but then promises "say that search adds nothing measurable here" conditionally, which is acceptable only if the condition is a pre-registered threshold (within one interval is not a threshold; an equivalence margin is). Sec. 6, "a competitor with a looser native gate may promote more edits, both real and spurious," is speculation presented as a consequence. Sec. 5.5 ("rule P cuts false promotion and costs power") is a simulation result and is labelled as such, but its title reads as a finding about the system; keep "in simulation" in the heading. The Conclusion's "let others check a gain before they believe it" is an unevidenced benefit claim.

**Statistics and inference.** (a) Specify the cluster unit for Suites B and C. tau2 tasks have no families, so clusters are tasks (19-39 df) and the family-clustered E2 is undefined there. (b) The shared seed measurement is one realization with its own noise; all deltas share it, which is acceptable for Holm but means the seed's trial count sets a floor on every interval. State how many seed trials are taken (Table 4 shows 0/240 missing for the seed versus 720 for arms, implying a different trial count). (c) tau2 trials use temperature 0 on gpt-oss with a simulated user; repeated trials of the same task are far from independent, so the "three trials" in the MDE overstate information. (d) Report E1/E2/E3 explicitly in the tables. Currently one number per arm appears and the Score column does not say which estimand it is. (e) Equivalence testing (TOST against a stated margin) is the right tool for "no detectable difference" claims; reporting the MDE alone invites readers to read non-significance as equivalence, which the paper elsewhere warns against.

**Rule P and the simulation.** Put the generative model of sim_budget in the body (null task-level noise, correlation across trials, the incumbent's measurement noise), because every operating characteristic cited in the abstract depends on it. Report the false-promotion rate per candidate as well as per search (0.055 over 12 candidates is about 0.5% per candidate; the abstract's wording suggests the former is a per-edit probability). Add the same table for Suite B and C configurations (selection 25 or 49 tasks, fewer iterations, n_conf=3), since those are the settings in which rule P will actually run. The claim that confirmation "removes selection bias from the reference" is plausible, but the paper offers no check that the confirmed baseline is unbiased in simulation.

**Leakage.** The negative-test evidence for proposer isolation is the best argument in the paper and should be shown as a table: for every agentic proposer on every suite, the test performed, the date, the readable-path result, and whether the arm ran before or after the fix. App. E says Suite A proposer user could read split-id files until 10:00; state which runs started before that time and whether they were restarted. For Suite B AO, either exclude the arm from the headline Holm family, or run a matching arm without gold actions, or show via an ablation that the artifact contains no task-specific content from the 74 tasks (an artifact-content scan, since the artifact is a text diff and can be audited).

**Budget and cost fairness.** Add one matched-cost axis: report the headline contrast also at matched optimizer USD or uncached plus cache-write tokens, and show that the arm ordering at B* is stable under B*-25% and B*+25% (for arms that reached them). Table 3 lists "cheap" arms at native budgets (Trace2Skill about 200, ACE and MIPRO below the cap); an arm that stops at 70-120 rollouts is being compared at a different operating point than one at 540, and the Holm family mixes them. Say whether cheap arms are in B*'s definition (the text says Tier 1; the cost-so-far table shows ACE at 68 and MIPRO at 123 rollouts, below the 200 floor that would drop an arm).

**Tables and figures.** Table 4/5: no per-seed values, no accepted-candidate count, no "artifact differs from seed" indicator, no column distinguishing the primary interval from the pessimistic-bound interval, the cheap arms share a column with Tier 1 arms, and the caption does not define the scored trials. Both tables at `\scriptsize` with 11 columns will be hard to read at print size; move the token columns to the appendix and keep Score, Delta, CI, p, rollouts, USD, missing. Fig. 2: each panel has its own x-axis range, which hides that Suites B and C intervals are about twice as wide as A; share a scale, add the per-seed points (the three raw values the text promises), and do not use different marker shapes and hues per arm without a legend key tying them to the table. The Suite C panel should be visibly marked as exposed and without significance fill. Fig. 5 (Pareto) uses USD at Sonnet list price, which is zero for the RITS-served targets and unmetered for several arms ("n/m" in Table 7), so a frontier on this axis would mislead; plot tokens including cache classes or wall-clock as the primary axis. Table 7 and Fig. 6 are real in-progress data and fine, but "n/m" for CapEvolve HC/AO on Suite A is a metering hole in exactly the arms under study; resolve before submission. Fig. 3 and Table 6 are good; Table 6 would benefit from a column giving the verdict (reproduces / contradicted / no artifact) so the "31/2/7" breakdown is visible.

**Operations as confounds.** The EvoSkill story shows the right instinct, but the same class of failure (priority tags, 600 s x3 litellm timeout stacking, saturated RITS deployments with ~127 calls/min) may have shifted other arms silently. Report queue-wait distributions per arm (the metrics pipeline has them) and a check that per-rollout latency tails do not differ by arm in ways that interact with the 30-turn limit or the 1800 s timeout. Different arms use different concurrency (4 rollouts for CapEvolve versus others), which changes how many rollouts straddle B* and are excluded.

**Judge.** GPT-5.5 as judge differs from the benchmark default (gpt-4.1), and the sensitivity result is a placeholder. Because the judge is a different model family from the target but the optimizer (Claude) sees judge-derived feedback, state whether feedback to optimizers includes judge rationales. The judge sensitivity analysis should be run on all arms' final artifacts, not a verdict sample, and its outcome should be able to change the headline (pre-state the criterion).

### Minor Issues
- Abstract: "falsely promotes a null edit with probability 0.055" is a per-search probability over 12 candidates; say so. Sec. 5.5 uses a different search size (six candidates: 0.050 versus 0.434), so the paper quotes three different false-promotion numbers for the same rule; reconcile or label.
- Abstract and Sec. 1: "official code of nine other optimizers" contradicts "community port of WikiSkill" and EvoSkill's replaced interface; say "eight official-code methods plus a port".
- Sec. 3.3 says agent-optimize has `active_eval` off, App. A.2 says active evaluation switched on, and the decision log says on (engine correction, 15:30). The method text is wrong.
- Sec. 3.3 says the confirmation cost is `2|D_sel| n_conf`; Sec. 3.3 also says the stock CapEvolve arms leave train unused, while the decision log says the stock baseline spends 80 train rollouts. State the effective rollouts available for search per arm.
- Table 1 caption and Limitations disagree on how many ids are removed by family prefix only if checked against the 55 in the decision log; make one number appear once.
- Sec. 4.3: "the user-approved protocol" is not a methodological justification; replace with the reason (capacity) and the pre-commitment date.
- Table 3 caption says "Pins are in App. A" but Table 9 leaves five arms (EvoSkill, Trace2Skill, ACE, MIPROv2, HarnessLens) with no pinned commit ("not recorded"), the exact arms the paper calls official code.
- Generated config appendix lists secrets-adjacent and irrelevant empty fields (e.g. `minimax_api_key=`, `copilot_*`); filter to non-empty settings and mark stray backticks (`k-se=1.0``).
- Seed row in Tables 4-5 has Missing denominators (240, 120, 60) that imply fewer seed trials than arm trials; define.
- The Holm "ten optimizer arms" on Suite A includes arms with unequal budgets and two CapEvolve variants; say what the family size is and whether controls are in it.
- "Cache rd/wr" abbreviations in the table header are unreadable at print size; use a spanning header.
- The body exceeds the limit, and the long appendix with the full per-arm config dump could be moved to the artifact repository with only a summary kept.
