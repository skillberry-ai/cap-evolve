## Reviewer 2: Mid-Career Researcher, Same Subfield (Postdoc / Senior PhD)

### Summary
The paper argues that reported gains from agent-capability optimizers are confounded by measurement problems. It audits the authors' own earlier CapEvolve results: scorer non-recalculation, test-split reuse, gold visibility and gate strictness. It then defines a protocol with a recalculating scorer, gold isolation, a shared promotion rule ("rule P"), matched-rollout checkpoints, fresh final sets and clustered paired statistics. Under that protocol it compares two CapEvolve search algorithms against up to nine published optimizers and two controls on SpreadsheetBench and two tau2-bench domains. Final numbers are mocked, so the claims I can assess are the audit, the protocol design, the simulated operating characteristics of rule P, and the design of the comparison.

### Strengths
- The audit is concrete and honest. It recomputes the authors' own prior headline gains (+33.0 to +18.7, +49.1 to +9.9) from retained artifacts and reports the 40-claim reproduction outcome (Table 6, Fig. 3). Self-correction of this kind is rare, and the scorer finding (cached-value reads versus formulas, 282/400 gold tasks with formula cells) is a real pitfall others in this niche will recognise.
- The measurement contract (Sec 3.4) is unusually explicit about what is enforced by code and what is only a deployment requirement. The limits of proposer isolation are stated plainly rather than implied.
- Rule P's operating characteristics come from a budget-exact simulation on the real gate code with a pre-declared selection criterion (App. B, Table 12), and the paper admits the rule is underpowered by design.
- Incidents (gateway priority starvation, adapter zero-versus-drop asymmetry, ACE content filter) are disclosed with their effect on the data (Sec 5.6, App. E). Admission control as a hidden treatment is a useful observation.
- The estimand discussion (E1/E2/E3, 2 df at three seeds) and the refusal to report the shared helper's SE are the right instincts. Cost is reported as tokens by class and never as zero when unpriced.

### Weaknesses
- **The core comparison is confounded by design in the direction the paper claims to control.** CapEvolve arms use rule P, which is very strict, while every competitor keeps its native gate (Sec 3.3, 4.1, 6). Rule P promotes a true +0.06 edit with probability 0.17 and a true +0.04 edit with probability 0.09 (Table 12). A CapEvolve arm will therefore often return the seed artifact or a single early promotion. The paper says rule P exists so the two CapEvolve algorithms are comparable to each other, but the headline claim is CapEvolve against nine others. The E12 ablation (one-stage versus rule P) is flagged "EVIDENCE NEEDED, or state that the campaign has no such ablation". The repo's decisions.md says E12 will not get a live arm for capacity reasons. As written, a positive or negative CapEvolve result cannot be attributed to search versus gate.
- **Data-exposure and proposer-capability confounds are only disclosed, not controlled.** CapEvolve sees 40 validation tasks while competitors see 40 selection plus 80 train (Sec 4.1). The seed baseline evaluation on train-80 is charged to CapEvolve's 600 rollouts (decisions.md), and I could not find this in the paper. Competitors' reflection LLM calls are not equivalent to a Claude Code agent with 80 to 300 turns and shell access. Suite A restricts CapEvolve to `capabilities=system-prompt` (App. A.4), so the paper's central selling point, a variable editable surface, is not exercised on the one suite where most competitors run. Without an equal-data CapEvolve arm and a surface-width arm (E16 is not reported), there is nothing to separate "CapEvolve loses" from "CapEvolve is handicapped".
- **The experiment is underpowered for its own contrasts, and the tables do not report the contrasts the claims need.** The paper's own MDE is 0.13 at T=40 and about 0.08 to 0.10 near T=100 to 120 (App. B). Pipeline-level MDE is 0.26 at T=40. Suite B (40 tasks) and Suite C (20, exposed) will almost surely show "no detectable difference" for every arm, and the mocked deltas of 2 to 8 points are far below MDE. Suite C is not rankable, and Suite B's MDE is of the same order as any plausible gain. Tables 4 and 5 report only each arm versus seed, Holm-adjusted. Statements such as "if a control or simpler method wins, that is the main result" (Sec 1) and "search adds nothing measurable" (footnote 7) need arm versus one-shot and arm versus best-baseline contrasts. The analysis plan lists method-versus-method contrasts in the frozen family, but the paper does not carry them. Comparing each arm to the seed and eyeballing overlapping intervals invites the standard "difference between significant and not significant" error.
- **"Nine competitors" is much thinner than advertised.** Per Table 3, Suite B and C contain only GEPA and HarnessLens (B only), plus CapEvolve and controls. Eight methods run on Suite A, and three reproductions failed. Cross-suite claims (footnote 8: rank correlation of paired deltas between suites) cannot be computed on two to four arms. The abstract and Sec 1 should state the true matrix.
- **The matched-checkpoint protocol has unresolved validity and consistency issues.** (a) B* is bound by the slowest Tier-1 arm (EvoSkill, restarted 5.5 h late). The checkpoint is therefore set by an infrastructure accident, and the paper concedes "capacity, not the cap, bound the search". B* of 420 to 540 target rollouts gives CapEvolve only a handful of candidates once confirmation costs 2|Dsel|n_conf = 240 rollouts per screen-passing candidate. (b) Reconstructing "what the method would have deployed at B*" from logs requires per-method reconstruction scripts. The paper should state which methods' intermediate artifacts are exactly reconstructible and which are approximated, and give the straddling-candidate rule. (c) "Cheap" arms (Trace2Skill about 200, ACE, MIPROv2) run natively below the cap. The protocol says a seed under 200 rollouts is dropped from the matched table, and Table 15 shows ACE at 68 and MIPROv2 at 123 rollouts. Yet mocked Table 4 shows ~530 rollouts for all three. Either they are exempt from the 200 floor (then say so), or they leave the headline table. (d) Rollout counts are not comparable across methods without a definition: GEPA `max-metric-calls=555`, SkillOpt `max-rollouts=600`, and CapEvolve counting confirmations.
- **Rule P's reported operating characteristics are simulation-only and its calibration is partly internal.** The 0.055/0.565 figures come from a normal-model simulation (val-40, one screening trial) whose noise parameters (dispersion 1.5, mean 0.55) are not shown to match Gemma/gpt-oss rollout noise. The abstract states them as properties of the protocol. They should be validated on the no-op repeated-evaluation pilot (E02, currently a footnote). More importantly, no false-promotion rate is given for competitors' native gates, though Sec 6 promises it (E04, footnote 12). Without those, "separate search quality from gate strictness" is not achievable. The gate also consumes the shared SE helper that the paper says overstates variance by 12 to 33% at three trials (App. B). The paper should say clearly that the quoted operating points are for the code as run, not for a textbook paired test.
- **Several framing claims outrun the evidence.** "Most of the historical gain was scorer compliance" (Sec 5.5 title) holds for GPT-5.5 (about 80%) but not for Gemma (14 of 33, about 43%). The sentence "most of the as-saved gain is compliance with a convention and not task skill" is an interpretation. A model that writes literals where the benchmark wants formulas is, for a deployment that cares about formulas, arguably wrong rather than merely "compliant", and for one that wants values, correct. The paper should say which reading it takes. The abstract's "may be measurement error" is softer than the body. The Sec 2.3 replay ("a stricter gate would have blocked the champion path in 6 of 16 runs whose champions later gained on the exposed test split") reads as evidence against strict gates, which the protocol then adopts. The paper does not reconcile the two.
- **"Fresh final set" is overstated for Suite A.** Table 2 says "120† fresh"; Limitations say residual ids were "scored in at most two earlier runs (score only, never for design)", and the sample comes from an older 912-task release after excluding 55 ids by family prefix. That is lightly exposed, not fresh. Decisions.md uses the more accurate label "lightly exposed (score-only)". The word "untouched" in the Conclusion has the same problem. Suite B's "sealed" test cannot rule out off-repository use, as the Limitations admit. Contamination of Gemma-4 and gpt-oss on public benchmarks is not discussed.
- **Cost and metering claims are internally inconsistent.** Sec 3.1/4.1 say all model calls pass through one metering gateway, but Table 15 shows CapEvolve Suite A optimizer usage as "n/m" (unmetered, own client). The largest-spending arms on Suite A therefore have no cost in the table that is meant to price the comparison. The optimizer cap is 4M non-cache-read tokens, yet the placeholder Table 4 shows 14 to 43M "uncached" tokens and Table 15 already shows Trace2Skill at 5.61M. The cap is either not enforced, or measured differently, or the column means something else. Totals disagree across sections: 113,059 calls and 584M tokens (Sec 4.5, App. F) versus 118,556 calls and 616M (Sec 5.3), and "$69" versus "at least $82".
- **Competitor fairness is not documented enough.** Per-arm settings for MIPROv2, ACE, Trace2Skill and EvoSkill are `max-rollouts=600` and little else (App. A.4). It is not stated whether competitors were run at defaults, tuned, or given pilot runs, or whether authors spent more effort on their own arm. ACE and HarnessLens needed "prompt and retry changes". SkillOpt's gold-to-agent reports were removed (decisions.md, not in the paper). WikiSkill is a community port. A skeptical PC will ask for a per-competitor deviation table and a statement of tuning effort.

### Detailed Comments
**Protocol and statistics.** I like that the unit of inference is the task, clustered by family, and that the artifact-level estimand is stated openly. But the paper's selling point is a *comparison*, and the current analysis (Sec 4.4, Tables 4/5, Fig. 2) is a set of one-versus-seed contrasts. Please add: (i) each arm versus the one-shot control and versus the best non-CapEvolve arm, within a pre-registered family; (ii) a table of the number of seeds per arm whose returned artifact is byte-identical to the seed. With rule P at power 0.09 to 0.27 for effects of 4 to 8 points, I expect a nontrivial share of CapEvolve seeds to return the seed, which makes E3 (champion spread) degenerate and the "gain" mostly a count of lucky promotions. (iii) The pessimistic missingness bound (missing cells of the evaluated arm score 0, the comparator's score 1) is very conservative. With missing counts like 5/720 to 8/360 it may dominate the interval, so report intervals both ways. (iv) The number of trials is 2 on Suite A (mock) while the MDE in Sec 4.4 is stated at three trials; state the MDE at the actual n.

**Rule P and the budget.** The 600-rollout budget cannot afford the confirmation design. Screening costs |Dsel| = 40 per candidate (25 on Suite B, 10 on C), and each promotion candidate costs 2 x 40 x 3 = 240 more. A search can afford about 15 screens and at most two to three confirmations. Appendix B simulates 12 candidates per search; please show the distribution of candidates actually evaluated per seed in the real runs (the audit tables will have this) and say whether the simulation budget accounting matches. On Suite B (49 opt tasks, decisions.md notes 150 to 300 rollouts per candidate) this cost is even larger, and Table 15 shows Suite C searches at 60 to 80 rollouts, which cannot support more than a couple of iterations. The paper should plainly tell readers that Suite C CapEvolve arms are close to one-shot.

**Suite design.** Suite C (20 exposed tasks, MDE ~0.2) contributes essentially nothing and costs page space and capacity. Either drop it from the headline analysis (it is already excluded from ranking) and move it to the appendix, or enlarge it using the train split under a held-out protocol. For Suite B, 40 tasks with a simulated user and a GPT-5.5 judge is noisy; the judge sensitivity result (footnote 13) is essential, as is the repeated-evaluation noise floor for the seed itself (three seed ctrl runs already exist in Table 15 and give a free noise estimate).

**Audit section.** The scorer finding is the paper's most reliable contribution. Two comments. First, the numbers in Sec 2.1 are hard to track: "58.3 to a recalculated 75.4 to 78.6 (+18.7)" gives a range for the champion, and 76.5 appears in Appendix D as the as-saved champion. Present a single small table (baseline and champion, as-saved and recalculated) in the body; Table 13 is in the appendix but is the best evidence. Second, App. C reports 25/30 under LibreOffice 7.1.8.1 and 29-30/30 under 25.8, while Sec 2.1 says "29 to 30 of 30" without the version caveat. Because the recalculation scorer itself fails on 19/400 gold outputs, say how that interacts with optimizing against it, since a prompt can learn the scorer's quirks. The compliance diagnostic tests only one shortcut (Limitations).

**Gold access and isolation.** "No gold read in 2,049 retained rollouts" is correctly labelled absence of evidence, but those rollouts are validation-only, which makes it nearly uninformative for the question that matters (test-time reads by optimizer sessions). The "at least 6 optimizer sessions" count is a lower bound from notes. Give the denominator (how many sessions in total) and what fraction of sessions were reviewable. The negative-test claim ("verifying denial before launch") should state what the test attempted. An open gap remains: agent-optimize on Suite B runs its evaluator in its own shell and can read gold actions of its 74 optimization/selection tasks. Decisions.md also records a shared scorer-feedback channel that carries gold-derived facts for all arms, and a scanner that "found no read" for a proposer that, per Sec 3.6, is not sandboxed. State in the Method that Suite A competitor proposers ran as `capopt` users and in mount-namespace sandboxes, and say whether CapEvolve and every competitor got the same isolation level at all times (EvoSkill, Meta-Harness and the Suite B AO relaunches show it was not, early on). Which of these early gaps affect arms in the final table, and which runs were discarded?

**Reproducibility.** Positives: pinned commits with blob checks, config tables generated from run records, derive_numbers script. Concerns: (a) pins are given by short hashes for the authors' own system and SHA of the "campaign branch" only; the MLSys reviewers need an anonymised artifact. (b) The "most complete community port" of WikiSkill is selected by a criterion that is not stated. (c) The seed artifact (prompt.md + task_template.md) is not described; baseline strength matters a lot to how much room an optimizer has. (d) Judge settings and rollout seeds for tau2 are given, but not how many trials the seed ctrl gets relative to arms.

**Internal inconsistencies I would fix before submission.**
- Sec 3.3 says agent-optimize runs with "active_eval off", while App. A.2 and decisions.md say active evaluation is on and the stock default selects an archived engine. Sec 3.3 should be corrected, and the paper should state that stock defaults were overridden.
- Table 3 lists the one-shot control for B and C only, but Table 4 shows it for Suite A.
- Sec 4.1 says Suite A uses SpreadsheetBench verified-400 with the SkillOpt 80/40/280 split, while the final set is drawn from the 912-task release. Say clearly that the final set is outside the 400.
- The abstract's "41" and other footnote numbers adjacent to claims render as superscripts that read like numbers in the PDF (e.g. "three search seeds per arm. 41"). Move the placeholders to margin notes in the next draft, or the pending text will be misread as data.

**Figures and tables.** Table 1 overflows the right column in the PDF, with agent-optimize cells clipped ("budge", "wa", "enfo"), so the comparison it exists to show cannot be read. Tables 4 and 5 are very wide and information-dense (ten columns including three token classes and USD), and at print size the main signal (delta, interval, p) is lost. I would keep Score, Delta [CI], pHolm, Rollouts, USD in the body and move token classes to the appendix. They should also contain a column or row for the contrast against one-shot. Fig. 2 puts three suites on different x-axes without a shared reference, and the CapEvolve arms are distinguished only by thickness, which is fragile for colour-blind readers and in greyscale; add direct labels or marker shapes. Fig. 3 right panel (31/2/7) is a three-bar summary with little information; fold it into one sentence. Fig. 4 shows bands "min to max of the CapEvolve arms" only, so competitors have no uncertainty. Fig. 5 uses a log cost axis with arms whose cost is unmetered (n/m); say how those are placed. Fig. 6 (task families with n=12 to 31) has intervals so wide that it cannot support any family-level statement. Fig. 7's left panel uses six-candidate searches while the text elsewhere quotes twelve (0.055 versus 0.050), which confuses the reader. Fig. 8 is a diagnostics figure with no role in the claims and could go. Also consider whether the "PLACEHOLDER" watermark layout hides the data region when real numbers come in.

**Page budget.** The body exceeds ten pages. Candidates to cut or move: Sec 3.5 (optional mechanisms not used in the main comparison; already duplicated in App. G), the long Sec 3.6 enumeration (compress to a table of "enforced / detected / not provided"), Table 1 (move to appendix or shrink to three rows), most of Related Work's acceptance-rule paragraph (a one-sentence pointer per cluster), and Fig. 3 right panel. The paper's novelty is the protocol, the audit and the comparison, not the two CapEvolve algorithms, so descriptions of the algorithms should be proportionate.

**Prose and over-claiming.** Overall the prose is clear. A few sentences state mock outcomes as findings: "Even at 40 tasks the simulated MDE ... intervals exclude it", "2† of the ten arms beat the seed" and "largest delta is +6.7† held by SkillOpt†" are marked, but sentence structure treats them as results. When the real numbers arrive, rewrite from the numbers rather than editing the placeholders; the risk of leftover framing (e.g., "Suite B gives the cleaner second test") is high. "Ten optimizer arms" in Sec 5.1 is inconsistent with Table 3 (nine competitors plus two CapEvolve arms, minus failures, so at most eleven). Phrases like "The arithmetic was mostly right", "The optimizer side is worse", and "nobody could tell from the draft" are conversational and, together with the many short declarative sentences, give a slightly staccato register; a PC reading for human authorship will not object to them, but they weaken technical precision in places.

### Minor Issues
- "Shawn, 2026" (PACE) is cited as if surname-first; check the author name format in the bibliography.
- Several cited 2026 arXiv identifiers (e.g. 2610.xxxxx) post-date the "accessed 2026-10-11" date convention. Verify they resolve before submission.
- "Rule P (issues #785/#786)" exposes repository issue numbers in a double-blind paper; also "Anonymous" artifact URLs vs the named repository (SkillBerry AI, github URL) in the reference list and artifact statement break anonymity.
- Section 3.3 has a stray fragment "(gate_footprint: false) and joint coverage (gate_coverage: joint), so the two algorithms share one acceptance rule." attached mid-paragraph after the budget statement; the rendered text appears to have dropped a sentence start.
- Throughput "about 12 rollouts per hour per search" conflicts with decisions.md (about 185 Suite A rollouts per hour in aggregate); state whether it is per search.
- Sec 5.3 says "Table 15 gives the per-arm breakdown", but Table 15 is explicitly provisional with different rollout counts from the placeholder Table 4.
- Define "Tier 1", "cheap", "E1/E2/E3", and "family" at first use in the body; Tier 1 appears in Sec 4.3 before it is defined, and the experiment IDs (E02, E04, E12) appear only in footnotes.
- Dwork et al. and Blum and Hardt should cite their published venues, not only arXiv. Several 2026 arXiv-only references lack venue or version details.
- Table 6: "GPT-5.5 recalculated baseline 77.5 ... reproduces; 110 of 217 recalculated passes need formulas" is hard to parse without the denominator definition.
- Table 12 omits an "all-null at six candidates" row though Fig. 7 and Sec 5.4 use six candidates.
- Fig. 4 and 5 axis labels include a garbled tau-squared glyph ("¿ 2") in the extracted text; check that the PDF font renders tau.
- The abstract's list of "nine other optimizers" includes HarnessLens, which failed on Suite A, and a "community port"; the abstract should say which suites each ran on.
