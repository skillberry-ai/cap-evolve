## Reviewer 2: Mid-career researcher, same subfield (agent-capability / harness / skill optimization)

### Summary
The paper audits an earlier CapEvolve result and finds that much of its SpreadsheetBench gain came from a cached-value scorer, a reused test split and optimizer sessions that read gold. It then proposes a measurement protocol: a recalculating scorer, gold isolation by uid and mount, a two-stage promotion rule P tuned by simulation, matched rollout checkpoints, and a paired family-clustered analysis with Holm correction. It will compare two CapEvolve search algorithms with eight to nine published optimizers and two controls on SpreadsheetBench (Gemma-4-31B) and two tau2-bench domains (gpt-oss-120b), with three search seeds per arm. All results are mock at this stage.

### Strengths
- The scorer audit (Sec. 2.1, App. C) is concrete, reproducible and useful to the field. Recomputing 40 historical claims, reporting that 31 reproduce, 2 are contradicted and 7 have no artifact, and disclosing the omitted 85.6 control all show honest measurement. The recalculation sanity checks (gold-as-output failures, 333 double-scored records, 4 unscorable ids, a stated 98.6% ceiling) are what a practitioner wants to see.
- The paper separates three variance sources (fixed-set execution noise, population-level family-clustered interval, seed spread). It states plainly that three seeds give 2 degrees of freedom and restricts claims to the artifact level. App. B reports an MDE that is derived from observed seed-artifact variance and includes a noise-only floor. Most comparative papers in this area do not do this.
- Operating characteristics of the acceptance rule are reported, including the unflattering ones (power of 0.17 to 0.40 at +0.08, "gives up power by design"). Sec. 3.5 lists what the rule does not control. Bonferroni, SE-helper and double-counting caveats are disclosed rather than buried.
- The exposure ledger and the labels "score-only exposed" and "development-exposed" are the right instinct. The explicit statement that a control winning is the main result is the right framing.
- Infrastructure incidents are disclosed per arm with their timing (App. E, Table 12). Few papers admit that gateway priority acted as a hidden treatment.

### Weaknesses
- **The comparison the title promises is, by the paper's own power analysis, unable to answer its question.** The artifact-level MDE is 0.12 on A, 0.16 on B and 0.22 on C. The optimizer-level MDE is 0.24, 0.31 and 0.41 (App. B). The gaps among published optimizers on these benchmarks are typically a few points; the mock Table 3 shows 1 to 7 points. Suite B is the only unexposed suite, and the paper itself says its 40 tasks cannot resolve small differences. Sec. 3.5 restricts "headline claims" to unexposed sets, so almost nothing can be headline. The expected outcome is "no detectable difference" nearly everywhere. The paper needs to say up front what the contribution is if that happens: the audit and protocol, not the ranking. Right now the title, abstract and Sec. 1 frame it as a head-to-head.
- **The CapEvolve arms are configured by the authors' own simulation to almost never promote.** The simulation output (sim_budget.md, which I checked) gives mean promotions of about 0.09 to 0.10 per search for the chosen rule P setting. Table 10 gives expected final-artifact gain of +0.0011 to +0.0024, i.e. 0.1 to 0.25 points. That is 2 to 4 times lower than the one-stage gate. Under this configuration a typical CapEvolve run returns the seed artifact. The comparison then measures gate strictness against competitors' native, more permissive gates, which Sec. 4.3 admits. The only exploratory arm with the stock gate is HC on Suite C, the weakest suite for inference. A stock-gate or looser-P CapEvolve arm on A (and B) should be a main arm. Otherwise a skeptical PC will read "CapEvolve is no better than the controls" as a consequence of the authors' choice of gate, not a finding.
- **The headline false-promotion claim covers the wrong failure mode.** The simulation (App. B) draws independent per-task realisations on selection tasks and new tasks. In the real loop the proposer reads the validation traces and the same 40 tasks gate acceptance (Dopt = Dsel). Genuine same-task gains that do not transfer pass both stages, and Sec. 3.5 says so. So the abstract's "falsely promotes with probability 0.040 to 0.113" is a trial-noise number that says nothing about the dominant generalization failure of an adaptive loop. The logs should allow a direct empirical measure: per promoted candidate, the validation delta versus the delta on fresh tasks. Report that instead of, or beside, the simulated rate. Also, the headline comparator is k = 1, the loosest gate (Pr(Z > 1) per test is about 0.16). Nobody serious uses it as a guarantee, so quoting 0.52 to 0.58 in the abstract is a strawman. The k = 1.645 numbers appear only in Sec. 5.4.
- **Competitor treatment is not demonstrably symmetric, and some of the asymmetries are in the data you plan to publish.** (a) Before 13:11 on day 1, adapter zeros from infrastructure failures stayed in the competitors' search histories, while CapEvolve dropped such trials. This changed which candidates the competitors accepted, and the planned sensitivity analysis fixes only the reporting, not the search (App. E). (b) SkillOpt shows 80 and 40 "candidate errors" in two seeds that are template placeholder breaks with no target call (Table 12). That is either an integration bug that burns budget or an arm misconfiguration; the paper treats it as a reporting issue. A skeptical reader will conclude SkillOpt or its harness was wrongly run. (c) Suite B agent-optimize can read gold actions for its 74 opt/sel tasks, which gives AO an information advantage over every other Suite B arm. (d) Competitors see 120 tasks (train + val) and CapEvolve sees 40 tasks. (e) Per-arm rollout caps differ natively (GEPA 555, EvoSkill 12 iterations, WikiSkill 8 iterations, "cheap" arms about 200), yet all are "scored at B*". The paper should state, per arm, what the "native deployed artifact at B*" is for each competitor. That is non-trivial for GEPA's Pareto frontier, SkillOpt epochs and EvoSkill's frontier, and I could not tell how it is reconstructed from counters alone.
- **Claims that outrun the evidence.** The abstract says the protocol "removes each problem". Yet (i) the proposer is not sandboxed by the library and isolation is a deployment-level uid/mount arrangement; (ii) the Suite A negative evidence is "absence of evidence" over validation-only rollouts; (iii) the scorer feedback given to every arm carries gold-derived facts (fill counts, error direction); (iv) AO on B can read gold actions; (v) Sec. 3.5 says the harness cannot detect adapter feedback that leaks gold. "Final sets that search never uses" holds for this campaign's search, but Suite C's final is development-exposed and Suite A's is score-only exposed. Sec. 2.1's "14 of 33.0 and 39 of 49.1 points came from a scorer" attributes the whole difference between +33.0 and +18.7 to the scorer, but Table 11's caption says the +33.0 is also "the anti-conservative pooled-observation figure" versus a paired mean. Estimator change and scorer change are confounded in the 14-point and 39-point decomposition. Redo the decomposition holding the estimator fixed.
- **Matched-checkpoint design has gaps.** B* is the minimum over all main seeds of all main arms, so one slow or starved seed (EvoSkill is already 5.5 hours behind) sets everyone's budget. The placeholder values 540, 480 and 420 are not "multiples of 50", contradicting Sec. 4.3. Throughput is shared and contended on one Gemma and one gpt-oss deployment, with gateway priority acting as a treatment, so time-based cutoffs interact with arm launch order. A rollout-count match is fair on target compute but not on optimizer compute or on wall clock (a "12 rollouts per hour" deployment is a real constraint for the systems audience). The cost frontier (Fig. 6) will plot optimizer dollars at Sonnet list price, while 621M of the 648M tokens are RITS target tokens that are excluded. For an MLSys industrial submission the cost axis currently omits the dominant cost.
- **Deployment cost of the artifact is promised but never planned in the results.** Sec. 3.1 says deployment cost per task is "reported separately". Skills and long prompts raise per-task tokens and latency, so a +3 point gain can be a net loss. I see no table, figure or evidence item for it.

### Detailed Comments
**Design and framing.** The most interesting content is Sec. 2 and App. B/C. The comparison in Sec. 4 and 5 is the weaker half and carries the title. I would reorder so the paper is honest about its type: an audit plus a protocol plus an underpowered, descriptive field study. State the pre-registered decision rule for what you will write under each outcome (control wins; all arms null; one arm leads). The EVIDENCE NEEDED notes show these sentences do not yet exist, which is acceptable for this stage, but Sec. 5.1 and 6 should not be written in a way that a mock "SkillOpt leads" reads as a result. Footnote 6 and 15 should ask for the null-everywhere case explicitly.

**Statistics.** The estimand ladder in Sec. 4.4 is good. Some things need pinning down. (1) How are the three seeds combined into a single "artifact" for the task-level paired contrast? If you average over seeds per task, the CI ignores seed variance and the headline contrast is conditional on these three artifacts, which is stated, but then p_Holm in Tables 3 and 4 is conditional too, and the table header should say so. (2) The number of clusters (task families) on each final set is not given, and a t interval with cluster-robust SEs behaves poorly with few clusters; report it and the degrees of freedom used. (3) The "pessimistic missingness bound" (missing cells score 0 for the evaluated arm and 1 for the comparator) is extreme and will be non-binding only if missingness is tiny, which Table 3/4 mocks suggest it is not on Suite C (up to 9/180). Provide a tipping-point analysis instead. (4) The effective strictness of rule P is not what the parameters say: App. B states that the shared SE helper overstates new-task variance by 1.13 to 1.33 for multi-trial estimands, so the "one-sided 90%" zconf = 1.282 is effectively about 1.45 to 1.7 in terms of the true SE. Say so, or fix the helper before final. Sec. 2.3's "12 to 33%" and App. B's 1.13 to 1.33 differ in format and should be reconciled. (5) The Holm families are confusing. The primary Suite A family lists seed, GEPA, SkillOpt, Meta-Harness, WikiSkill and EvoSkill (12 contrasts) and has no one-shot control. Table 3 has a column "Δ vs one-shot" (the "does search pay" test per Sec. 5.1), includes Trace2Skill, ACE and MIPROv2 (cheap arms outside the primary family), and prints a single p_Holm column. A reader cannot tell which family each p belongs to. The CapEvolve-versus-competitor contrasts, which are the research question, appear only in App. J Fig. 4 and are not in any main table. Also, Table 2 lists the one-shot repair only on Suites B and C, while Table 3 has a one-shot row on A (and the Suite A "one-shot" has no configuration in App. A.4). Resolve this.

**Simulation inputs.** The edit-mix (60% null, 25% positive uniform +1 to +6, 15% negative) and the diffuse/sparse split are assumptions; Table 10's Gain column and the "pre-declared criterion" depend on them. Report sensitivity of the chosen (nconf, zconf) to the null share and the positive-effect scale. Sec. 5.4 quotes 0.41 and 0.54 for a ten-point effect, but Table 10 gives 0.55/0.64 (A), 0.48/0.59 (B) and 0.25/0.42 (C) at +0.10. The Sec. 5.4 numbers come from the generic val-40, mean-0.55 simulation (sim_budget.md), not the observed-difficulty runs. Mixing the two in one paper without saying so invites a "which is it" question. Also say in the main text that the screening stage alone costs 40 rollouts per candidate and a confirmation 240 rollouts on A; with a 600-rollout cap rule P can promote at most about two candidates in total, which is the mechanical reason for the near-zero promotion rate.

**Splits and exposure.** The Suite A final set is a 912-release sample ("older 912 text, 3 graded cases per task") that the repository records as score-only exposed to up to two historical runs. Decisions.md says defective/unscorable ids and gold-as-output failures were excluded, which is good, but the paper should say so in Sec. 4.1, since the verified-400 subset exists precisely because many of the 912 tasks are ambiguous. Without that statement a reviewer will assume the final set is noisier than the 400. The paper has the final size as 120 tasks and 2 trials in Sec. 5.1 and Table 1, but the MDE statements (Sec. 4.4, App. B) assume 70 tasks and one trial, and the campaign decision record targeted 60 to 80 tasks. Reconcile; if it is a mock, mark it. The "reuse count with every headline number" is a good idea, but nothing in Sec. 5 shows where it appears.

**Reproducibility.** There is no code or data availability statement. Sections cite repository-internal paths (audit/..., analysis/out/...) and "campaign scratch store" blocker files as if reviewers can open them. For a double-blind industrial submission, say what will be released (scripts, run records, ledger, hashes) and give an anonymous link. App. A.4 gives raw settings dumps, including empty API-key and Azure/Minimax fields that no reader needs and that look like a secrets dump; these tables eat about two pages and the same space would be better used for an actual argument about which settings matter. Also give seeds for the user simulator and for LLM judge sampling, and the GPT-5.5 judge's agreement with the benchmark's default judge (currently only a promised "stored verdict sample" in a footnote). Models like Claude Sonnet 5.5 and GPT-5.5 as optimizer and judge raise a reproducibility caveat for later readers; state model snapshot ids and dates.

**Related work and novelty.** The positioning is honest. The closest prior art on evaluation hygiene (Wang et al. 2026b, Nguyen et al. 2026, Chang & Chen 2026, Singh et al. 2026) is cited but arrives as one paragraph; say what your protocol does that those do not, concretely (the scorer audit and per-arm gate comparison are the two real differences). Include a method that uses a statistical acceptance gate (CAPO, SAGE, PACE are cited as having guarantees that rule P lacks) as an arm, or explain why none runs; they are the natural comparator to a rule that you are tuning by simulation. A reviewer from this subfield will ask why a simple "best-of-N prompt rewrite with held-out selection" or the strongest-known test-time-scaling baseline is not present, given that you cite the papers motivating it. The one-shot control is the nearest, but it is single-step.

**Figures and tables.** Fig. 1 is clear at print size but conveys little beyond the text; a figure showing the splits and which arm reads which split would carry the Dopt/Dsel/Dfin and 40-versus-120 asymmetry that currently sits in prose. Fig. 2 is a good design; the markers use shape and fill but the CapEvolve arms are distinguished from baselines mainly by line thickness and one colour. Check that shape/line style works in grayscale, and add the one-shot interval as a shaded band so "beats the control" can be read off. In Fig. 2 the x ranges differ per panel, which is right but makes cross-suite comparison harder; say so in the caption. Table 3's "Missing" column reports counts over scheduled cells without saying whether the missing cells are the same across arms; the denominators (240, 720) also hint that the seed row has 2 trials while arms have 3 seeds, so the pairing structure should be in the caption. Table 3 sorts baselines by outcome, which is fine for presentation, but CapEvolve rows should be separated from baselines so the reader can tell the primary-family rows from cheap-arm rows. Table 12 has 40+ rows of per-seed counts; collapse to per-arm and keep the CSV. Fig. 3 right panel (stacked 31/2/7) is a bar for a three-number fact; use a sentence.

**Writing.** The body reads dense with parenthetical definitions and many numbers per sentence; the sentence in Sec. 2.2 about the GPT-5.5 session ("byte-identical to the earlier run's first candidate") is striking, but several paragraphs read like a log of the campaign (Sec. 5.6, App. E). Some prose in Sec. 3.4/3.5 repeats the same caveat three times (what is and is not guaranteed). Merging 3.5 into 3.4 and moving Sec. 5.6 entirely to the appendix with a two-sentence pointer recovers most of a page, and the Sec. 3.3 parameter paragraph can lose the library-defaults comparison. Dropping the App. A.4 dumps and trimming Table 12 will cover the remaining budget problem without touching the argument.

### Minor Issues
- Sec. 4.3: "rounded down to a multiple of 50" conflicts with the placeholder B* = 540, 480, 420. Either mark them as mock or fix the rule.
- Abstract: "14 of 33.0 points" reads as garbled; write "about 14 of the 33.0 points".
- Abstract and Table 3 caption count arms differently (nine other optimizers versus ten optimizer arms); define in one sentence that HarnessLens runs only on B.
- Sec. 2.1: "75.4 to 78.6 (+18.7)": say what the 75.4 to 78.6 range is (scorer version? rerun?) and why +18.7 is a point inside it relative to a 58.3 baseline.
- Sec. 2.3: "6 of 16 runs whose champions later gained on the (exposed) test split" is used as evidence that the one-SE gate would have blocked good edits; since that split is exposed, it is not evidence of a lost good edit. Rephrase or drop.
- Sec. 3.1 "never the best-looking candidate on Dsel" and Algorithm 2 line 13 "score a* and a0 once" use a0 for two different things (seed artifact and candidate). Rename the candidate.
- Algorithm 1 uses ∆ with both a per-task and a mean overbar and "SE(∆)" without defining which SE helper (with its double-counting) is used; refer to App. B.
- Sec. 3.3: "one-sided 90% normal quantile" for z = 1.282 is correct, but the SE helper issue makes the label misleading (see Detailed Comments).
- Table 1: Suite A "120†" and "score-only exposed" in the same cell; the dagger should be explained in the caption (final size is mock?).
- Sec. 4.1: footnote 3 hash and "ids whose task family shares a prefix" - say how many families result.
- Sec. 5.3 "$84 at Sonnet list prices": give the optimizer-only versus total split; the main cost number should be per-arm, not campaign total including smoke tests.
- Cost accounting treats RITS tokens as unpriced; give at least a tokens-per-rollout column for the target so a reader can estimate a proxy.
- Table 5: "temperature 0" for the target on a hosted Gemma deployment is not deterministic in practice; the paper later notes 0.088 batch-to-batch drift in the seed mean. State the temperature-0 nondeterminism once in Sec. 4.
- Reference list: check "Shawn, Z." (looks like a given name used as a surname), and a few arXiv ids (2606.28374, 2609.36043, 2610.05671) for correctness. Several citations are 2026 preprints with no venue; say which are peer reviewed.
- Ethical considerations paragraph is one sentence; it can be merged into the limitations or extended to mention the gold-reading behavior of coding-agent optimizers as a misuse/risk pattern.
- Footnote markers (4 to 17) are all author notes and should be removed before the final copy; count the page budget without them.
