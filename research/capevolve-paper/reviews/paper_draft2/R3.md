## Reviewer 3: Early-Career Researcher, Same Subfield (PhD Student)

### Summary
The paper audits the authors' earlier CapEvolve results on SpreadsheetBench, finding that a cached-value scorer, reuse of the test split, gold files visible to the optimizer, and a lax acceptance gate inflated the reported gains. It then defines a measurement protocol (functional scorer, gold isolation, a shared two-stage promotion "rule P", matched rollout checkpoint B*, unseen final sets, paired family-clustered statistics). Under that protocol it compares two CapEvolve search algorithms with nine other optimizers and two controls on three suites. The result tables are mock, so no outcome is claimed yet.

### Strengths
- The audit in Section 2 is concrete and checkable. The headline numbers recompute consistently: 33.0 - 18.7 is about 14 and 49.1 - 9.9 is about 39, matching the abstract. The limitation that the retained rollouts cover validation tasks only is stated plainly (Section 2.2).
- Section 3.5 and Section 8 state what the rule does not control (same-task luck, adaptivity over the validation split, no sandbox for the proposer). This is unusually candid.
- Rule P's operating characteristics are stated with numbers (false promotion 0.040 to 0.113 vs 0.52 to 0.58; power 0.17 to 0.40 at +0.08) rather than described as "robust".
- The paper says in advance that a control may win and that the tau2 suites are descriptive (Section 1, 4.1). The cost, incident and exposure disclosures (Section 5.6, Appendix E, Table 17) are what MLSys reviewers want.
- Figure 3 and the Table 3/4 column design (a delta to the seed and a delta to the one-shot control side by side) answer the right question, namely whether search pays.

### Weaknesses
- **Inconsistent task counts for Suite A.** Table 1, Section 4.1 and Table 3 give 120 final tasks and 80/40 opt/sel. Section 4.4 and Appendix B give the minimum detectable effect (MDE) for "Suite A (70 final tasks, one trial)". Section 5.1 gives "2 trials" per task. Table 3's denominators (240 seed cells, 720 per arm) only fit 120 tasks x 2 trials. A literal reader cannot tell which design the MDE numbers (0.12, 0.24) describe.
- **Three different MDE statements for Suite B.** Section 4.4 and Section 8 give 0.16 (artifact contrast) and 0.31 (optimizer claim). Section 5.2 gives "0.10 to 0.13" for a single comparison at 40 tasks, citing sim_mde.md. Appendix B gives 0.16, 0.12 and 0.11 for 1 to 3 trials. These are never reconciled, so the reader cannot tell which number governs the "no detectable difference" rule.
- **Too many different simulation numbers for rule P.** Section 3.3 and Table 10 use about ten candidates, +0.08 and 0.040 to 0.113. Section 5.4 and Figure 8 use six candidates, a "ten points" effect, 0.050 and 0.41. Section 2.3 and Section 3.5 add a third set. Three setups appear without saying why they differ. "Campaign setting" in Figure 8 is not tied to Table 10.
- **Table 10's "Gain" column undercuts the story.** Expected true gain of the final artifact is +0.0014 to +0.0024 for rule P, versus +0.0034 to +0.0051 for the one-stage gate. That is 0.1 to 0.5 points, and rule P is 2 to 4 times worse. The text says "a trade, not an improvement in final scores", but the 0.15 false-promotion criterion is not justified anywhere. The reader never learns why a 0.2-point expected gain is worth the trade or whether the main comparison can see it. This depends on an assumed 60/25/15 edit mix that is not measured.
- **Notation collisions.** B is the search budget (Eq. 1), B* is the matched checkpoint, and "Suite B" is a suite. "Section 3.3" says "four parameters (k, nconf, δmin, zconf) plus ρ", but the same k appears as k, kse, k_se and "k = 1.645" in different places. D_sel, "val", "validation split", "screen set" and "optimization/selection" are used for overlapping things (Section 3.1 vs 4.1 vs Appendix B, where Suite B "screens" on 49 optimization tasks).
- **Terms are used before they are defined.** In the abstract and intro these appear without definition: "functional scorer", "gold isolation by process and mount", "promotion rule", "matched search checkpoints", "family-clustered". Section 3.2 and 3.3 use "digest", "footprint", "judge", "evidence ledger", "reward-gated judge" and "joint coverage" with only a pointer to Appendix H or I. Algorithm 1 uses cov, ρ and the offset o with no description in the body, and "Eval(a, D, n; o)" is never defined.
- **Mock placeholders in prose and structure.** Section 5 is built from dagger numbers and EVIDENCE NEEDED footnotes (about 15). I take these as deliberate. However, Sections 5.1 to 5.3 are structured as "largest delta held by X", which pre-commits the prose to a leaderboard framing that the paper says it will not use (Section 4.4 says only claims that clear Holm and the pessimistic missingness bound count).
- **Figures are hard to read at print size.** See "Detailed Comments" on Figures 2, 4, 6, 7 and 8.

### Detailed Comments
**Clarity and flow.** The introduction packs a lot into the first page. The 33.0/18.7 example appears in the abstract, the intro and Section 2.1, with slightly different phrasing each time ("14 of 33.0", "roughly 14 and 39"). The five-part protocol paragraph and the three contributions overlap heavily. Contribution 3 ends with "Section 6 discusses... Section 7 places them", which is a roadmap and not a contribution. I would merge the protocol paragraph into contribution 2 and drop the roadmap. The sentence "If a control or a simpler method wins, that is the main result" is good, but Section 5.1 then reads like a leaderboard.

**Section 2.** "20 SpreadsheetBench runs, none expired" is cryptic (expired what?). "verified-400 gold" is used before the 400-task subset is introduced (Section 4.1). "As-saved" and "recalculated" scorers are defined only in running text, and a small table of the two scorers and what each reads would help. In 2.2, the statement "A one-iteration run from an empty seed reached 85.6 where an eight-round run reached 76.4" is very strong, but it is unclear which scorer and which split these numbers are on. It also conflicts with "Gemma moved from 43.5 to 76.5". In 2.3 the sentence "Every recorded-versus-unmasked mismatch... was a dropped task, not footprint masking, so the footprint concern rests on code and simulation" is hard to parse. It seems to say the historical replay cannot support the footprint claim; state that directly.

**Section 3.** Section 3.1 is formal but the notation is not carried through. The text says "the shipped implementation uses the validation split for both roles... Dopt = Dsel = val" and then says competitors read train and select on val. This is the key confound (40 vs 120 tasks of feedback), and it is buried mid-paragraph in 3.1 and again in 4.1 and Section 8. Put it in the Table 2 notes or a one-line "data exposure" column. Eq. (1) is introduced and then not used. Algorithm 1 mixes "m" for per-task mean vectors with "Δ" for deltas and uses an overbar for the mean; the pdf rendering makes ∆̄ and ∆ hard to tell apart. The prose says rule P has "four parameters plus a coverage floor", while the algorithm's Require line lists five. Please state the stage-1 and stage-2 logic in a sentence ("screen at k = 1, then confirm on fresh trials with a one-sided z test") before the algorithm, since the algorithm is the first place a reader can see it. The footnote-sized statement "z is taken as infinite when SE is zero" belongs in the algorithm or the appendix, not the middle of a paragraph. The "Experimental configuration" paragraph is a configuration list and not a method. It should be in Section 4. Also "at most 4M non-cache-read optimizer tokens" is introduced as an unexplained quantity here and defined only in Section 5.3.

**Section 4.** The matched-checkpoint rule (B* from counters, rounded down to a multiple of 50, floored at 200) is clear. The sentence "Each arm is scored on what its own selection rule would have deployed at B*" needs an example, since for GEPA, SkillOpt and EvoSkill this means different things. The statistics paragraph (4.4) is dense: "score on the fixed final set", "population-level contrast", "pipeline-level interval", "artifact level" and "pessimistic missingness bound" are all used with no definition of what the "population" is (the 239-id pool? the 912 release?). A short table of the three quantities, their unit and their interval would make this readable. "Missing" in Tables 3 and 4 is introduced only in the caption and in 4.4 as "missing cells score 0 for the evaluated arm and 1 for the comparator", so a reader cannot say whether the CIs and p values in Tables 3 and 4 include that bound. Table 2's "Notes" column mixes algorithmic descriptions with budget notes ("cheap, about 200 rollouts") and the "Suites" column uses "ABC"; "Unoptimized seed" and "One-shot repair" are labelled controls in the text but appear as arms.

**Section 5.** Footnote numbering restarts in the body and makes the page feel like a draft; fine for now, but make sure every EVIDENCE NEEDED footnote is replaced by prose and not left as a gap. Section 5.1 says "2 of the ten optimizer arms beat the seed" while Table 2 lists eleven non-control rows (ten search arms plus HarnessLens, which is Suite B only). Count and name them. The sentence "Figure 6 plots delta against cost and Figure 5 plots score against rollouts, both in Appendix J" lists the figures in reverse order. Appendix J has figures numbered 4 through 9 but the text references Figures 8 then 3. Order figures by first reference. Section 5.5 repeats Section 2 almost verbatim; consider cutting one. Section 5.4 repeats Section 3.3 numbers.

**Tables.** Table 3: the "∆ vs one-shot" column for the seed row is "−1.9", which reads as a result and not as a reference. The pHolm column is not monotone in the mock data (0.724 for +3.3 vs 0.738 for +3.4) which is fine for a mock, but the design should state the Holm family size (12) in the caption, since the footnote-less reader will not know it. The suffix "(community)" for WikiSkill appears in the table, "WikiSkill-comm." in the figures and "community port" in text; use one label. The cost column "USD" mixes "lower bound" Claude list prices with unpriced tokens; add a column for tokens or mark the arms with unpriced components. Table 4 reports "Missing" up to 9/180 on Suite C for a mock, but the real counts in Table 12 look larger; make sure the design can show how missing cells change a delta. In Table 1, the Final column mixes a number and a status ("120† score-only exposed"), and the Opt/sel column has no unit; "exposed" and "sealed" should be a separate column. The dagger on 120 shows a plan number is placeholder, which conflicts with Section 4.1's statement that the final set is fixed and hashed before scoring.

**Figures.** Figure 1 is a pipeline diagram. It is generic and could carry one number per stage (e.g. "3 seeds", "70/120 tasks"). Figure 2 has the correct design but is crowded: each arm has a different marker shape and a different colour, the filled/hollow distinction that carries the Holm information is subtle, panels (b) and (c) use different x-axis scales (so it is hard to compare the width of intervals), and the panel titles render the tau as "¿" (a font glyph failure). Row order differs from Table 3, so rows cannot be matched across the two. Colour alone (orange, teal, magenta) separates arms; a colour-blind reader needs the shape to carry identity and the legend is only the y-axis labels, which works. Figure 4 repeats the same information as Figure 2 with a second delta, and its panel (b) and (c) rows are different from (a), which the caption notes only briefly. Figure 6 has a log x-axis with only a "10.0" tick, so the axis cannot be read. Figure 7 has very large fonts compared to every other figure and has no scale hint for n (the "n=31" labels are tasks or families?). Figure 8's legend mentions "Rule P, stricter" and "Rule P, z = 1.645" which are not defined in the caption or in Section 5.4; the left panel title says "searches with a false promotion" and the right "candidates promoted", with different y-axes, and the lines in the right panel for rule P and one-stage k = 1.645 are nearly indistinguishable. The caption of Figure 8 is a source file path; give the settings. Figure 3's right panel is a three-bar chart of a count (31, 2, 7) that would be better as a sentence, and "Reproduced / Contradicted / No artifact" are defined only in Section 5.5.

**Reproducibility and wording.** The paper relies on file names in text (audit/historical_results.md, analysis/out/sim_mde.md, bypassed_usage.csv, error_label_parity.csv). This is good for the artifact but double-blind review cannot follow them; say where an anonymized artifact lives. "Official code" is claimed for eight methods, but the Table 6 check column mixes "92 of 92 blobs", "clone" and "merged PRs", so the notion of "verified official code" is inconsistent across arms. The prose has some phrases that read like a lab notebook ("hidden treatment", "a stale low-priority tag", "13:05 to 18:40 UTC on the first day"); these belong in Appendix E with the body keeping a one-sentence summary. The Ethical Considerations section is one sentence about deployment of optimized artifacts; it says nothing on the two things that matter more here, gold files and sessions that read test results, and the use of paid third-party model APIs.

**Page budget.** The body (through the Conclusion) runs to the bottom of page 10 and the final text will replace about fifteen footnote placeholders and several paragraphs. Candidates to cut: the second telling of the audit (Section 5.5), the numbers repeated between Section 3.3 and 5.4, Section 3.4's tamper-detection detail and the experimental-configuration paragraph (which duplicates Appendix A.2).

### Minor Issues
- "Shawn, 2026" is cited for PACE, but the reference reads "Shawn, Z." Check the surname/given name order. "Anthropic" in the introduction has no year in the in-text cite.
- Citations: "Zhang et al., 2026a/2026b" and "Wang et al., 2026a/2026b" are fine in the list but the DGM paper cited as ICLR 2026a with an arXiv id of 2505.xxxxx is odd; verify.
- "tau2" is written "τ 2 -bench", "tau2", "τ 2" in different places (the PDF shows spacing like "τ 2 -bench"). Use τ²-bench consistently.
- Figure panel titles show "¿ 2" in Figure 2 and in Figure 4, 6 and 7 (font fallback).
- The abstract ends mid-sentence with the placeholder; the abstract also has several numbers (0.040 to 0.113, 0.52 to 0.58) that could be left to the body.
- "hill-climb" / "HC", "agent-optimize" / "AO", "CapEvolve HC" are all used; define the abbreviations once.
- Algorithm 2 is in Appendix I and referenced in Section 3.2 as the "shared skeleton" but is never described in the body.
- "Table 14 (Appendix I)" and "Algorithm 2 (Appendix I)" are referenced from 3.2 as if the reader will go to the appendix; summarize the differences in two sentences.
- Section 2.1: "29 to 30 of 30" is awkward; say "29 or 30 of 30".
- Section 3.4: "(iii) Tamper detection" lists only that it is optional and after the fact; the scope sentence at the end ("Detection is host-independent but...") needs a subject.
- Footnote 3 gives a truncated sha256 ("10ef893d...") with no way to verify; give the full hash in the appendix.
- Table 3: "Missing is missing over scheduled cells" is circular; say "Missing: rollouts that did not return a score / scheduled rollouts".
- Section 4.2: "VERSE, CoEvoSkills, and HarnessLens on Suite A failed to reproduce" is the first mention of these three names; add citations or a pointer.
- The line numbers in the margin are fine for review but the footnote text in Section 5 overlaps the column on page 8/9 in places; check the final layout.
