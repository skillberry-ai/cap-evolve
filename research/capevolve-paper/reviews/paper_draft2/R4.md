## Reviewer 4: Adversarial Methodologist and Statistician

### Summary
The paper audits an earlier CapEvolve draft (a literal-versus-formula scorer, reused and gold-exposed splits, a lenient one-SE gate) and proposes a protocol to fix those problems. The protocol has a recalculating scorer, gold isolation, one shared promotion rule P, a matched rollout checkpoint B*, and paired family-clustered statistics with Holm correction. It then compares two CapEvolve algorithms with eight to nine published optimizers and two controls on SpreadsheetBench (Suite A) and two descriptive tau2-bench domains (Suites B and C), using three search seeds per arm. All outcome numbers are mock. The methodological contribution is real, but the design as written cannot answer its own question ("which optimizer finds the best artifact"), and several sentences claim more than the design supports.

### Strengths
- Reporting the failure of the authors' own earlier numbers (Sec. 2, Fig. 4, Table 18) is unusually candid and is the strongest part of the paper. The pairing of both scorers on the same output files is the right way to isolate the artifact.
- The paper states operating characteristics for its own gate, including that rule P is "underpowered by design" (Table 16, Sec. 3.3), and it computes MDEs from observed seed-artifact variance, with noise-only floors, rather than from assumed effect sizes (App. B).
- The matched-checkpoint idea (B* from rollout counters only, artifact reconstructed from information inside the first B* rollouts) is a sensible alternative to nominal budgets. It is fixed without looking at scores.
- The paper discloses the incidents that hurt it (EvoSkill starvation, 502 asymmetry, Suite B agent-optimize reading gold for its own selection tasks, error-label parity in Table 17). The error-label parity table is a good template for how to report retry asymmetry.
- Pre-specified controls (seed, one-shot) and the statement that a control winning "is the main result" are the right framing.

### Weaknesses
- **The central question is unanswerable at S=3.** Sec. 1 asks "which optimizer finds the best artifact", yet the pipeline-level MDE is 0.24 on A, 0.31 on B and 0.41 on C (Sec. 4.3, App. B), with two degrees of freedom. The conditional artifact-level MDE (0.12 on A) is also larger than essentially every effect the mock tables show (1 to 7 points). Whatever real numbers arrive, the design can only report "no detectable difference" for nearly every arm pair. The paper never says what outcome it could have. The abstract, introduction and conclusion read as if a ranking will be delivered.
- **The unit of inference and the claim do not match.** Cluster-robust task-level CIs (Tables 4 and 5) treat the three seed artifacts of an arm as fixed. Search-seed variance enters nowhere in the interval, though it is the dominant noise for the "optimizer" claim. The per-seed values (Table 15) are shown beside the means, but nothing ties the headline interval to them. Mean-of-three-artifacts with a task-clustered CI is an artifact-level, conditional-on-these-seeds statement. The Discussion and Conclusion sentences will inevitably be phrased as statements about methods.
- **Winners are decided by non-significance, with no equivalence or non-inferiority margin.** "No detectable difference" at MDE 0.12 to 0.41 will be read as "simple methods match complex ones". The `\needs` notes ("search adds nothing measurable", "a control is competitive") invite exactly that inference. Without a pre-declared margin, a null is also compatible with a large effect.
- **CapEvolve is configured to be nearly inert, and the only bound on that is underpowered.**
  - Table 16 gives rule P an expected true new-task gain of +0.0014, +0.0011 and +0.0024 per search (about 0.1 to 0.2 points). That is 3x to 4x below the one-stage gate, and the simulation says the arm should be indistinguishable from the seed.
  - Competitors keep native, looser gates. The paper then compares "a method together with its own acceptance rule", but one side's rule was deliberately chosen to promote rarely.
  - The decisions log shows rule P was re-tuned once, from (n_conf=10, z=2, dmin=0.02) to (3, 1.282, 0), because the first setting had power 0.15 at +0.08. That is a forking path for the very arm under test.
  - The ablation that would show how much the rule matters (stock gate) runs only on Suite C: 20 development-exposed test tasks, 10 selection tasks, MDE 0.22. It "bounds" nothing (Sec. 4.3).
- **Simulation fidelity is asserted, not validated.**
  - The all-null false-promotion rates (0.040 to 0.113) rest on a stress edit mix and independent per-task realisations on selection and new tasks. This removes proposer targeting, i.e. edits written after reading the failing validation traces, which the paper itself says rule P does not control (Sec. 3.4). Edits targeted at failing selection tasks have positive selection-set effect and zero population effect, which this null never generates.
  - The abstract states 0.040 to 0.113 as a property of the rule. It is a property of the simulator.
  - Sec. 5.4 uses six candidates (0.050 vs 0.434) and a +10-point power of 0.41 vs 0.54. Table 16 uses about ten candidates, and its +0.10 column reads 0.55/0.48/0.25 vs 0.64/0.59/0.42. I could not reconcile the numbers.
  - The simulated screen set for Suite B is the 49-task optimization split (App. B), while Table 1 lists 49/25 for optimization/selection. Which tasks gate CapEvolve on B is ambiguous.
  - A cheap calibration is available once results exist. Compare accepted-candidate counts and observed screen-to-confirmation shrinkage against the simulator, and say in advance what would falsify it. The `\needs` for accepted-candidate counts is the right place.
- **Data exposure differs by 3x between arms, and the authors control it.**
  - Stock CapEvolve reads 40 validation tasks and gates on the same 40 (Sec. 4.1). Competitors read 80 train tasks and select on 40 validation tasks. The authors call this a disadvantage for CapEvolve, but that is asserted, not shown.
  - Using the same split for feedback and gating is also the textbook adaptive-overfit setup, and it is not clear which way the net effect goes.
  - The decisions log also records that the stock arm scores the seed on train-80 at baseline, consuming 80 of the 600 rollouts. The paper says the train split is unused. This needs reconciling.
  - One config change (train-80 for feedback) would remove the confound for at least a sub-arm.
- **Competitors are not demonstrably "run as their authors intended".**
  - The text relies on "official code" and discloses only ACE's field rename.
  - The decisions log records that SkillOpt's native gold-to-agent and gold-vs-prediction reports were removed, that EvoSkill's "task interface [was] replaced", and that Meta-Harness was changed at driver level. The paper does not tabulate these.
  - Trace2Skill, ACE and MIPROv2 are "cheap" arms with native budgets (Trace2Skill "about 200 rollouts"), so they are not at B*. It is unclear how the matched-checkpoint comparison applies to them. Table 13's mock shows about 530 rollouts for the same arms.
  - Pinned commits are missing for five arms (the `\needs` in Table 9).
  - Hyperparameters beyond "run record" dumps are not justified. For example, GEPA runs with max-metric-calls=555 and MIPROv2 has no recorded settings at all.
  - Editable surfaces differ and are not tabulated: CapEvolve on A edits `system-prompt` only, on B and C `system-prompt,tools`; competitors produce skills, harness code or prompts. The thesis that the editable surface is a parameter of one loop is never varied in the experiments.
- **The one-shot control is CapEvolve-flavoured and weak.** It is `hill-climb` with `max_iterations=1`, i.e. the CapEvolve proposer prompt. It is the cheapest thing to beat. The paper cites evidence that single-lineage optimization, one analysis pass and test-time scaling rival complex optimizers (Sec. 7). The matching strong controls are a budget-matched best-of-k independent one-shots chosen on validation, and a naive single-lineage loop at B*. Neither is an arm.
- **Operational confounds are disclosed but not neutralised, and Appendix E is not fully consistent with the decisions log.**
  - Pre-13:11 gateway 502s scored zero for competitors (1 to 2.5% of calls, about 4 hours of each search) and were dropped for CapEvolve. Those zeros shaped competitors' search-time selection, not just the final report. The "treat as missing" sensitivity only addresses reporting. The paper cannot say how many promotion or selection decisions flipped.
  - The Appendix D "hang rule" is described as shared. The decisions log (13:45) records that on Suite B, GEPA and controls retry infrastructure errors up to three times inside the budget, CapEvolve leaves missing cells with no retry, and the watchdog is 2 h rather than 3 h.
  - Gateway priority was a hidden treatment (EvoSkill, plus HarnessLens smokes). Priority is audited only for the arm that was caught. A per-arm queue-time table is the minimal control.
  - Restarts and discarded runs (Suite B v1, AO engine correction, HC pilot, Meta-Harness restarted three times, Suite B isolation relaunch losing about 49 rollouts per run) are decided by humans after seeing partial runs. A full list of discarded runs per arm with a rule that restarts depend only on infrastructure causes is needed.
  - Provisional metering (Table 17) shows GEPA at 635 rollouts against a 600 hard cap and Meta-Harness at 599. "Rollouts from counters only" needs a definition of which counter (native vs gateway).
- **Leakage and final-set provenance claims are stronger than the evidence.**
  - The abstract and Sec. 1 say "final sets that search never uses" and that the protocol "removes each problem". Section 3.4 and Table 1 then concede that Suite A's final set is score-only exposed, Suite C is development-exposed, an unsandboxed proposer is a deployment requirement, tamper detection is after the fact, and agent-optimize on Suite B reads gold for its own selection tasks.
  - Sec. 3.4 says "headline claims are restricted to sets that were not [exposed]", but Suite A is the primary suite and is exposed. Sec. 5.6 says "Suites A and B use fresh final sets". These conflict.
  - Gold-free sandbox is evidenced by absence in transcript scans (the same weak evidence the paper criticises in Sec. 2.2). "Negative test for every agentic proposer" is not documented per arm. Non-agentic competitors (GEPA, MIPROv2, ACE) run in-process with unknown file access.
  - Scorer feedback "carries gold-derived facts such as fill counts and error direction" for every arm. This is a channel through which gold content can be learned.
  - Public-benchmark pretraining contamination of Gemma, Claude and gpt-oss is not discussed. SpreadsheetBench-912 and tau2 retail are public.
- **Evaluation noise appears to be large and is under-addressed.**
  - The seed-artifact mean "moved by 0.088 across six evaluation batches" (Sec. 4.3). The decisions log shows the same artifact scoring 0.425 to 0.650 on val-40 across six concurrent evaluations. If that is the real drift, it is larger than every plausible effect, and it is the signal gating and selection run on.
  - The decisions log also records 27% of seed rollouts ending in an unparsed tool-call format failure. A one-line fix to that gives a large gain to any arm that finds it, so Suite A deltas may mostly measure who discovers that single defect. The paper should say so and report the rate.
- **Multiplicity is under-specified and the tables do not match the stated families.**
  - The primary Holm family has 12/8/6 contrasts per suite and excludes baselines vs the seed. Table 4's single `p_Holm` column mixes CapEvolve rows (primary family) with baseline rows (secondary family) and is unlabelled.
  - The question "does CapEvolve beat X" is in the appendix (Fig. 5). Cheap arms (the mock's top arms) fall into secondary families.
  - There is no correction across three suites, and the secondary "native end-of-run" family doubles the number of analyses.
  - The win criterion (Holm p<0.05 and a CI excluding zero under the pessimistic missingness bound) is defined for a single evaluated arm vs comparator. It is not defined for CapEvolve vs a baseline.
- **Cost accounting favours agentic arms.** The 4M cap and "USD" count non-cache-read tokens only. Claude Code arms (EvoSkill, CapEvolve) rely on cache reads of tens to over 100M tokens (Table 13). Target-model tokens and RITS cost are never priced, so the Pareto plot (Fig. 7) shows optimizer list-price dollars only. A cost frontier on this axis will mislead.

### Detailed Comments
**Design vs claim.** The paper's strength is its measurement posture; its weakness is that the measurement cannot support the comparative claim in the title and abstract. With S=3 and 40 to 120 final tasks, the honest deliverable is (a) a table of artifact-level paired deltas with per-seed values, (b) a statement of which contrasts are resolvable, and (c) an explicit "we cannot rank X and Y". I would reframe the abstract and conclusion around the first two and remove the framing as an optimizer ranking. If any ranking claim survives, report seed-level variability in the interval (a seed-level bootstrap or a mixed model with seed and task random effects, df stated). If a null is reported as "control is competitive", pre-declare a margin and use an equivalence test or at least report which differences are excluded (the CI bound against the margin). Also state the probability, under your own assumptions, that this design yields a significant CapEvolve-vs-best-baseline contrast, so readers know the prior.

**Mock-table design (Tables 4, 5, 14, 15).** The main table must contain the contrast the paper is about. Add CapEvolve vs best baseline and vs the matched strongest control rows, or a column for it, with the Holm family labelled per row. The mock rows also expose design problems. (i) CI half-widths of about 5 points sit beside a stated artifact-level MDE of 12 points (0.12) on A. Either the MDE or the interval method will not survive real data, and the final table should show both on the same scale. (ii) The MDE is computed for 70 final tasks and one trial. Table 1 lists the final set as mock 120 tasks with two trials. Fix the plan and let the MDE follow. (iii) In Table 15, several "Mean Δ" cells are not the mean of the three per-seed values shown (EvoSkill, WikiSkill), and some missingness bounds do not bracket the mean. If this stems from the mock generator, fix it so the real table's arithmetic is checkable. If the mean is taken at the task level with unequal denominators, say so. (iv) The mock B* values (540/480/420) and the per-arm "Rollouts at B*" (528 to 538) contradict the stated rule (multiple of 50, same for all arms). A real table should show a single B* per suite and any arm below it flagged. (v) The "Missing" column denominators (240, 720, 360, 180) are not explained by the visible design (trials × tasks × seeds); define them. The "USD" column should say whether it is per search or summed over seeds.

**Matched checkpoint.** B* depends on the slowest main seed. Arms with slow proposers (agentic arms, EvoSkill after restart) therefore set the budget for everyone, and the floor of 200 may leave only a very small number of promotion steps for CapEvolve (confirmation costs 240 rollouts on A for 40 tasks × 3 trials × 2 sides; Suite B's 49 tasks need about 294 per confirmation). Report, per arm, the number of candidates screened and promoted by B*; if CapEvolve promotes zero times by B* then its B* artifact is the seed and the comparison is vacuous for that arm. For arms with batch or epoch schedules (SkillOpt, GEPA's full-val evaluations) the "artifact at B*" can be ill-defined; the decisions log contains per-method rules that should be in the paper, with the straddling-candidate exclusion rule.

**Leakage forking paths.** Document the final-set freeze timeline (sampling seed, hash, B* computation, artifact reconstruction script) with commit hashes and times, and state that the set of arms and contrasts is closed. The secondary "native end-of-run" scoring and the transfer-table scoring with other targets (decisions log) touch the same final set and should be labelled exploratory, with the reuse count promised in Sec. 3.5 reported. Report the reuse ledger count in the paper once it exists. Also note the Suite A final tasks come from the older 912-question text, so results are not on the verified-400 distribution that the audit uses; say whether the scorer ceiling (98.6%) applies.

**Judge and tau2 adaptation.** For B and C the target and user simulator are the same gpt-oss-120b with provider-default reasoning effort at temperature 0. Max steps is 100, not 200. The judge is GPT-5.5. Optimized artifacts can overfit the simulator. The "judge sensitivity" is pending; if it uses a stored verdict sample, say who sampled it and whether arm identity was hidden. With 20 to 40 tasks a single flipped assertion is 2.5 to 5 points, which is the effect scale of the mock data. I would shrink the claim that these suites show "the protocol transfers" to "the pipeline runs".

**Sentences that will read as findings once placeholders are filled.** "On Suite A, N of the ten optimizer arms beat the seed" (the count is not a measure of method quality when each arm's CI is 10 points wide). "Suite B gives the cleaner second test, because its test tasks are sealed" (a public benchmark; off-repository use is "invisible to us" per Limitations). "Whichever arm leads, the isolation tests and matched checkpoint let others check a gain" (isolation tests are not shown to be independent of the code under test). "Gateway priority and retry choices also changed outcomes for individual arms" (written as a finding; no evidence yet). "This disadvantages CapEvolve" (Sec. 4.1, unsupported). "Rule P cuts false promotion" in a section title (Sec. 5.4) is a simulation result, not an experimental one; the text correctly says "in simulation", but the heading and Fig. 8 caption should say so too.

**Audit section.** The headline "14 of 33.0 points" is the difference of two deltas computed on different baselines (43.5 vs 58.3), so the attribution to a scorer is accounting, not decomposition. Say so. "A widely used optimizer's own history" is the authors' own prior draft: "widely used" is unsupported, and in a double-blind setting this will look like an arm's-length third party. The 85.6 vs 76.4 comparison (Sec. 2.2 and 5.6) is from the exposed test-280 with single runs, which Sec. 2.2 says "rank nothing", then Sec. 5.6 says it matters for the comparison with simpler methods. Pick one stance.

**Page budget (relevant to validity).** Sec. 3.1 to 3.2 spend space on footprint masks, Beta-posterior and reward-gated judges that the experiments switch off. App. F (merge verification, screen accounting) is default-off and unused. Move these out, and spend the space on the arm-deviation table, a per-arm accepted-candidate table, the reuse ledger, and a statement of what each stage of the pipeline could and could not detect.

### Minor Issues
- Abstract: "nine other optimizers" while HarnessLens runs only on B (and appears in Suite C rows of Tables 5, 13 and 14 although Table 2 says B only) and each competitor except GEPA runs on one suite only. State this in the abstract. Table 4 also contains the one-shot control on Suite A, but Table 2 lists it for B and C only.
- Abstract and Sec. 1 call the controls "unoptimized and one-shot", while Table 2 says Suite A has no one-shot arm.
- Sec. 5.2 gives "0.10 to 0.13" for a 40-task MDE; Sec. 4.3, App. B and Limitations give 0.16/0.12/0.11 for 1/2/3 trials. Use one set of numbers.
- Suite C: 10 selection tasks with 3 confirmation trials per task is too small for rule P's SE to be meaningful (the sim gives 0.17 power at +0.08). Say what a promotion can mean there.
- Table 1 "Opt / sel" for CapEvolve vs competitors is unclear (CapEvolve uses validation for both).
- Table 17 and the surrounding text mix "candidate error" and "infrastructure" labelling without saying how many SkillOpt zeros come from the 120 template-contract failures, which look like an adapter-side defect rather than a candidate fault.
- The sequence "Table 3. Table 16. Table 4." on one page is out of order, and several appendix tables are cited out of order (the `\label` usage is fine; check numbering).
- Fig. 2: marker shape and hue carry arm identity for 11 rows (tough in greyscale); row order differs between panels (A is not sorted by delta, B and C use different orders); x-axes differ across panels (to about 10 vs 20), so effect sizes cannot be compared by eye. A shared axis, or at least the same row order, would help. The PLACEHOLDER watermark overlaps labels; fine for a draft.
- Fig. 4 (audit) and Fig. 8 (rule P) captions should state the simulation or mock basis in the caption text itself.
- Fig. 9 (roles) and Table 17's caption are very long; move the metering-audit prose to the text.
- Define "family" for tau2 tasks (clusters are singletons), and the degrees of freedom of the t interval for Suite A (number of families).
- "Search cutoff" is listed as 2026-10-13 06:00 UTC but incident times are given only on 2026-10-11; state the timeline for all three days.
- Table 9: "92 of 92 blobs" style checks are only reported for two arms; EvoSkill, Trace2Skill, ACE, MIPROv2, HarnessLens say "not recorded".
- The footnote `\needs` markers are numerous in the body; make sure each is resolved or removed in the camera-ready build.
