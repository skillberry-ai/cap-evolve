## Reviewer 5: Writing Editor (applies the authors' own writing guide and anti-slop rules)

### Summary
The paper argues that reported gains from agent-capability optimizers are unreliable. It audits the authors' earlier CapEvolve SpreadsheetBench results, finds that a cached-value scorer, reused splits and gold visibility inflated them, and builds a protocol (functional scorer, gold isolation, a two-stage promotion rule P, a matched rollout checkpoint B*, final sets that search never uses). Under that protocol it compares two CapEvolve search algorithms with eight other optimizers and a community WikiSkill port, plus seed and one-shot controls, on SpreadsheetBench and two tau2-bench domains. All outcome numbers are mock, so the review below concerns prose, story, tables and figures.

### Strengths
- The surface prose is cleaner than most drafts. I found no em or en dashes used as punctuation, no "delve/leverage/seamless/crucial/pivotal/landscape/underscore/showcase/unlock", no "notably/furthermore/moreover" chains, no rhetorical questions and no "not only X but also Y". Short sentences land well ("The headline is not." "Rule P gives up power by design." "We call it score-only exposed, not fresh.").
- Several headings and captions already state the finding (Sec. 2 title, "Rule P cuts false promotion and costs power", Fig. 3, Fig. 8, Fig. 9 captions lead with the claim), which is what the guide asks for.
- The text is unusually honest about what it cannot show ("an absence of evidence", "rule P gives up power by design", "descriptive", the Limitations bullets). This reads as human and credible.
- Fig. 3 (left) and Fig. 8 (left) pop out: one message, values printed on the bars, a colorblind-safe palette.
- Every mock number is flagged, and the \needs notes mark where prose is missing, so the remaining writing work is visible.

### Weaknesses
- **The story has no single crux and the title does not match it.** The title names CapEvolve, but the three contributions are an audit, a protocol, and a comparison. CapEvolve-as-method has no contribution of its own, and Sec. 3 spends most of its length on rule P and the contract. The skim path through the first sentences of paragraphs (Sec. 1, 2, 3.3, 4, 5) gives: gains can be wrong; here is an audit; here is a rule; here are tables of placeholders. The reader cannot tell whether the paper is about the audit, the rule, or the league table. The guide's "what, how, why, then the crux" is not met until the final results exist, and the abstract and conclusion currently have no result sentence at all.
- **The same facts are told four to six times.** The 33.0 to 18.7 and 49.1 to 9.9 gains appear in the abstract, Sec. 1, Sec. 2.1, Sec. 5.5, Fig. 3, Table 11 and Table 18. The 85.6 vs 76.4 control appears in Sec. 2.2, Sec. 5.5 and Table 18. The rule P operating characteristics (0.040 to 0.113; 0.33 at +0.08) appear in the abstract, Sec. 1, Sec. 3.3, Sec. 3.5, Sec. 5.4, Sec. 6, Table 10 and Fig. 8. The 600-rollout, 4M-token budget appears in Sec. 3.3, Sec. 4.3 and Appendix A. The guide says a topic should be presented once. Sec. 5.5 and most of Sec. 6 add nothing new and are the first things to cut for the page budget.
- **Several sentences over-claim or contradict another section.**
  - Sec. 5.5 title and Fig. 3 caption say recalculation removes "most" of the gain. For Gemma it removes 14.3 of 33.0 points (43%); only GPT-5.5 (39 of 49) supports "most".
  - Sec. 5.5 says Suites A and B "use fresh final sets", Fig. 1 labels the final set "fresh", and Sec. 7 says "fresh final sets", but Sec. 4.1 says the Suite A set is "score-only exposed, not fresh".
  - Sec. 1 says gold isolation is "checked by a negative test for every agentic proposer", yet Sec. 5.6 and Limitations say the Suite B agent-optimize arm "can read gold actions" for 74 tasks, and Appendix E says it was moved into a shim at 15:00. The reader cannot tell which is true now.
  - The abstract and Sec. 1 say the paper compares CapEvolve with "nine other optimizers". Table 2 shows eight on Suite A, two on B and one on C.
  - Sec. 6 says gateway priority and retries "changed outcomes for individual arms" before any result exists to show it.
  - The abstract calls the audited optimizer "widely used", with no citation, while Sec. 1 reveals it is the authors' own earlier draft. The abstract should say "our own".
- **Terminology is overloaded and several terms are never defined in the body.**
  - "Seed" means the initial artifact ("seed artifact", the Table 3 row "Seed (no search)"), the search seed ("three seeds"), the rollout seed, the split seed, and the sampling seed. "Baseline" means both competitors (Table 3 caption, Sec. 5.1) and the seed artifact. "Arm", "method", "optimizer" and "algorithm" are interchangeable.
  - "HC" and "AO" appear in every table and figure but are never introduced. "Digest/act engine", "probe", "ledger", "footprint", "pregate", "cost gating", "smart merge", "failure clustering" and "context digest" appear in Sec. 3.3 as unexplained switch names. "Measurement-first" (title) is never defined in the text. "E10", "E12" and `analysis/out/...` paths surface in the body.
  - "B*" is used in Fig. 1 (p. 5) and defined only in Sec. 4.3.
- **Figures carry too little or too much, and several break the guide's Graphics checklist.** Details are under Detailed Comments. The main ones: Fig. 1 uses tiny text and says little the introduction does not; Fig. 5 is a ten-line spaghetti plot with unlabeled y-semantics; Fig. 7 uses a different font size and unexplained F1 to F6 labels; Fig. 9 has a legend over the data and a symlog stack that hides segments; Fig. 6 clips its x-axis label.
- **The page budget is spent on darlings.** The body just fits 10 pages now, but about 15 \needs footnotes will be replaced by real text, and the headline paragraphs and result discussion are still missing. Candidates for cutting or moving:
  - The Appendix "Optional Mechanisms" (merge verification, Horvitz-Thompson screen accounting), which the experiments do not use.
  - The generated per-arm settings tables, which list empty fields such as `minimax_api_key=`, `copilot_exec_home=` and `cursor_exec_path=`.
  - Sec. 2.3 (gate replay), the resume-time detail in Sec. 3.3, and the zero-SE edge case.
  - Fig. 7, Fig. 9 and Table 12.
- **Prose slips toward the AI register in places, mostly through vague quantifiers and generic openers.**
  - Openers that announce rather than state: "Table 1 lists the three suites." "Table 2 lists the arms." "Two design points matter for the measurement contract." "Evaluation hygiene is the oldest problem here."
  - Vague amounts: "far less often", "much of an earlier gain", "The arithmetic was mostly right", "cleaner second test", "Even at 40 tasks the ... MDE is 0.10 to 0.13", "Roughly".
  - Aphoristic ending: "A reported gain is a measurement before it is a capability." followed by "Whichever arm leads, the isolation tests and matched checkpoint let others check a gain." This says nothing a reader can use. The Ethics section is one generic sentence that ignores the real risk the paper documents (proposer shell access and gold exposure).

### Detailed Comments

**Story level.** Pick the crux and let it organize the title, abstract, and section order. If the claim is "a protocol changes which conclusions survive", the title should say so, and CapEvolve should be introduced as the system studied, not as the headline. The introduction currently has two framings: an audit of one's own earlier gains (paragraph 2) and a head-to-head comparison (paragraphs 3 and 4). Contribution 3 ("full configurations, costs and incidents") is not a contribution; it is reproducibility material, and it also carries a section roadmap and a \needs note. The abstract's last sentence ("the tau2 suites are descriptive") ends on a caveat where the guide wants the finding or the stakes; once results exist, put the headline outcome there and keep "If a control or a simpler method wins, that is the main result" as the framing sentence, since it is the strongest line in the paper.

**Section level.** Sec. 3 starts with a formal definition and no gist sentence. A two-line opener ("Both CapEvolve algorithms share one promotion rule; this section defines the rule, the budget, and the measurement contract it assumes") would orient readers. Sec. 3.3 holds seven topics in one block: the algorithm, an edge case, two design points, the parameter values with library defaults, the simulation results, the per-candidate cost, and the experimental configuration. Split the paragraph by claim, move the edge case and the library defaults to the appendix, and open each paragraph with its point. Sec. 4.4 is one 20-line paragraph with about 12 sentences of mixed definitions, contrast counts, and decision rules; a three-item list or three short paragraphs (what we estimate, how we test, when we claim a win) would be easier to skim. Sec. 5 uses topical headings ("Suite A, SpreadsheetBench", "Cost"); once results exist these should state the finding, as 5.4 and 5.5 already do.

**Paragraph and skim test.** Reading only first sentences of Sec. 2: "Four hazards inflated or obscured the earlier CapEvolve results", then "The historical SpreadsheetBench scorer loads cell values ...", "Both metrics were recorded from the same output workbook ...", "The historical test-280 was scored in development runs ...", "Gold answers sat in the directory ...", "The hill-climb gate accepts ...". This survives and is the best-structured part of the paper. Sec. 3 and 4 do not survive: "Both algorithms follow ...", "Hill-climb. Each iteration ...", "Because the two default gates ...", "When the paired SE is exactly zero ...", "Two design points matter ...", "Table 1 lists ...". The last two are generic. Sec. 3.5 first sentences are good ("We make no statistical guarantee ...", "The proposer is an LLM agent ...").

**Sentence level.**
- Intro paragraph 3 strings five short passive sentences ("is reported", "is checked", "governs", "are compared", "are not used") with different grammatical subjects. Make "the protocol" or "we" the subject and keep one form, e.g. one parallel sentence per part.
- Sec. 3.5, sentence beginning "Rule P lowers the false-promotion rate but does not make it zero: the confirmation controls trial noise ..." runs about 50 words with three coordinated clauses and ends its stress on "inflated deltas". Split it so the stress falls on what rule P does not control (same-task luck and task-set adaptivity), which is the sentence that follows and is the actual message.
- Nominalizations: "the selection of a candidate on its own screen draws", "the removal of selection bias from the reference", "the confirmation of the promoted candidate, not its screen measurement, is the next baseline, which removes the selection bias from the reference that later candidates must beat". Verbs ("we select", "we re-measure the promoted candidate and use that measurement as the next baseline") are shorter.
- Stress: Sec. 4.3 ends "...labelled as unmatched." Sec. 5.1 ends "...is the test of whether search pays." Those are good. Sec. 2.2 ends the paragraph on the byte-identical champion anecdote, which is vivid but weakens the paragraph's point (gold access); consider moving it earlier.
- Parallel form: Sec. 5.1 and 5.2 report the same quantity in different forms ("2 of the ten optimizer arms beat the seed" vs "0 arms clear"). Use one template for both suites.
- Repeated hedging with "can", "may", "would": the Sec. 1 claim "the numbers cannot be compared" needs either a cite or a softened form ("are not directly comparable").

**Units and numbers.** The paper writes gains as fractions in Sec. 3 and Appendix B ("+0.08", "0.0014", MDE "0.12"), as points in Sec. 5 and the tables, and as percent in Fig. 8. The guide says use %. Pick points throughout. Probabilities are reported as 0.050 in text and 5.0% in Fig. 8. The null search is "about ten candidates" in the abstract, Sec. 3.3 and Table 10 (0.040 to 0.113), but "six candidates" in Sec. 5.4 and Fig. 8 (0.050, 0.434). A reader will see 0.040 and 0.050 for what seems to be the same quantity. The "true effect of ten points" in Sec. 5.4 (0.41 vs 0.54) does not appear in Table 10 (Suite A 0.55 / 0.64, B 0.48 / 0.59), so the reader cannot trace it. State, for every number, which simulation produced it. Sec. 4.4 gives the Suite B MDE as 0.16, Sec. 5.2 as "0.10 to 0.13", and Appendix B as 0.145 (floor); reconcile or explain. The hard-coded "0.088 across six evaluation batches" in Sec. 4.4 equals the macro `withinVarA` (0.088, within-task variance) in Appendix B; please check that this is not a copy slip between two different quantities.

**Mocked outcomes stated as findings (flag per the author conventions).** Sec. 5.1 ("2 of the ten optimizer arms beat the seed ... the largest paired delta is +6.7 points, held by SkillOpt") and Sec. 5.2 ("HarnessLens", "0 arms clear") are written as findings around red placeholders. This is fine for a mock if the \needs notes remain, but the sentence skeleton already commits to a ranking narrative ("largest delta ... held by X"). When real numbers arrive, a sentence that ranks arms whose intervals overlap will contradict the paper's own rule ("no detectable difference"). Write the result sentence in the form the stats rule allows: which contrasts reach Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 and which do not.

**Tables.**
- Table 1: the Final column mixes a number and a status ("120 score-only exposed"); split into "Final tasks" and "Status". A large blank gap follows the table.
- Table 2: the "Notes" column wraps to five lines ("rule P, last promoted candidate"), which wastes about a quarter of a column. Widen Notes, shorten entries, drop "Pins are in Appendix A" from the caption. Add HC and AO abbreviations here, since they are used later. Mixed-case "A B C" suite list could be a compact check mark.
- Table 3 and 4: the caption opens with a description ("Suite A results, baselines by ascending delta ...") instead of the point. The intro promises the functional scorer "reported next to the legacy one" and Fig. 1 says "both scorers", but the main tables show a single Score column; add the as-saved score or state it is in the appendix. The table reports the paired delta to the seed, to the one-shot, and a Holm p for each arm against the seed, but Sec. 4.4 says competitor-vs-seed contrasts belong to a secondary family with its own Holm correction while only CapEvolve rows are in the primary family. Say in the caption which rows belong to which family, or the reader cannot tell what "2 of ten arms beat the seed" is adjusted for. The tables duplicate Fig. 2; one of them should move to the appendix.
- Table 15: the column "Rollouts at B*" lists 528, 538, 529, ... for arms said to be compared at the same B* (540). Either the column should be identical by construction or it should be renamed "Rollouts used (at most B*)" with a sentence on why they differ.
- Table 18 (audit claims) has a first column about 0.9 in wide, so most cells wrap and the table reads as a ragged list. Widen the first column.
- Table 17 has a caption of about 190 words; much of it should be body text or removed. The "0 (not started)" strings in numeric columns break alignment.
- Table 12 (error rows): 40 rows of integers with no summary; show totals per arm or move to the repository.

**Figures.**
- Fig. 1: most text is \scriptsize or \tiny (about 5 pt at print size), well below the guide's "as large as \small". The teal vs light-grey coding is low-contrast and fails in grayscale. The legend says teal means "fixed by the protocol", yet the promotion box ("rule P or native rule per arm") is teal even though the caption admits competitors keep native rules; the message "same everywhere except the rule" is muddled. The figure repeats the intro paragraph almost word for word, so its main job should be what the text cannot do: show which data (opt, sel, final, gold) each component can see. Start the caption with the claim ("Only the acceptance rule differs between arms") instead of "The measurement pipeline."
- Fig. 2: arms are ordered neither by delta nor in the table's order, so the eye path is long; sort by delta with CapEvolve last, as the table does. Panels have different x ranges (about 10, 12, 20) and panels B and C have no row labels for C; share the y-axis. The caption does not name panels (a) to (c) or say that open vs filled marks significance, which is the one thing the figure should pop out. Marker shape plus color per arm is redundant with direct row labels.
- Fig. 3: left panel is the best figure in the paper. Right panel (three bars for 40 claims) is one sentence of text and can go. The word "most" in the caption does not hold for Gemma (see above). The right panel uses green/orange/grey with a different meaning than the left's grey/blue.
- Fig. 4: x label "Difference in paired delta (points)" is unclear; "CapEvolve minus baseline" is the quantity. Rows are in no stated order. The two CapEvolve arms are drawn with thick vs thin lines of similar blue, which is hard to separate for colorblind readers.
- Fig. 5: ten lines in panel A with 11 legend entries; the y axes are truncated differently in each panel (about 50 to 58, 56 to 62, 40 to 48), which magnifies small gaps. The caption does not say what "best score so far" is measured on. If it is the final set, intermediate artifacts were scored on a set that the paper says is scored once; if it is the selection set, it is selection-biased and "best so far" is exactly the "best-looking candidate" the method section says is never returned. State the set and justify.
- Fig. 6: the x label is clipped at the right edge in the PDF ("Optimizer cost (USD, log scale"), the only tick label is "10.0", and the seed (cost 0) cannot appear on a log axis. Use "$1, $10, $100" ticks. Dashed frontier lines connect stepwise through points without explaining dominance in the caption.
- Fig. 7: its fonts are about twice as large as every other figure, the legend sits over the top gridline, and F1 to F6 are unreadable labels; name the families or move the figure out.
- Fig. 8: the clearest evidence for a contribution sits in the appendix. The legend names "Rule P, stricter" and "Rule P, z = 1.645" that appear nowhere in text. Five series blur the one comparison that matters (campaign rule P vs one-stage k = 1); show those two prominently and grey the rest. The caption cites an internal file path as the source.
- Fig. 9: the legend is drawn over the bars, and the symlog axis makes the output and cache-write segments invisible, so the plot cannot support the claim "Target calls dominate tokens" for Suite B, where the optimizer's cache-read bar nearly matches the user and judge bars. The figure is a candidate to cut.
- Cross-figure consistency: font sizes, tick label styles ("10.0" vs "10"), and panel layouts differ by figure. Several captions end with "(placeholder)", which is fine for a draft, but the watermark already says this.

**Repository paths in running text.** The text and captions cite `audit/historical_results.md`, `audit/exposure_ledger.csv`, `audit/recomputed_numbers.csv`, `analysis/out/sim_mde.md`, `analysis/out/sim_budget.md`, `scripts/gen_config_tables.py` and `error_label_parity.csv`. In a double-blind submission these are unreachable unless there is an anonymized artifact, and they interrupt sentences. Replace them with an appendix table number or a single "artifact" pointer.

**Page budget.** Cut in this order: Sec. 5.5 reduced to two sentences and a pointer to Sec. 2; Sec. 6 first and second paragraphs merged into one that states what distinguishes arms (once results exist); Sec. 2.3 moved to an appendix with one summary sentence; Sec. 3.2 hill-climb and agent-optimize descriptions shortened to the parts the comparison depends on (who proposes, what is read, which gate); the Optional Mechanisms appendix removed; the per-arm settings tables replaced by the few settings the text uses. This frees roughly a page to carry the headline-results text and a real forest figure in the body.

### Minor Issues
- Title: "CapEvolve: A Measurement-First Comparison of ..." is a colon-titled title, which the authors' own style rule bans for headings. "Measurement-first" is not defined in the text.
- Dashes: the TeX source uses `propose--evaluate--promote` (renders as en-dash compounds). Use "propose, evaluate and promote".
- Italic run-in headers inside paragraphs ("Frozen model, editable artifact.", "Tasks and splits.", "Hill-climb.", "Evaluator isolation.", "Estimand and caveat.", and the Limitations bullets) function like the banned lead-ins. Use subsubsection titles or drop them.
- Sec. 3.2 cross-reference reads "Table 14 (Appendix I)"; Sec. 5.3 mentions Figure 6 before Figure 5. Reorder or renumber.
- Sec. 4.2: "VERSE, CoEvoSkills, and HarnessLens on Suite A failed to reproduce" reads as if HarnessLens failed everywhere; say "HarnessLens on Suite A" with a different construction.
- Sec. 4.2: "Five more methods (AHE, MCE, RHO, Memento-Skills, GraphSkillEvo) were fixed as one-seed exploratory runs ... and stay out of the headline tables" is a darling; the appendix lists three of them as "stopped". One sentence suffices, or remove.
- Algorithm 1 uses |Δ| both as an absolute value and as a count (line 2, line 7); use |T| or n for the count. The superscript c on Δ^c and the subscript c on m'_c should match.
- Sec. 4.5 "Claude models use list prices as a proxy" and Table 5 repeat each other.
- Budgets disagree: text and Appendix A shared settings say 600 target rollouts per search for all suites, while the generated tables record max_metric_calls=550 for Suites B and C (and Appendix B simulates 550 for Suite B).
- Sec. 5.2 "Suite B gives the cleaner second test" and Sec. 5.2 "Even at 40 tasks" use "cleaner" and "Even" without a comparison; state the comparison.
- Table 3/4 columns "USD" lacks the note that it covers the optimizer only; the caption of Table 3 says so but Table 4 only says "Columns as in Table 3".
- "tau2", "tau^2" and "$\tau^2$-bench" appear in headings; use one form.
- Footnote 3 (file sha256 ...) repeats a hash that is also in Appendix A.
- Related Work: "the tables of GEPA and SkillOpt print one score per cell without an interval" is a claim about other papers; cite the tables or soften. "Evaluation hygiene is the oldest problem here" is unsupported; "A reused holdout becomes a training set" is the point.
- Ethics: one sentence; either expand with the actual risks (proposer shell access, gold leakage, tamper detection limits) or fold into Limitations.
- "Preliminary work. Under review ..." footer and anonymous author block are fine; the "Anonymous Country" placeholders can be tidied.
