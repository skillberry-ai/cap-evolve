# Review R2: Mid-Career Researcher, Same Subfield

Paper: CapEvolve: Optimizing Any Editable Surface of a Frozen Agent Under Code-Owned Evaluation (MLSys 2027 draft). Placeholders (red dagger values, mock figures, EVIDENCE NEEDED slots) are not penalized. I judge the design, the competitor protocol, the statistics, and the prior-work characterization.

## Summary

CapEvolve is an optimization loop for frozen agents with four parts. (1) The editable file set (prompt, tool code, MCP tool descriptions, skill package) is a parameter of the loop, with a per-type edit policy. (2) Gold isolation, a two-stage promotion rule ("rule P", screen then fresh-trial confirmation) and a sealed final set sit in core code the optimizer cannot edit. (3) The optimizer sees a "causal" record of which tasks each earlier edit fixed and broke. (4) The loop runs either as a deterministic hill-climb or as a coding agent driving the same gate. The paper compares three CapEvolve configurations with nine published optimizers on SpreadsheetBench (Suite A) and tau2 retail and airline (B, C) at a matched rollout checkpoint, with a Suite D ablation family on gpt-4.1-mini. The pre-registered statistical protocol is careful and unusually honest. I like the self-audit in Appendix E and the refusal to write "no difference" under low power. But the experimental design cannot support the headline claims, one of the four contributions (history) has no experiment, and the novelty of the gate and history ideas is overstated against DarwinX and Meta-Harness.

## Strengths

- The measurement discipline is real: scorer audit, split ledger, sealed final set, TOST equivalence margin, two interval types, Holm families fixed before launch, dated amendments in Appendix I. Most papers in this subfield do none of this.
- The limitations section is candid. It lists surface asymmetry, power, exposure, and the unrun ablations.
- Appendix B reports false-promotion and power operating points for the gate from a budget-exact simulation rather than asserting them.
- The negative result on one-standard-error gates under trial noise (about half of no-op edits look like small wins) is a useful, citable observation.

## Top 6 weaknesses, ranked

### W1. The design cannot discriminate CapEvolve from competitors, and the paper's own numbers say so (Sections 4.4, 7; Appendix B)

Three search seeds give df = 2 for method-level claims. The method-level MDE is 24, 23 and 29 points (Section 4.4). The artifact-level MDE is 11 to 15 points, and 16/21/28 after Holm. The final sets are 70 tasks x 1 trial (A), 40 x 3 (B) and 20 x 3 (C). Published gaps among GEPA, SkillOpt, ACE and MIPROv2 are single-digit points on tasks like these. The pipeline-level 90% half-width is about 0.2, twice the equivalence margin, so "equivalence of the optimizer is never claimed". The abstract and Section 5.1 nonetheless promise a verdict on "standing against the nine competitors". With this design the most probable outcome is "inconclusive" on every contrast. A paper whose headline table cannot rank its own method is not a method paper yet.

Fix: raise search seeds to at least 5 per arm and trials to at least 3 on Suite A. Spend the budget on fewer competitors (see W2) rather than 11 arms at 3 seeds. State a power calculation for the headline contrast in the abstract: what difference vs the best competitor would be detectable. If that is not achievable, retitle the claim around "the audited protocol and the ablations", and cut the "standing against nine optimizers" language from the abstract and introduction.

### W2. The competitor comparison is asymmetric in ways that favor CapEvolve, and the "nine competitors" count overstates what is compared (Section 4.2, Table 3, Appendices A, C)

- **Count.** Of the nine, one is a community port (WikiSkill). Three are "cheap" arms at native budgets, below the cap (Trace2Skill about 200 rollouts, ACE with a renamed output field, MIPROv2). HarnessLens, the only competitor that edits more than a prompt, runs only on Suite B (and C), having failed to reproduce on A. On the two suites where tool editing matters (B, C), the competitor set is GEPA (prompt only) and HarnessLens. So the surface claim is never tested against a tool-capable baseline on a suite where tools matter.
- **Surface restriction.** Table 3 says "On Suite A every arm edits the same two prompt files". That strips Meta-Harness (harness code), EvoSkill and Trace2Skill (skill folders) and WikiSkill (a wiki) of their native surface and mechanism. Yet the paper presents them as run "from official repositories". A result like Meta-Harness = -1.1 on Suite A mostly measures the adapter, not the method. The paper needs to say plainly that these are re-targeted versions, and show at least one competitor in native-surface form.
- **Error handling favors CapEvolve (Appendix C).** Before 13:11 on 2026-10-11, 1 to 2.5% of Gemma calls failed. The shared adapter scored these as zero for competitor arms while CapEvolve "dropped errored trials", and the paper says this "may favour CapEvolve in search feedback". Competitor search histories keep these zeros for ACE, MIPROv2, GEPA, SkillOpt and Meta-Harness (86 infra errors in Meta-Harness alone). That is a systematic noise injection into the competitors' selection signals, not a footnote. Separately, on Suite B CapEvolve "leaves a missing cell without retry" while GEPA and the controls retry inside the budget.
- **Data and compute exposure.** Stock CapEvolve sees 40 tasks where competitors see 120 (acknowledged, "direction we cannot sign"). The CapEvolve proposer is Claude Code with 80 to 300 turns and up to $8 or $40 per run, against a single reflection call in GEPA. Optimizer cost is 2 to 5x the competitors (Tables 4, 5). Matching on target rollouts only is defensible, but then "loop quality" (Section 4.2) is confounded with proposer agency.
- **Tuning asymmetry.** Rule P was tuned by simulation on the same suites' seed variance (Appendix B). Competitors run with defaults. The paper says so (Section 7) but offers no mitigation.
- **Pins.** Table 10 gives a blob-by-blob check only for GEPA and SkillOpt. For EvoSkill, Trace2Skill, ACE, MIPROv2 and HarnessLens the pin cell reads "official repositories" with no commit or check.

Fix: (a) report an "adapter-fair" table with all competitors under the same error policy (re-score or re-run with identical zero/drop handling), or restrict claims to arms unaffected. (b) Add Meta-Harness and one skill method (SkillOpt or EvoSkill) in native form on one suite, even single-seed, so the "re-targeted" caveat can be bounded. (c) Pin every repo with commit and file check. (d) Move the cheap arms out of the "nine competitors" count, or give them the full cap.

### W3. Missing strong cheap baselines, on the paper's own benchmarks (Section 6, Section 4.2)

The related work cites Singh et al. 2026 (CASD) and Chang & Chen 2026 (NPO) as showing that one offline analysis pass or single-lineage revision "rival GEPA and SkillOpt". CASD evaluates on SpreadsheetBench-Verified and tau2 retail, the same benchmarks used here. The paper says "We therefore include seed and one-shot controls", but:

- The one-shot control is CapEvolve's own unconditional step. It is not CASD or NPO.
- Suite A has no one-shot control at all (Table 4 caption).
- There is no best-of-k or test-time-scaling control except on Suite C. Wang et al. 2026b, which the paper cites, argues exactly this baseline is missing.

Without these, a CapEvolve gain over GEPA cannot be attributed to the gate, the history or the surface rather than to "a coding agent reads trajectories".

Fix: add a CASD-style single analysis pass and an NPO-style single-lineage arm on Suites A and B (they are cheap), and a one-shot control on Suite A.

### W4. Rule P is under-justified, yields near-zero expected gain by the authors' own simulation, and the shipped default was never evaluated on the clean suite (Section 3, Table 14, Section 5.1)

- **Expected gain.** Table 14 shows rule P's expected true new-task gain is +0.0014, +0.0011 and +0.0024 (0.1 to 0.24 points). The one-stage k = 1 gate gets +0.0034 to +0.0051. The "pre-declared criterion" (maximise gain subject to false promotion at most 0.15) selects the rule with 2 to 4x lower expected gain. The primary-family CapEvolve arms use rule P, and search-time data show the seed artifact returned in 2/3, 3/3 and 3/3 searches. The main comparison is therefore largely CapEvolve-as-inert-wrapper vs competitors. Section 7 admits this ("promotes almost nothing; the final evaluation decides whether that is reliability or inertia").
- **The shipped gate is the post-hoc arm.** The shipped default is the stock gate. Its arm was added post-search-time (Amendment 6, after seeing selection results). It does not exist on Suite B, the only suite with a sealed, non-exposed test. So the arm that the abstract describes ("CapEvolve") is evaluated only post hoc on A and on the exposed C.
- **Strawman comparator.** The motivating figure "a one-standard-error gate promotes a no-op in 0.52 to 0.58 of searches" is computed for the unmasked one-stage k = 1 gate under an assumed edit mix (60% null, 25% positive, 15% negative). It is not the shipped gate (footprint masking on, coverage 0.6) and not the gates in GEPA (minibatch then full-set), SkillOpt (strict gain on held-out split) or WikiSkill. No competitor gate is run through the same simulator. A gate with k = 2 and the same total rollouts spent on extra selection tasks is the obvious fair control, and it is missing.
- **Confirmation removes less than claimed.** Confirmation re-evaluates the same selection tasks with new trials, so it removes trial noise but not task-selection bias. The paper concedes this ("does not control adaptivity over the task set") and then says confirmation "removes the selection bias from the reference". On 10 to 25 selection tasks (Suite C: 10), task adaptivity dominates. The confirmation costs 2|Dsel| nconf = 60 to 240 rollouts per screened candidate of a 250 to 400 matched budget.
- **No comparison with the cited statistical gates.** SAGE (paired one-sided test), PACE (anytime-valid e-process), BudgetAPO and CAPO are cited as "these papers contribute the statistics". None is run as a gate in the same loop, so "rule P is simpler and carries no formal guarantee" has no empirical counterweight.

Fix: (a) report, in the abstract and Table 1, which gate is the headline, and run the stock gate on Suite B pre-final. (b) Add a matched-rollout one-stage k = 2 gate and a SAGE-style paired test in Suite D. (c) Run at least GEPA's and SkillOpt's accept logic through the Appendix B simulator. (d) State the edit-mix assumption's effect on the rule P choice with a sensitivity table (null fraction 40/60/80%).

### W5. "Causal history" is a headline contribution with no experiment, and the name and the novelty are overstated (Abstract, Section 2.4, contribution 3, Section 6, Section 5.2)

- **Unrun ablation stated as run.** The abstract says "and ablate the surface, the gate, the orchestration mode and the history". Contribution 5 says "ablations of the surface, gate, mode and history". Section 5.2 and Section 7 say the history ablation "was not approved" and was not run. These statements conflict. The abstract states a result that does not exist.
- **"Causal" is a misnomer.** The record is a pair of sets (tasks fixed, tasks broken) from one-trial paired outcome differences on multi-part edits. This is association under trial noise (Suite A final is one trial; selection runs use num_trials = 1). Flaky tasks will populate both sets. No attribution to a hunk or an edit component is made, and nothing is intervened on. Only the merge-verify mechanism in Appendix F has a causal flavour, and it is default-off.
- **Novelty vs prior art.** Meta-Harness's central design is a proposer that queries a filesystem of all prior candidates' code, scores and traces. This is a richer history than the one here. EvoSkill logs prior proposals with score deltas, SkillOpt keeps a rejected-edit buffer, DarwinX stores per-task scores, ACE tracks helpful/harmful counters. Related work lists the last four but omits Meta-Harness from the "record of earlier edits" sentence. CapEvolve's proposer gets the full trajectories of the current best only, "not the seed and every rejected attempt" (Section 2.4), the opposite of Meta-Harness's choice. That is an interesting design contrast that should be tested, not assumed.

Fix: either run the history-off ablation (a single cell on Suite D is enough) or remove history from the abstract, the contributions list and the ablation claims. Rename to "fix/break ledger". Add a Meta-Harness-style full-history cell.

### W6. Surface claims rest on one different-model suite and may include an environment-editing confound (Sections 2.1, 5.2, 5.3; Table 1)

- **Evidence base.** "What a surface is worth" is drawn only from Suite D: gpt-4.1-mini on the Suite B splits, 25 selection tasks and 40 sealed tasks, not pooled with A to C. Its MDE is about 11 points, and the P+T vs P contrast needs more than the 10-point margin to be called "surface width matters". Section 5.3 itself says E10 "cannot separate surfaces".
- **Edit space is not equalised.** Prompt+tools gets a larger edit space under the same budget, with one run per cell.
- **Failure taxonomy.** The statement that the failures are "the kind of failure tool code can enforce" (Section 5.3) is an interpretation of Table 7 labels (forgotten step, wrong arguments). Wrong arguments are not obviously fixable by a tool guard.
- **Environment confound.** In tau2-bench the tool bodies are the environment: they mutate the database whose state the reward checks. Table 1 lets the optimizer "add guards to existing tool bodies, enrich errors, ... swap through a wrapper" and alter "return values". The paper never says whether the scorer replays gold actions or compares final DB state through the candidate's edited tools, so a tool-code edit could change the reference outcome or make the task easier by construction. Code-owned evaluation covers gold and the final set, but not the scorer's dependence on the code being edited.
- **Coverage.** Only two of four surfaces are evaluated. The title says "Any Editable Surface". MCP is "implemented and unit-tested but not evaluated"; the skill package cell is conditional.

Fix: state precisely what the scorer executes (candidate tools or pristine tools) and show a negative test where a hostile tool edit tries to change the outcome. Add a second target model for Suite D. Retitle, for example "Prompts and Tool Code of a Frozen Agent", unless the MCP and skill cells run.

## Mischaracterized or misleadingly incomplete prior work

1. **DarwinX, Table 8 and Section 6.** Table 8 gives DarwinX's acceptance rule as "must extend solved set without regressions", and Section 6 mentions its candidate DAG and per-task stores. It omits the second stage. DarwinX states: "candidates with bounded regression risk may become ancestors, while stricter avg@k confirmation controls promotion" and "stricter avg@k re-tests and preservation probes decide which variants may steer future search". DarwinX's own Table 1 has a "noise-aware avg@k" column. This is a select-then-reconfirm structure with repeated trials, the closest precedent to rule P's screen-then-confirm. The paper calls the statistics of acceptance something "these papers contribute" (CAPO, BudgetAPO, SAGE, PACE) and places DarwinX only in the search-structure bucket. Rule P's novelty is claimed against the wrong neighbours.
2. **MCP descriptions, Section 6.** "In the papers we read, none reports editing the descriptions an MCP server exposes." The hedge is literal, but the surrounding framing implies tool-description optimization is new. DRAFT (Qu et al., 2024; described in the EvoTool related work) "iteratively refines tool documentation through self-driven tool interactions and feedback-based rewriting", and EvoTool evolves tool-use policy modules (Planner, Selector, Caller, Synthesizer). Neither is cited. Since the MCP surface is not evaluated here, the novelty claim sits on an unexecuted surface.
3. **Meta-Harness history.** Section 6 lists methods that "keep a record of earlier edits" and leaves out Meta-Harness, whose proposer inspects "the filesystem" of prior harness code, scores and traces ("The filesystem is typically far larger than the proposer's context window, so the proposer queries it through terminal tools"). Table 8 also appears to give Meta-Harness only the H column. Its own text describes search over "prompts, context-management rules, and tool-use logic".
4. **Checked and fine.** SkillOpt ("rejected edits are retained as negative feedback", "strictly improves a held-out validation score"), EvoSkill (logs "all prior proposals, their outcomes, and score deltas"; frontier of k programs), ACE (counters for helpful/harmful), GEPA (Pareto) and Chang & Chen and Singh et al.'s headline claims are characterized correctly.

## Questions for the authors

1. What does the tau2 reward execute when a candidate edits tool code: the candidate's tools or a pristine copy? Can an edit change the gold DB state?
2. Why is the unmasked k = 1 gate the comparator, rather than the shipped (footprint-masked) gate or competitors' gates, in the 0.52 to 0.58 false-promotion figure?
3. For Table 6 and Section 5.2: the text says rule P returned the seed in 2/3, 3/3, 3/3 searches, while Table 6 shows "0 of 3" (A) and "1 of 3" (C) for rule P. The text also gives 2.0 and 1.0 stock-gate promotions per search against 4.0 and 4.3 in the table, and Figure 3 shows 2.3 / 1.3 / 4.3 promoted per search. Which is right, and are any of these the "orange real" numbers?
4. With the shipped gate promoting 2 to 4 candidates per search on selection sets of 25 to 40 tasks, what is the expected selection-to-final shrinkage, and is it reported per competitor too?
5. Is the history ablation planned? If not, why is history in the abstract?
6. Suite C's selection split has 10 tasks and its test is development-exposed. What can it show?
7. Are competitors' optimizer calls (not just target rollouts) capped at the same 4M tokens?

## What would change my score

- Up to 6 or 7: five or more seeds with per-arm adapter-fair error handling (W1, W2); native-surface runs of at least Meta-Harness plus one skill method; CASD and NPO baselines on A and B (W3); the stock gate and a k = 2 one-stage gate on the sealed Suite B (W4); the history-off cell, or its removal from every claim (W5); a scorer statement plus a hostile-edit negative test for tool code (W6).
- Down to 3: if the final evaluation again returns "inconclusive" on all headline contrasts and the abstract still claims "standing against nine competitors"; if the stock-gate arm stays post hoc as the headline; if the abstract keeps the history claim without an experiment.

## Score and confidence

**Score: 4 / 10** (borderline reject on the current design; the audit and protocol work is valuable and could carry a workshop or systems-track version).
**Confidence: 4 / 5** (I checked the SkillOpt, EvoSkill, ACE, Meta-Harness, DarwinX, CASD and NPO characterizations against the PDFs; I did not independently verify the simulation numbers).
