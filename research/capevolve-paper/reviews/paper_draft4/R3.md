# Review R3: Early-Career Researcher, Same Subfield

Paper: CapEvolve: Optimizing Any Editable Surface of a Frozen Agent Under Code-Owned Evaluation (MLSys 2027 draft 4)
Standard applied: top-venue, draft with placeholders. Dagger values and mock figures were not penalized; internal consistency, notation, undefined terms and claim/evidence fit were checked.

## Summary

The paper proposes a loop in which the editable surface (prompt, tool code, MCP descriptions, skill package) is a parameter. Evaluation decisions (gold isolation, a two-stage promotion rule "rule P", a sealed final set) live in code the optimizer cannot edit, and the optimizer gets a record of which tasks each earlier edit fixed and broke. It runs as a deterministic hill-climb or as an agent driving the same gate. The evaluation covers SpreadsheetBench (A), τ2 retail (B), τ2 airline (C) and a retail ablation family (D), against nine competitors at a matched rollout checkpoint, with an unusually careful statistics plan and a candid audit of the authors' own earlier inflated results. The framing and measurement discipline are the real strengths. The draft as written, though, has many places where one quantity is reported two ways, where text, table and appendix describe different protocols, and where the abstract promises ablations and surfaces that Section 5 and Section 7 say were not run.

## Score and confidence

Score: 5/10 (borderline, leaning weak reject in the current state; the inconsistencies are fixable, the structural risk in defect 4 is not purely editorial).
Confidence: 4/5. I checked every table against its neighbours and against the LaTeX macros, but I cannot see the run records.

## Strengths

- Code-owned evaluation, the pre-registered family structure, the explicit equivalence rule ("inconclusive, not no difference"), and the two-interval (artifact vs pipeline) device are good practice and rare in this subfield.
- Appendix E arithmetic checks out (33.0 - 18.7 = 14.3, 43%; 49.1 - 9.9 = 39.2, 80%; 70.5% = 282/400; ceiling 98.6% = 276/280; Fig 5 right bars sum to 40).
- Table 14 matches the main text (0.068/0.040/0.113 vs 0.58/0.52/0.55; power 0.40/0.33/0.17 vs 0.53/0.49/0.36).
- Table 22/33 shrinkage column equals selection minus final in every row I checked, and the Table 21/32 "whole minus parts" column adds up.
- Limitations and infrastructure incidents are disclosed in unusual detail.

## Defects, ranked by severity

### 1. Which split gates acceptance is described three incompatible ways (Table 2, App. B, Table 25, Fig. 6)
Location: Table 2 (B: opt/sel 49/25; C: 20/10); Sec. 2.3 and Table 2 caption ("sel ... gate acceptance"); App. B first paragraph (simulation run on "Suite B: 49 optimization tasks, 550 rollouts; Suite C: 20 tasks", confirmation cost "2 * 40 * 5" on A and "490" on B, i.e. 2 * 49 * 5); Table 25 caption ("A: val-40, B: opt-49, C: opt-20"); Fig. 6 caption ("the selection split on Suites B and C").
Problem: If gating is on the 25 (B) and 10 (C) selection tasks, the rule-P operating characteristics in Sec. 3 / Table 14 (0.040, 0.113, power 0.33, 0.17) were simulated at the wrong task counts, although Sec. 5.2 says "at the real task counts". If gating is on the 49 and 20 opt tasks, then "sel" in Table 2 and the proposer-reads-opt / gate-reads-sel separation are not what the run did, and on B the optimizer both reads and gates on the same tasks. Either way the headline gate numbers are not shown to apply to the reported runs. Suite C with 10 gating tasks would make the |Delta| < 2 and coverage 0.95 conditions in Algorithm 2 nearly always binding.
Fix: One table stating, per suite and per arm, the task set the proposer reads, the set the gate scores, and the set the simulation used; rerun Table 14 at the true gating counts if they differ.

### 2. Suite A final set: verified-400 or the 912-question residual pool?
Location: Sec. 4.1, Table 2 caption, Table 4 header ("verified-400"), App. A.2 (SkillOpt split 80/40/280), Table 19 (11:35), Sec. 7 "Exposure" ("an older release"), App. E.
Problem: The target runs on the "400-task verified subset", Table 4 is headed "verified-400", and the split file is 80/40/280. But the final set is "a stratified sample of 239 ids from the 912-question release" that remain after removing development-exposed families. The 280-task test split is not 239, and 239 residual ids of 912 need not lie in the verified 400. The scorer audit (19 of 400 gold files fail, 4 unscorable, 70.5% formula answers) covers only the verified 400, so scorer validity on the final 70 tasks is unaudited. Sec. 7 calls the same pool "an older release", a third name. Table 2 says "score-only exposed" (scored at most twice) while Sec. 4.1 says exposed families were removed.
Fix: State exactly how the 70 are drawn, whether they are inside verified-400, rerun the gold-as-output scorer check on those 70, and use one name for the pool.

### 3. The abstract, intro, Sec. 2.4 and contribution 5 promise ablations that Sec. 5.2 and Sec. 7 say were not run
Location: Abstract ("ablate the surface, the gate, the orchestration mode and the history"), Contribution 5, Sec. 2.4 ("Section 5 tests whether this history matters by removing it", footnote 4 still asks for the history-off result), Sec. 5.2 "Causal history and intake" ("The history ablation and the descriptions-only and skill-package cells were not approved"), Sec. 7 Ablations, Sec. 8.
Problem: Two of four surfaces (MCP, skill) and one of the three named design components (causal history, listed in the title-level claim "gives the optimizer a causal history") have no evidence. The title says "Any Editable Surface" while only prompt and tool code are evaluated, and Table 8's caption admits this. A reader of the abstract will expect a history ablation.
Fix: Either run the history-off cell or remove it from the abstract, contribution 5 and Sec. 2.4, and soften the title or subtitle to the surfaces actually tested.

### 4. The method whose operating characteristics are the main technical contribution is, in the real search-time data, almost inert, and the cost in expected gain is not surfaced
Location: Abstract and Contribution 2 (two-stage rule, "measured operating characteristics"); Table 23 / Table 27 / Table 29 (real data); Table 14 "Gain" column; Sec. 5.2 Gate; Sec. 7 Gates.
Problem: Rule P is off by default and the shipped configuration is the stock gate, which by the authors' own simulation falsely promotes in 52 to 58% of null searches. In the live data, agent-optimize returned the seed in 3/3 searches on A, B and C, rule-P hill-climb in 2/3, 3/3, 3/3, and Table 29 shows 1 promotion from roughly 26 rule-P hill-climb candidates. Table 14's own expected true gain of the final artifact is +0.0014 to +0.0024 for rule P (0.14 to 0.24 points) and +0.0034 to +0.0051 for the one-stage gate, under the authors' stress mix. That is far below every effect discussed in Sec. 4.4 (MDE 11 to 15 points) and is not mentioned in the body. The primary-family contrasts for the two main CapEvolve arms are then largely seed-versus-seed. The paper does say the verdict is "open", but the abstract and contributions are written as if the gate is a validated win.
Fix: Put the Gain column in Sec. 3, say plainly that under the simulated mix neither gate yields more than half a point, and make the stock-gate arm (currently labelled post-search-time) the headline or justify not doing so. Also report what fraction of primary-family cells are inert-by-construction.

### 5. The "nine competitors" are never compared together; roster is inconsistent across Sec. 4.2, Table 3, Tables 4/5, Fig. 2 and App. B
Location: Abstract, Intro, Sec. 4.2, Table 3, Table 4, Table 5, Fig. 2/4, App. B "primary contrast family".
Problem:
- Table 3 lists MIPROv2 and ACE on Suite A only and HarnessLens on Suite B only, yet Sec. 4.2, Sec. 5.1 and Sec. 7 say "GEPA, MIPROv2 and ACE edit the prompt only" on B and C, and Table 5, Table 22/33, Table 36 and Fig. 2(c) report HarnessLens on Suite C.
- Per suite the comparison is at most 8 (A), 2 (B), 2 (C) competitors. The abstract claim "against nine published optimizers" is never realized in one suite, and HarnessLens failed to reproduce on A.
- Table 4 and Table 5 label the competitors "secondary family", but Fig. 2 and Fig. 4 say the contrasts shown are "the primary family". App. B defines the primary family on A as only five competitors (GEPA, SkillOpt, Meta-Harness, WikiSkill, EvoSkill, 12 contrasts) but Fig. 2(a) draws eight. On C the primary family is seed, one-shot, GEPA (6 contrasts), but Table 5 and Fig. 2(c) include HarnessLens.
- Table 4's caption says pHolm is "within the suite"; the rows say "in family".
- Sec. 4.2 names VERSE, CoEvoSkills, AHE, MCE, RHO, Memento-Skills and GraphSkillEvo without citations; App. A.4 shows three of the five "exploratory" methods as "stopped, not recorded" and omits AHE and RHO.
Fix: One roster table (method, suites run, family membership, status), and make the captions of Tables 4/5 and Figs. 2/4 agree with App. B.

### 6. Primary Holm family on Suite D has nine contrasts, not ten
Location: Sec. 4.4 and App. B last paragraph ("ten contrasts").
Count from the text: prompt-only, tools-only, prompt+tools vs seed (3); prompt+tools vs each single surface (2); rule P and one-stage gate vs stock (2); prompt+tools vs one-shot (1); prompt-only vs GEPA (1) = 9. The Holm step-down multipliers depend on m. Fix: list the ten explicitly or correct the count, and re-derive the adjusted p-values.

### 7. Budget and candidate-count figures disagree
Location: Sec. 4.3 (N = 600), App. A.2 (600), App. A.4 (Suites B and C CapEvolve and one-shot: max_metric_calls = 550, GEPA 600, HarnessLens 600), App. B ("Suite B ... 550 rollouts"), Sec. 4.4 (Suite D "fixed budget of 550"); Sec. 3 ("a search of about ten candidates"), App. B (7.6, 6.7 and 9.4 candidates screened), Fig. 9 caption ("six candidates"), Sec. 2.4 ("ten-candidate searches (Figure 9)").
Problem: The budget "every arm" gets is 600 in the text but 550 in the B/C configs for the CapEvolve arms, so Suites B/C are not budget-equal across arms, and Suite D is described as a different budget. The 0.52 to 0.58 false-promotion rates are cited for "ten-candidate" searches, from a simulation that screens 6.7 to 9.4 candidates, while the figure the text cites for this claim (Fig. 9) reports 43.4% for the same one-stage k = 1 gate on six candidates. Also, rule P cost is "2 |Dsel| nconf" per screened candidate (240 on A); with 600 rollouts, 40 gating tasks and a baseline, only 2 to 3 confirmations fit, so please state that the "about ten candidates" comes from the screen cost only and is not the number of confirmations affordable.
Fix: Reconcile 550 vs 600 (and say which is the cap used in N*), use one candidate count for the 0.52 to 0.58 figures, and cite Fig. 9 only for what it shows.

### 8. Cost and token totals contradict each other across Sec. 5.4, App. D, Table 16 and Table 36
Location: Sec. 5.4 ("650 million non-cache tokens, at least $84"), App. D (623M RITS + 26.7M capped = 650M; "$84"), Table 16, Table 36, Table 28, Table 30.
Checks I ran:
- Table 36 optimizer USD sums to 83.97 on Suite A alone and 130.8 over A, B and C; the text's $84 matches only Suite A. Table 36 non-cache optimizer tokens sum to 40.4M, not 26.7M.
- Table 36 target tokens sum to 534M (A), 317M (B), 135M (C), about 986M; App. D says RITS served 623M, and Table 16 gives Suite A as 199M total, below Table 36's Suite A target tokens alone. If these are different snapshots the totals would not decrease; if different definitions (cache reads?) say so.
- Table 36 shows SkillOpt at 119% of the 4M optimizer cap on Suite A, contradicting Sec. 4.3 "up to 4M non-cache-read optimizer tokens" and Sec. A.2 "at most 4M"; no text addresses the overrun or how the matched-checkpoint comparison treats it.
- Table 28 (Opt. USD) is roughly one third of Table 36 (sum over seeds) without saying per-seed vs summed; Table 22/33 divides by 3 implicitly.
- Table 30 "Cap used (%)" equals the summed-over-seeds non-cache tokens times 10 in every row (5.1 -> 51, 7.2 -> 72), which is not "the largest seed as a share of 4M".
Fix: One metered-usage source, timestamped, with the same definition everywhere.

### 9. Paired deltas in Tables 4/5 are inconsistent with the score columns beside them, and the same contrast gets two intervals

- Suite B, delta vs one-shot: GEPA 56.3 - 57.9 = -1.6 but shown -1.0; CapEvolve HC 60.7 - 57.9 = +2.8 but shown +3.6.
- Suite C, delta vs one-shot: HC 42.6 - 39.2 = +3.4 but shown +5.7; AO 2.4 vs +3.4; GEPA 4.5 vs +4.1; HarnessLens 6.8 vs +7.1; stock 8.2 vs +8.6; best-of-k 3.0 vs +3.7. ### 11. Duplicated tables and figures from a double \input

### 12. Equivalence design cannot support the equivalence claims made
Location: Sec. 4.4 (margin 10 points), Sec. 5.2 Orchestration ("reach equivalent final gain"), App. B (MDE 0.106 on A, 0.110 on B; "pipeline-level 90% half-width ... is about 0.2, above the margin, so equivalence of the optimizer is never claimed"; "Suite C is descriptive"), Table 20/31 (verdict vocabulary).
Problem: The margin (10) is below the 80%-power MDE (12 on A, 11 on B, 15 on C), so TOST at the artifact level will rarely reject unless the point difference is near zero. Appendix B says optimizer-level equivalence is never claimed, but Sec. 5.2 states the orchestration claim as a statement about the modes. Sec. 4.4 applies the equivalence rule to all suites while App. B makes Suite C descriptive only. Table 5 CI half-widths on C are about 11, so even the 90% interval cannot sit inside +/-10.
Fix: State per suite whether equivalence can be concluded, restrict the orchestration claim to the three frozen artifacts, and say what happens to the "supported/equivalent/inconclusive/contradicted" verdict on C.

### 13. Table 7 denominators are not given and look inconsistent with the seed scores
Location: Table 7 and Sec. 5.3.
Problem: Failed-task counts are "summed over seeds and trials-pooled tasks" but the number of task-seed units is not stated. With 10 selection tasks on C and three seeds there are 30 units, yet C hill-climb shows 31 failed. With 25 selection tasks on B (75 units) a count of 58 to 63 failed implies a 77 to 84% failure rate against a 55% seed score on the test split. With A (40 tasks, 120 units, 54 to 59 failed) it is consistent. Table 7's caption defines four of the seven failure columns (unexpected side effect, infra, other undefined), and its labels (forgotten rule or step, tool misuse, output/contract, knowledge or rule gap) differ from Table 26's (missing_action, wrong_argument, no_output_file, wrong_result, communication, state_side_effect), so Sec. 5.3's statement "the surface-by-failure table is Table 26" cannot be read against Table 7. "Knowledge gap" is also just "output exists and fails the checks", which is not a knowledge diagnosis.
Fix: Add denominators, use one taxonomy, and rename the Suite A categories.

### 14. Notation clashes and undefined terms
- k: SE multiplier of the gate (Sec. 2.4, Algorithm 2), number of independent one-shot proposals ("best of k", Table 6), random-subset size and sentinel count k_s (App. F), "frontier of k programs" (Table 8).
- N: rollout budget (Sec. 4.3), N* matched checkpoint, pool size N and N_r in Eq. (2); B: budget (Algorithm 1), branch B and block B in Eq. (1) (the same symbol in the same equation), and Suite B.
- rho: coverage floor in Sec. 3/Algorithm 2 and Spearman correlation in Table 25.
- n, n_conf, n_0, "num_trials" vs "trials" (Suite B one-shot has both num_trials = 1 and trials = 2 in A.4).
- Delta: per-task difference vector (Algorithm 2), block gain (Eq. 1), selection change (Table 25), paired delta to seed (Table 4).
- "stock gate" vs "one-stage gate": Sec. 3 reports 0.58/0.52/0.55 for "the one-stage gate", Sec. 2.4 and the intro attribute the same behaviour to the stock "one-standard-error gate", but Suite D treats them as two different cells (Table 20/31: PT with one-stage gate vs the stock reference) and Table 8 says stock uses a footprint mask by default. Is the simulated one-stage gate masked or not? The footprint mask is described only in prose; the simulated numbers should say which they model.
- Algorithm 1 appears to use a_0 for both the seed artifact and the candidate (the extracted text shows a0 for both; confirm primes in the PDF), and Eq. (2) uses N undefined.
- "Footprint", "joint coverage", "protect set", "inert", "shrinkage" are used in the body before definition (inert appears in Sec. 5.2 and Table 23, defined only in a caption).
- Table 4 caption refers to "E12A" and "E12C", Table 23/27/29 to "A-E12" and "hcstock", Table 25 "ctl", Table 21 "rulep/onestage/pt/ctl": internal run codes not defined in the paper.

### 15. Fairness of the baseline comparison and late protocol changes
Location: Table 34, Table 19, Sec. 4.2 ("Eight of the nine competitors run from official repositories"), Sec. 7.
Problem: Every deviation in Table 34 is judged to lower or leave neutral the competitor (SkillOpt loses oracle feedback; Meta-Harness proposer SKILL.md "written by us", 4 iterations of 2 candidates instead of about 20; Trace2Skill LLM-only analyst; ACE batch class and one round instead of three; MIPROv2 reduced to one Predict; EvoSkill skills cannot run scripts; HarnessLens different runtime). "Official code" therefore means something weaker than the abstract and Sec. 4.2 imply. Table 19 shows roster, final sizes (22:30) and the matched-checkpoint rule (23:00) set after searches began (about 14:45), and the stock-gate arm added after search-time results were seen. These are disclosed, but the abstract and intro do not carry the caveat. The competitors also get 120 gating tasks vs CapEvolve's 40 on A "in a direction we cannot sign"; the direction for tasks seen is clearly in the competitor's favour for overfitting and against it for gate noise, and a robustness arm with 120 for CapEvolve would remove the question.
Fix: Reword "official code for eight of nine" to "official code with the deviations in Table 34", and add the equal-exposure CapEvolve arm if budget allows.

### 16. Adapter and disclosure gaps
- App. A.2 says the Suite B step cap (100 vs default 200) is "disclosed in every Suite B table"; it is not in Table 5, Table 22/33, Table 28 or Table 35 captions.
- Sec. 4.1 says the gold-reading gap is on "Suite B, agent-optimize" but the abstract claims gold isolation as a property without qualification; Table 2 still marks B "sealed*".
- Table 2 labels Suite D "sealed*" although its final set includes the 25 selection tasks that gate acceptance (Table 2 dagger and Sec. 4.4); only the 40 test tasks are sealed.
- Sec. 4.1 says tuned user-simulator/judge for D is gpt-4.1 while B/C use GPT-5.5 as judge; the cross-suite claims in Sec. 5.3 ("what a surface is worth") rest on D only, a different target model, so say what transfers.
- Sec. 4.4: Suite A's seed mean "moved by 8.8 points across six batches", with an MDE of 12; the interleaving mitigation is stated but no drift estimate within a window is given.
- Table 3 says "On Suite A every arm edits the same two prompt files" while the Table 8 / Sec. 6 description and Table 34 have EvoSkill and Trace2Skill writing skill folders (reduced here to prompt appendices). Say that the artifact class is forced to prompt text for those methods.

### 17. One-shot control is described inconsistently
Table 3 says "one unconditional step"; App. A.4 shows the control as hill-climb with max_iterations = 1 (a gate in the loop), and Tables 23/27 show it promoted 1 candidate in 3 searches and "inert" in 2 of 3 on both B and C. An unconditional step cannot return the seed. Table 20/31 (placeholder) lists "promoted per search 4.0" for a single-iteration arm. The Suite B one-shot also changes settings between seeds ("Settings differ between seeds for B/ctl-oneshot/s1, s2", trials = 2 vs 1). Fix: describe the control as gated or ungated, and ensure the Suite D cell definition is the same one.

## Questions for the authors

1. What exactly are the gating set sizes (A 40, B 25 or 49, C 10 or 20), and was Table 14 simulated at those sizes?
2. Is the "one-stage gate" in Sec. 3 / Table 14 identical to the shipped stock gate, with or without the footprint mask and the coverage rule (0.6 candidate-side, App. B)? If not, how do the 0.58/0.52/0.55 numbers bear on the shipped default?
3. For a null candidate, a one-sided test at k = 1 should pass about 16% of the time per candidate; over 6.7 to 9.4 screened candidates that implies 0.7 to 0.8 per search, but the table says 0.52 to 0.58. What in the gate (strict inequality, ties, footprint, coverage, baseline update after the first promotion) accounts for the difference?
4. Are the 70 Suite A final tasks inside the verified 400? Were gold-as-output and recomputation checks run on them?
5. Can inert seeds be given a pre-specified treatment in the primary Holm family (their paired delta to the seed is exactly 0, or noise from re-scoring)? How many of the 12/8/6 contrasts per suite will be about seed-versus-seed artifacts?
6. Why does SkillOpt exceed the 4M token cap by 19% and how is it handled at N*?
7. Why do B and C CapEvolve arms use 550 rollouts while the text and the GEPA/HarnessLens arms use 600?
8. What is the denominator of Table 7 per suite?
9. Is the history-off ablation still planned? If not, what supports contribution 3 beyond description?
10. How will the final paper reconcile the stock gate as the shipped default with the abstract's emphasis on the two-stage rule that the authors themselves describe as off by default?
