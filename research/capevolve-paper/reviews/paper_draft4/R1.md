# Review R1: Senior Researcher, Adjacent Area (AC level)

Venue: MLSys 2027. Draft stage: placeholders (red dagger values, mock figures) are not penalized. I judge whether the design, claims, comparisons and ablations would convince me once filled.

## Summary

CapEvolve is a loop that optimizes the editable files around a frozen agent (system prompt, tool code, MCP tool descriptions, skill packages). Its second idea is "code-owned evaluation": the optimizer cannot touch gold, the acceptance rule, or the final set. It offers a stock paired-SE gate and a conservative two-stage "rule P", two orchestration modes (deterministic hill-climb, or a coding agent calling propose/probe/promote verbs), and a ledger of which tasks each edit fixed and broke. It is evaluated on SpreadsheetBench and two tau2-bench domains against nine optimizers at a matched rollout checkpoint, with a retail ablation family (Suite D). The most memorable part is the authors' audit of their own earlier draft, where scorer compliance explained 43% and 80% of the reported gains.

## Score and confidence

Score: 4 of 10 as drafted (borderline reject). It could reach 6 or 7 if the results land as a well-measured method plus a credible negative or null finding about optimizer evaluation. Confidence: 3 of 5. I am adjacent: I know evaluation methodology and agent systems, not each competitor's code.

## Top 5 weaknesses, ranked

### 1. The paper does not yet say what it is: the central claim ("any editable surface") is mostly untested, and the one surface experiment lives in a different setting

Locations: title; Abstract; Sec 2.1 ("The design bet is that failures live on different surfaces"); Sec 2.1 footnote 42 ("MCP tool surface ... not evaluated"); Sec 5.2 "Causal history and intake"; Sec 7 Ablations bullet; Sec 8; Table 8 (CapEvolve marked Y for P, T, M, S).

The title and Table 8 claim four surfaces. The evaluation covers two (prompt, tools). Skill packages and MCP are explicitly unrun, and the conclusion admits the open question is "whether skill packages and MCP surfaces repay their edit space". So the surface-as-parameter idea, which is contribution 1, is demonstrated for a prompt versus prompt+tools contrast. Prior systems such as GEPA, DSPy and ADAS-style harness search already cover that. The evidence for "failures live on different surfaces" is a deterministic failure-type table (Table 7) computed on search-time selection data, and Sec 5.3 itself says attribution is "description" only. The only causal evidence is Suite D. Suite D uses a different target (gpt-4.1-mini on Azure), reuses Suite B's splits, is "never pooled", and is declared to establish "mechanism, not rank". It is also the only place the stock gate is tested against the surface. At Suite B's stated minimum detectable effect (11 points for artifact contrasts), a prompt versus prompt+tools gap is likely to come out inconclusive.

Fix: either (a) retitle and reframe to what is shown (a gated optimizer for prompt and tool code, with MCP and skills as implemented but unevaluated extensions) and remove "Any" from the title and the Y marks for M and S from Table 8's headline reading, or (b) run at least one skill-package cell on a main suite. Pre-commit in the text to the decision rule you will use if the prompt versus prompt+tools contrast is inconclusive: in that case the surface claim is dropped from the abstract, not softened.

### 2. The abstract and contributions advertise an ablation that was not run, and "causal history" is not causal

Locations: Abstract ("ablate the surface, the gate, the orchestration mode and the history"); Sec 1 contribution 3 and 5; Sec 2.4 end ("Section 5 tests whether this history matters by removing it", footnote 44); Sec 5.2 ("The history ablation ... were not approved"); Sec 7 Ablations bullet.

Three sections promise a history ablation. Sec 5.2 and Sec 7 say it was never run. This is a claim-evidence mismatch in the headline sentences. Separately, what the optimizer receives (Sec 2.4) is a ledger of per-task fixed/broke outcomes plus a protect set. That is an observational log of paired outcomes under a confounded multi-part edit. Calling it "causal" invites exactly the reading a systems reader will push back on, because an edit-level fix/break is not an attributable effect when edits are multi-part and noisy trials flip tasks. The related work admits ACE, SkillOpt, EvoSkill and DarwinX already keep per-edit records, and the paper cites no evidence that this one's variant helps.

Fix: either run the history-off switch on Suite D (the authors already describe it) or delete history from the abstract, contribution list and Sec 2.4 and demote it to an implementation detail. Rename "causal history" to "outcome ledger" unless a per-edit attribution analysis (for example single-edit replays) is added.

### 3. The safety story is only partly code-owned, and the shipped default is not the arm that carries the safety claim

Locations: Abstract; Sec 3 (para on gold isolation: "The library does not sandbox the proposer itself, so this is a deployment requirement"; "can detect tampering ... cannot stop a read"); Sec 2.2 ("the library does not enforce that"); Sec 4.1 ("on Suite B, agent-optimize can read gold actions of its own optimization and selection tasks"); Table 2 footnote; Sec 4.3 and Sec 5.1 (stock-gate row "added after search-time results were seen"); Sec 5.2 Gate paragraph; Table 6.

The title claim is code-owned evaluation. In practice gold isolation depends on OS-level deployment, was breached in one of the main arms (Suite B agent-optimize), Suite A's final set was "score-only exposed", Suite C is "development-exposed", and Suite B is "sealed" only by absence of observed access. Credentials leaked into two proposer environments (Ethics). Rule P, the arm that carries the false-promotion argument (0.04 to 0.11 versus 0.52 to 0.58), is opt-in and "off by default", whereas the shipped configuration is the stock gate, which is the one the paper itself shows promotes noise. The stock-gate row was added post hoc after the authors saw results. Rule P also trades 0.53/0.49/0.36 power for 0.40/0.33/0.17 at an 8-point gain, and in search-time data it returned the seed in 8 of 9 searches. So the design offers "safe but inert" versus "default but noisy", and the paper's own title argument supports shipping the arm that does not win on power.

Fix: state in the abstract which guarantees are enforced by code and which by deployment, in a two-column table (enforced / assumed / violated in our runs). Make rule P the default or justify why not. Report the stock-gate arm as the pre-registered primary on every suite where it ran, or label the whole comparison honestly as exploratory. Add a Rule P-vs-stock power curve over effect size so readers can choose.

### 4. Power and design make the main comparison unable to support the ranking story

Locations: Sec 4.3 and 4.4 (seeds, df, MDE); Sec 5.1; Table 4 and Table 5 structure; Table 2; Sec 7 Power and Exposure bullets; Sec 4.1 ("it sees 40 tasks where competitors see 120").

Three search seeds give 2 degrees of freedom for method-level statements, and the authors concede that method claims need MDEs of 23 to 29 points. Artifact-level MDEs are 11 to 15 points. The placeholder deltas shown (3 to 7 points) are in the range such a design cannot resolve, so the likely honest outcome of the headline table is "all inconclusive". Final sets are 70 single-trial tasks (A), 40 tasks on B, and 20 tasks on C, and C is already development-exposed, so it cannot rank anything. N* is chosen from the minimum rollouts every seed reaches by a wall-clock cutoff (2026-10-13), which means the common budget is set by the slowest arm's infrastructure, not by a design choice, and is described as 250 to 400 rollouts against a nominal 600. CapEvolve sees 40 tasks, competitors 120, in "a direction we cannot sign". Every competitor but one runs on a single weak target model (Gemma or gpt-oss), with the same Sonnet optimizer, authors tuning their own method and running the baselines (Sec 7 first bullet). Section 5.1 says the primary family also differs per suite (12, 8, 6 contrasts), so the Holm burden varies.

Fix: (a) say in the introduction, not just Sec 7, that the study is powered for artifact-level differences of 11 to 15 points and what outcome would count as a win; (b) add a pilot-free power justification for the sample sizes, or enlarge the final sets (Suite A has 239 eligible ids and uses 70); (c) pre-register what the paper will claim if, as the MDE suggests, no pairwise contrast clears Holm: the contribution then becomes the measurement framework and the audit; (d) include a stronger target model on one suite to show the effect does not vanish as the base model improves; (e) equalize the exposure (give a competitor arm the 40-task variant, or CapEvolve 120).

### 5. Framing, audience fit and accessibility: the paper reads as two or three papers and is hard to follow from outside

Locations: Abstract; Sec 1 paragraph 1 to 2; Sec 2.3 to 3 (stock gate, rule P, footprint mask, nconf, zconf, rho); Sec 4 (Suite A to D, E10, E12A, Table 31 in the appendix); Sec 6; the self-audit at Sec 1 and Sec 3 end.

An adjacent reader gets three storylines: a multi-surface optimizer, a statistical acceptance rule, and a post-mortem on how earlier results were inflated. Each is interesting. The post-mortem (33.0 and 49.1 point gains falling mostly to scorer compliance, test set scored during development, gold read by optimizer sessions) is arguably the most valuable and most transferable contribution, yet it is one sentence in the introduction and an appendix. The gate is positioned against SAGE, PACE and BudgetAPO, which the paper itself says "contribute the statistics", leaving rule P as a simple confirmation-rerun with no guarantee. Novelty over concurrent work then reduces to "the combination" (Sec 7). Jargon (stock gate, rule P, footprint, champion, E-codes, nconf, zconf, rho, "family-clustered") is introduced in passing and used in tables the reader cannot decode without Appendix J. For an MLSys audience the systems content is thin: there is no throughput, latency, cost-per-point or scheduling analysis in the main text beyond "12 rollouts per hour per search" and a total of "$84"; intake cost is an open EVIDENCE slot. Sentences that state outcomes as if known today and should be flagged as placeholder-outcome statements: Sec 5.2 Orchestration ("agent-optimize ended its own search early in several seeds on Suites B and C") and the Sec 7 Gates bullet ("Rule P ... promotes almost nothing"), both of which pre-judge the final-evaluation reading; the Figure 3 mock annotations (2.3, 1.3, 4.3 "promoted per search") also should not survive into the text as findings.

Fix: pick a lead claim. My recommendation is the evaluation-integrity framing (code-owned evaluation, the audit, null-edit false-accept rates, injected faults), with the surface-as-parameter loop as the vehicle. Move the audit to a main-text subsection with a table (original gain, recomputed gain, what changed). Add a one-page notation and arm glossary, make Table 3 and Table 8 self-contained, and add the systems measurements MLSys readers expect (rollout throughput, wall-clock per accepted point, intake time per benchmark). Remove or rewrite the two outcome-presupposing sentences so each states only the pre-specified claim until the data exist.

## Smaller remarks

- Related work asserts "in the papers we read, none reports editing the descriptions an MCP server exposes". This is a negative claim on a surface that you did not evaluate; it supports novelty of something you do not test.
- Sec 6 says DarwinX and DGM have a similar candidate DAG; Table 8 shows DarwinX covers P, T, M, S as well. State plainly what CapEvolve does that DarwinX does not besides the gate and seal.
- The 0.52 to 0.58 false-promotion rate is a simulation under a null pool at real task counts; it should be validated on the empirical null-edit runs before it is quoted in the abstract-level argument (the E04 slot).
- The optimizer is Sonnet 5.5 for every arm and is also priced at list; competitors designed around weaker proposers may be disadvantaged. Say so.

## Questions to the authors

1. If the Suite D contrast of prompt+tools over prompt-only is inconclusive at the 10-point margin, what remains of contribution 1?
2. Why is rule P not the default? Is there any configuration where the stock gate beats rule P on held-out reliability, and what would you conclude from that?
3. How much of the competitor shortfall (if any) is from running them at 600-rollout-capped, truncated schedules that their authors never tuned (SkillOpt cosine schedule, EvoSkill iteration cap)? The "unmatched" secondary analysis should be promoted if so.
4. For Suite B agent-optimize with readable gold actions: how are these results reported next to arms with a working isolation, and can the conclusions be drawn without that arm?
5. The audit: did the earlier draft's headline numbers come from the same code as this paper? Does the inflation decomposition (43%, 80%) get reproduced by the current scorer and sealed test, and what would the old pipeline report today?
6. What are the optimizer token and rollout costs per accepted candidate, and does the confirmation stage's 2 x |Dsel| x nconf rollouts change the ranking at equal cost?
7. Does anything change with a stronger frozen target (for example a frontier model) where most failures are not "forgotten steps"?

## What would change my score

Up to 6 or 7:
- The history ablation is run or removed from the headline claims, and the abstract is limited to evaluated surfaces.
- A clear decomposition of the audit (original versus recomputed gains) is promoted to the main text, with the false-accept and fault-injection results showing the gate and seal catch what they claim.
- Suite D shows a surface contrast beyond the 10-point margin, or the paper cleanly reports it as inconclusive and drops the surface-width conclusion.
- Either enlarged final sets or a reframed claim that matches the 11 to 15 point resolution, with equalized exposure between CapEvolve and competitors.
- A skill-package or MCP cell on a main suite, or a rescoped title.
- Systems measurements (cost per point, throughput, intake time) in the main text.

Down to 3:
- The final tables show no pairwise contrast clearing Holm, yet the abstract still leads with a gain over seed, or the stock gate remains the post-hoc primary.
- Suite B isolation breach arm is pooled into headline comparisons.
- The abstract keeps "ablate ... the history" and "Any Editable Surface" unchanged.
