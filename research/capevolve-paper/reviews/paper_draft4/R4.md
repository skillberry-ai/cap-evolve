# R4: Methodology and experimental-design review (adversarial)

Paper: "CapEvolve: Optimizing Any Editable Surface of a Frozen Agent Under Code-Owned Evaluation" (MLSys 2027 draft 4).
Reviewer role: statistics and experimental design. Read from paper.txt only; no earlier reviews or campaign notes used.
Placeholder numbers (dagger) are treated as placeholders. I judge the design and the sentences, not the dummy values, except where a dummy value exposes a structural defect.

## Bottom line

The paper is unusually candid about exposure, post-hoc analyses and search-time labels. That honesty is its strongest asset and the reason most problems below are fixable by rewording rather than new data. But as designed, (a) the confirmatory machinery is powered only for effects 2 to 4 times larger than any gain the paper's own search-time data suggest, (b) the equivalence tests cannot succeed in the cases the paper wants them for, (c) the "headline" arm was chosen after seeing pre-registered arms fail, (d) one ablation factor is bundled, one is absent, and (e) the key Suite D ablation scores tasks that gated acceptance. Expect most final verdicts to be "inconclusive", and rewrite the abstract and contributions now so they do not promise more than that.

## Ranked problems

### 1. Suite D's final set contains the selection tasks (selection-vs-test leakage)
- Location: Section 4.4 ("The final set is the 25 selection tasks plus the 40 sealed test tasks, five trials per task"); Table 2 footnote dagger and the Suite D row ("65 x 5", "sealed*"); Sections 5.2 and 5.3; Tables 20, 21, 31, 32.
- Why it matters: 25 of 65 final tasks (38%) gated acceptance and fed the proposer. Gains on them are selection-optimistic, and the optimism is largest for exactly the arms the ablation compares: stock gate and one-stage gate (high promotion counts) versus rule P (near inert). The gate contrast is therefore biased toward the permissive gates, the opposite of what the sealed-final design is meant to guarantee. Shrinkage cannot be read from Suite D either. Suite D is also labelled "sealed" in Table 2 although 38% of it is not. Every claim about what a surface or a gate "is worth" (Sections 5.2, 5.3, the abstract's "what each surface is worth") rests on this family.
- Fix: score every Suite D contrast on the 40 sealed tasks only (report the 65-task number as exploratory), or drop the 25 selection tasks from the final set. Re-run the power calculation at 40 tasks x 5 trials. Remove "sealed" from the Suite D row or qualify it.

### 2. The headline arm was chosen after the pre-registered arms looked inert; the headline is not named
- Location: Section 4.3 ("A stock-gate secondary family ... added after we saw search-time selection results"); Appendix B ("Amendment 6 ... written before any final-set data but after we had seen search-time selection-split results, which showed rule P nearly inert and the stock gate promoting"); Table 4/5 "CapEvolve as shipped, stock gate (section sign)"; Table 19 (14:45 pilot stopped, later reversed); abstract and Section 1 [RESULT: headline gain over the seed artifact].
- Why it matters: this is a textbook forking path. The registered CapEvolve arms (rule P, HC and AO) returned the seed artifact in 2/3, 3/3, 3/3 (HC) and 9/9 (AO) searches at search time (Section 5.2, Tables 23, 27). The only arm likely to show a positive final-set delta is the arm added afterwards, which is also the selection-optimistic arm. If the abstract headline is "CapEvolve +X", a reader will assume the configuration was fixed in advance. Suite B has no stock-gate arm at all, so on the one sealed main suite the headline can only come from the inert arms. There are nine candidate headlines (3 configurations x 3 suites) and no pre-declared single choice.
- Fix: before any final score is computed, commit a dated one-line statement naming the headline estimand (arm, suite, contrast) for the abstract. If the headline is the stock gate, say in the abstract that it was added after search-time results and sits outside the primary family. Report all three configurations on every suite where they exist. Do not describe stock-gate rows as "primary family" in the Table 4/5 captions (they are in S-stock).

### 3. The equivalence (TOST) machinery cannot deliver the claims it is used for
- Location: Section 4.4 ("margin of 10 points"); Appendix B (margin "about the planned conditional MDE of 0.106"; "pipeline-level 90% half-width ... about 0.2 ... equivalence of the optimizer is never claimed"); Section 5.2 Orchestration ("The claim is that they reach equivalent final gain"); Table 20/31 caption ("pre-registered equivalence margin of 10 points"); the [EVIDENCE NEEDED 9, 12] slots "level with (TOST)".
- Why it matters, four separate defects:
  a. Margin equals the MDE. A margin chosen so that a 10-point difference is only just detectable is not a smallest effect of interest. It is larger than every gain in Table 4/5 (3 to 6 points), so a 10-point margin would call the best arm "equivalent" to the seed. The word "equivalent" would carry almost no information.
  b. Power. With the paper's own implied artifact-level SE (MDE 12/2.8 = 4.3 on A; 11 gives 3.9 on B; 15 gives 5.4 on C), TOST at margin 10, alpha 0.05 has power about 0.50 (A), 0.64 (B), 0.16 (C) when the true difference is exactly zero, and less before the Holm step over the per-suite TOST family. For two different arms (CapEvolve versus a competitor, no seed double-scoring) the SE is larger still. "Level with the best competitor" is therefore unlikely to be demonstrable; "inconclusive" is the expected outcome.
  c. Level mismatch. Section 5.2's Orchestration claim ("hill-climb and agent-optimize reach equivalent final gain") is a statement about the two methods. Appendix B states equivalence can only be tested at artifact level and is "never claimed" for the optimizer. The sentence contradicts the analysis plan.
  d. Vacuous equivalence from inertness. Search-time data show HC and AO returning the seed artifact in 3/3 searches on B and C (Tables 23, 27). Two arms that both return the seed are "equivalent" trivially; this says the gate is inert, not that the orchestration modes match.
- Fix: set the margin from a smallest effect of interest argued on substantive grounds (for example 5 points or half the seed-to-best-competitor gap), not from the MDE, and state the TOST power at the realised SE. State equivalence claims only for frozen artifacts and exclude seed-identical artifacts from them (or report them separately). Rewrite the Orchestration claim to "the three frozen HC and AO artifacts are / are not distinguishable at 10 points".

### 4. Inert searches inject pure noise into the delta, and shrinkage is mechanically confounded
- Location: Section 4.4 (finalization "scores the returned candidate and the seed on the same final tasks exactly once"); Tables 4, 35 versus Tables 23, 27 (HC rule P on A: seeds 0 and 2 inert, yet per-seed deltas +1.5 and +2.2); Appendix B shrinkage paragraph ("Seeds that return the seed artifact contribute 0"); Table 28 note ("seeds with no positive gain are left out of the per-point means").
- Why it matters:
  a. If the returned artifact is byte-identical to the seed but is scored as a separate arm in the final window, its "gain" is a difference of two independent noisy measurements (within-task variance 0.088 to 0.148). The artifact-level mean over three seeds then contains noise presented as method gain. Alternatively, if the paper imputes delta = 0 by identity, say so; then the cluster-robust interval is not computed on the same footing as the other arms.
  b. Shrinkage is defined as 0 for inert seeds. Rule P arms, which are mostly inert, will therefore look low-shrinkage by construction; stock gate arms, which promote, will look high. Shrinkage then measures promotion rate, not selection quality. The planned test "each arm against zero shrinkage" mixes these.
  c. Leaving seeds with non-positive gain out of USD-per-point means (Table 28 note) is survivorship selection on the outcome, and flatters every arm with failed searches.
- Fix: decide and state the inert-seed rule for the final delta (identity, imputed 0, with the noisy re-score reported as a measurement check). Report shrinkage only over non-inert seeds, with the inert fraction beside it, or report the promoted-candidate shrinkage. Report cost per point as total cost over total gain (ratio of means) with an interval, not a mean of per-seed ratios over positive seeds.

### 5. Power at the stated n: the study is powered for effects far larger than any plausible gain
- Location: Section 4.4 and Appendix B power paragraph; Limitations "Power"; Tables 4, 5.
- Why it matters: see the Power check below. Detection probability for a true 5-point gain is roughly 20% (A, unadjusted) and about 4% at the Holm first step; every pipeline-level (method) claim needs 23 to 29 points before Holm. The text "We claim at the artifact level and call smaller gaps inconclusive" is honest, but the abstract, the five contributions and the Section 1 [RESULT] slots are written as if comparative claims will be available.
- Fix: put the MDE next to the expected effect in the Setup, state in the abstract that comparisons are on three frozen artifacts, and reframe contribution 5 as "a controlled protocol and descriptive estimates with intervals". Consider adding seeds on the single most important contrast (HC or stock gate versus seed on Suite A and B) rather than spreading budget over 12 contrasts.

### 6. Clustering is specified three different ways, and the cluster count may be tiny
- Location: Section 4.4 ("The unit of inference is the task"); Section 5.1 ("intervals are family-cluster-robust"); Appendix B ("cluster-robust, t with T - 1 degrees of freedom"); Figure 8 (six families F1 to F6, n = 31, 24, 21, 17, 15, 12, summing to 120, not 70); Table 2 note on "families".
- Why it matters: if the cluster is the task family and there are about six families, cluster-robust SEs from a sandwich estimator are badly biased and the t interval needs about 5 df (critical value 2.57 versus 1.99), so all Suite A intervals widen by roughly 30 to 70%. If the cluster is the task, the family language is wrong. Figure 8's family counts add to 120 (the 80 + 40 development tasks), not to the 70-task final set, so it is unclear which tasks the families describe. For tau2 suites no family definition is given. Suite C has 20 tasks, where task-clustered inference with 19 df is fragile in any case. Trials within task are averaged (fine), but multiple search-seed artifacts on the same task are not independent; say how they are combined.
- Fix: define the cluster unit per suite, report the number of clusters in the final set, use a small-sample correction (CR2 / Bell-McCaffrey or wild cluster bootstrap) when clusters are fewer than about 30, and confirm Figure 8 uses the 70 final tasks.

### 7. Missingness and infrastructure failures are handled asymmetrically, and the primary rule can reward or punish arms
- Location: Section 4.4 (failed cell scores 0 for every arm; sensitivity treats as missing); Appendices A.2 and C; Tables 4, 5, 15, 35.
- Why it matters:
  a. Missing counts are not small relative to effects: up to 9/210 (4.3%) in one arm versus 0/210 in another (Table 4); 9/180 (5%) for the Suite C one-shot control. At 40 to 50% accuracy, scoring 4% of cells as 0 shifts the mean by about 2 points, comparable to the claimed gains. The primary analysis therefore ranks arms partly by infrastructure luck. The pessimistic and optimistic bounds in Table 35 already straddle zero for AO on Suite A and for the one-shot control on C (and span 8 points for several arms), yet nothing in the win rule consults them.
  b. The paper itself reports that infrastructure labels can be caused by the artifact (Meta-Harness sandbox error 17% versus 5%), so zero-versus-missing is informative missingness, not ignorable.
  c. "Handled by one rule for every arm" (Section 3 and 4.4) conflicts with Appendix A.2 (Suite B: GEPA and controls retry inside the budget, CapEvolve leaves a missing cell without retry, HarnessLens uses 3 h with one retry) and Appendix C (competitors scored infra errors as 0 in search feedback while CapEvolve dropped them; the text itself says this "may favour CapEvolve"). These hit search-time decisions that cannot be repaired post hoc. The text says the effect is labelled, not bounded.
  d. ACE was relaunched at 00:59 on 12 October on a fixed library; MIPROv2, Meta-Harness, GEPA, EvoSkill, SkillOpt kept earlier handling. Arms did not receive the same measurement protocol, and the authors "did not check whether the ACE reflector or the MIPROv2 bootstrap showed the error text".
  e. Eight tau2 rows are counted as infrastructure because a simulated-user fault is "a frozen environment component". That is a decision made after seeing the rows; the sensitivity flip is a good guard, but state which result it must preserve.
- Fix: make a win require both the primary result and the pessimistic-bound result (or report both columns in Tables 4/5 and phrase wins as "robust" only when both pass). Report the per-arm infra rate with the Wilson interval next to every delta. Where handling differed in search, state it in the Table 4/5 captions, not only in Appendix C. Replace "one rule for every arm" by "one rule at final scoring; search-time handling differed (Appendix C)".

### 8. Pre-registration and amendment disclosure is incomplete and unverifiable
- Location: Section 4.4 ("The family is registered ... before launch"); Appendix B ("The analysis plan fixed the following before the search cutoff ... commit 029335d8c"; "Amendment 6"; "amendment 5O"); Appendix I and Table 19 (changes through 23:00; "entries after the searches began (about 14:45) could have seen partial search progress").
- Why it matters:
  a. "Registered" refers to a commit in the authors' own repository. There is no external timestamp, and for an anonymised submission reviewers cannot check it. The plan is fixed "before the search cutoff", not before the first outcome was visible.
  b. Amendments are numbered at least 6 and 5O, but only #6 and #5O are described. Amendments 1 to 5 are not listed with a before/after-data status. Table 19 lists roster and parameter changes but does not map them to amendments.
  c. Several consequential choices fall after the searches began at about 14:45: AO engine switch (15:30), Suite C added (17:40), EvoSkill restart (18:55), final sizes pinned A 70x1, B 40x3, C 20x3 (22:30), and the N* computation restricted to primary-family arms (23:00). The last two are justified by "capacity and power" and "slow arms would otherwise bind it", which are outcome-blind in intent but made with partial search progress visible. The first Suite B run (09:40 to 12:38) and the stock-gate AO pilot (stopped 14:45) produced results that were then "superseded, not used": say who saw them.
  d. Suite D "registered before launch" has no date and Suite D reuses Suite B's splits; if Suite B finished first, the Suite D contrasts could have been informed by it.
  e. How the 70 final tasks were chosen out of the 239 sampled ids is not given; "final sizes pinned" at 22:30 after the sample was drawn at 11:35.
- Fix: add a table "Amendment, date/time, relation to first search outcome visible (before/after), what changed" covering every amendment, and state the selection rule for the 70. Replace "registered" by "specified in a time-stamped repository commit (hash, date)" and give the date in the camera-ready (a hash-timestamped public archive if allowed).

### 9. Matched-checkpoint rule: data-dependent, unmatched in several respects, and its operating characteristics are not those that were simulated
- Location: Section 4.3; Appendix B simulation paragraph; Tables 14, 30; Section 5.1.
- Why it matters:
  a. N* is the minimum over primary-family seeds of progress made by a wall-clock cutoff, so it depends on infrastructure speed (gateway priority, a slower arm restarted from scratch). It is computed from counters only, which is a good rule, but it makes the comparison point arbitrary, and the paper's own Figure 6 curves are for arms that differ in rollouts per candidate.
  b. Rule P was tuned to 600/550 budgets with about ten candidates per search (6.7 to 9.4 screened). At N* = 400, 350, 250 only about 4 to 6 candidates are screened, and one confirmation costs 240 rollouts on Suite A, so the false-promotion and power figures in Table 14 and the abstract-level claim ("0.068, 0.040, 0.113") do not apply at the checkpoint where it is evaluated.
  c. "Compared at the smaller of N* and their own count" gives cheap arms (Trace2Skill about 200, MIPROv2 about 400) fewer rollouts than main arms; the label "matched" overstates the control. The comparison is explicitly not compute matched (AO uses 72% of the optimizer token cap, HC 43%).
  d. Candidates whose evaluation straddles N* are excluded, and "what its own selection rule would have deployed at N*" must be reconstructed for arms whose rule is internal (GEPA Pareto state, Meta-Harness frontier). The reconstruction is not specified per arm.
  e. Floor of 200: say what happens if any primary seed is below 200 at cutoff.
- Fix: recompute Table 14 operating characteristics at the realised N* and candidate counts. Give a one-line reconstruction rule per arm. Report, beside N*, the number of candidates screened by each arm at N*.

### 10. Multiplicity control is local, and the interval and test do not agree
- Location: Section 4.4; Appendix B; Tables 4, 5 (ACE interval [+0.1, +11.1] with pHolm 0.290).
- Why it matters:
  a. Holm is applied within families of 12, 8 and 6 per suite, and again within S-stock (6 and 2), the second unmatched family (12, 8, 6), TOST families, shrinkage families, and Suite D (10 + 4). That is roughly 80 or more tests with no control across families or suites, and the abstract needs one statement across three suites. A reader who sees "wins on any suite" has a family-wise error far above 5%.
  b. A win requires "Holm p < 0.05 and an interval that excludes zero", but the interval is unadjusted. Table 4's ACE row has an interval excluding zero and pHolm 0.29. The two criteria disagree by design, so the second is redundant at best and misleading at worst.
  c. Primary-family membership is odd: CapEvolve-versus-competitor contrasts are primary, but only five of eight Suite A competitors are in it; the abstract says "nine competitors" while the primary family contains five (A), two (B), one (C), and HarnessLens runs only on B.
- Fix: report Holm-adjusted (simultaneous) intervals for the primary family, state a hierarchical gatekeeping order across suites (for example A first, B only if A passes) or report per-suite results explicitly as separate hypotheses, and change "nine competitors" to the competitors actually in each family.

### 11. Ablations do not isolate each claimed contribution
- Location: Sections 2.1, 2.4, 5.2, contributions 3 and 5, abstract ("ablate the surface, the gate, the orchestration mode and the history"); Section 7 (history, descriptions-only, skill package "not run"); Table 18.
- Why it matters:
  a. History: contribution 3 and the abstract claim an ablation of the causal history; Section 2.4 says "Section 5 tests whether this history matters by removing it"; Section 5.2 and Limitations say it was not approved or run. The text is inconsistent and the contribution is unevidenced.
  b. Orchestration mode is not a single factor. HC versus AO differ in controller, proposer input, parent selection (incumbent versus DAG), screen design, accept rule (footprint-masked one-SE versus z-test with SE floor, both overridden by rule P), stopping rule, max turns (80 versus 300 on B), and Suite B gold visibility (Appendix C: AO could read gold actions of its own optimization and selection tasks; "never view test" is prose). Any HC-versus-AO difference is a bundle, and on Suite B it is confounded with gold access, which contradicts the paper's gold-isolation thesis.
  c. Gate: stock gate differs from rule P in more than "two-stage": stock uses footprint masking, candidate-side coverage 0.6 and one stage; rule P has masking off, coverage 0.95, confirmation baseline reset, and spends 2|Dsel|n_conf rollouts per screened candidate (fewer candidates for the same budget). The "one-stage gate" cell is the right isolating cell only if it matches rule P in masking, coverage and baseline handling; the paper does not say so.
  d. Surface: tool-only versus prompt-only cells also differ in the volume and type of edits permitted; the claim "prompt and tools beats prompt only on tasks whose failures need deterministic enforcement" is a subgroup claim (failure-type subgroup defined from search-time labels), with no pre-specified subgroup, no interaction test and no power. Suite D's target (gpt-4.1-mini) differs from the main suites, and the paper itself says D "establishes mechanism, not rank".
  e. Code-owned evaluation (contribution 2) has no ablation in this paper. Appendix E is a retrospective audit of an earlier system with a different optimizer (Opus 5), exposed test sets and single runs ("rank nothing"). It motivates the design but does not show the current system's gains are uninflated.
  f. Rule P's operating characteristics are called "measured" in contribution 2, but they come from a simulation whose edit mix (60% null, 25% positive, 15% negative) and independence between selection and new-task noise are assumptions. Rule P was selected against that simulator, so the reported 0.068 is in-sample to it, and the simulator does not model adaptivity of the proposer to the selection tasks (which Section 3 admits is uncontrolled). The null-edit empirical rates are [EVIDENCE NEEDED].
- Fix: delete the history from the abstract and contributions unless it is run. Describe orchestration as "two shipped configurations", not a one-factor ablation. State the full set of differences between gate cells and confirm the one-stage cell holds masking and coverage at rule P's values. Replace "measured" with "simulated" for operating characteristics, or run the empirical null pool before submission.

### 12. Control arms do not support "the loop beats sampling" on any unexposed suite
- Location: Sections 4.2, 7 (only compute-matched control is best-of-k on Suite C); Table 5/6; related work (Wang et al. 2026b; Chang and Chen 2026; Singh et al. 2026).
- Why it matters: the paper itself cites evidence that simple test-time scaling or single-pass coding-agent optimization can match harness evolution. The only compute-matched control is exploratory, outside Holm, and on the development-exposed Suite C. The one-shot repair is far cheaper than the main arms (about 10 to 200 rollouts, one optimizer pass) and so cannot separate "iteration helps" from "more optimizer compute helps". Any placeholder showing CapEvolve above the one-shot control would not support "the loop matters".
- Fix: add best-of-k to Suites A and B (or one of them) as a pre-specified, Holm-included contrast, or soften all loop-value wording to "relative to a cheap one-pass control".

### 13. Test-set exposure: statements are honest but some conclusions run past the evidence
- Location: Table 2 (A "score-only exposed", B "sealed*", C "development-exposed"); Section 4.1; Section 7 Exposure; Appendix C and E.
- Why it matters:
  a. Suite A's final set was scored at most twice before. The authors have seen those scores and, in an earlier system, a GPT-5.5 session read a sealed-test evaluation and "distilled rules appear in champion prompts". The paper should state that no such text reaches the current seed, skills, memories or proposer prompts. The seed artifact's provenance (prompt.md, task_template.md) is given only by hash.
  b. The Suite A final set comes from the 912-question release outside the verified-400 subset, but the scorer audit (19 of 400 gold files fail, 4 unscorable, ceiling 98.6%) is measured on verified-400 only. The unverified remainder may have a higher gold-error rate, and the final-set scorer validity is unmeasured.
  c. "Sealed*": Suite B's test files were visible on the runner filesystem; the scan of 68 transcripts is "absence of evidence"; the seal blocks scoring and edits, not reads (Section 3: hooks "deny edits"; manifest "cannot stop a read"). The abstract's "a seal on the final set" overstates this.
  d. Suite C is development-exposed yet carries Holm-adjusted p-values, a primary family, a stock-gate headline row (+7.4, the largest in Table 5) and tables with bold structure; "arms are not ranked on it" conflicts with the presentation. 
  e. Suite D shares Suite B's 40 test tasks (different target); that is acceptable if D's design is time-stamped before B's results, which is not shown.
  f. Gold-isolation is the core claim, yet on Suite B the AO arm could read gold actions of its own tasks, and "the arm was moved into a mount-namespace shim at 15:00" after searches began (about 14:45). Say which AO sessions pre-date the shim and whether they are in the final analysis.
- Fix: add one sentence on provenance of the seed and proposer inputs; rerun the scorer self-check on the Suite A final ids; replace "sealed" in the abstract with "scored once"; drop Holm columns and stars for Suite C or label the whole block exploratory; list which Suite B AO sessions ran before the shim.

### 14. Drift and the final-window design
- Location: Section 4.4 ("Suite A's seed-artifact mean moved by 8.8 points across six batches"); Appendix B ("A one-hour block sensitivity analysis applies a two-standard-error drift rule").
- Why it matters: an 8.8-point seed drift is larger than every claimed gain. Interleaving is the right fix, but the plan does not say at what level (task, arm-by-task, block), whether block effects are in the model, or what the drift rule does when triggered (exclude the block? re-score?). A rule triggered after seeing results is a researcher degree of freedom.
- Fix: state the interleaving unit and a block fixed effect in the primary model; make the drift rule report-only (sensitivity), not an exclusion.

### 15. Search-time evidence used where only held-out evidence should be (risk list)
The paper labels search-time evidence carefully. These are the places where the planned conclusion may still lean on it:
- Section 5.3 and Table 7/26: failure-type attribution ("most failed seed tasks are a forgotten required step or a tool call with wrong arguments, the kind of failure tool code can enforce"). This motivates the "design bet" in Section 2.1 and feeds [EVIDENCE NEEDED 16] ("decision guide ... each rule traceable to a Suite D or attribution table cell"). Table 26's "fixed" counts include noise flips (caption says so). A decision guide built from them would be search-time evidence presented as guidance. Restrict the guide to Suite D sealed-40 cells and say attribution is hypothesis-generating.
- Section 5.2 Gate: "rule P hill-climb returned the seed artifact in 2 of 3 searches ... the verdict stays open" is correctly labelled, but Table 6 (seed columns, promotion counts) is not labelled final or search-time and its placeholder disagrees with Table 23 (Suite A rule P: 3.0 promoted, 0 of 3 returned = seed, versus 0.33 promoted and 2 inert). Label each column.
- Table 24 "Fresh delta per promotion" is called "least biased" but rests on one promotion on one suite.
- Section 1's "0.52 to 0.58" and "a no-op in a large share" are simulation figures (stress mix, assumed), not observations; the phrasing "Under trial noise a one-standard-error gate promotes a no-op candidate in 0.52 to 0.58 of ten-candidate searches" reads as empirical.
- Fix: label every column of Tables 6 and 24 as search-time or final; reword the Section 1 sentence to "in simulation".

### 16. Statistical details to resolve
- The shared SE helper double counts within-task noise (variance 1.33x at three trials, 1.6 to 8.6x for fixed-task estimands). Appendix E says "we do not report" the replay that depends on it, while Rule P's confirmation z (1.282, n_conf = 3) uses this helper. Say plainly that the rule's nominal z is effectively about 1.1, which is what the simulation measured, and that the final-set intervals do not use the helper.
- Power check mismatch (see below): my reconstruction gives 13 to 15 points on Suite A for the stated variances, not 12.
- Table 4/5 intervals on A are narrower than the power section implies (half-width about 6.5 implies SE about 3.3 versus 4.3 assumed); expect the placeholder to be replaced, but check consistency.
- Judge validity: GPT-5.5 grades natural-language assertions on B and C in place of the benchmark's default judge. No agreement audit against the default judge or humans is reported, so the absolute scale is not comparable to published tau2 numbers and the proposer sees judge-specific feedback text.
- Competitor handicaps (Table 34: Meta-Harness four iterations of two candidates instead of about twenty, Sonnet instead of Opus, SkillOpt without oracle feedback, Trace2Skill's weaker analyst) are mostly in the direction that lowers the competitor and the "Direction" column is the authors' own reading. "Against nine published optimizers" should read "against adapted versions of nine".

## Power check (reconstructed)

Assumptions: the paper's own MDE (80% power, alpha 0.05 two-sided, before Holm): A 12, B 11, C 15; implied SE = MDE / 2.8 = 4.3, 3.9, 5.4 points. Normal approximation. Holm first step alpha divides by the family size (12, 8, 6).

| Suite | True gain | Power, unadjusted | Power at Holm first step |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (70 x 1) | 3 pts | 0.10 | 0.02 |
| A | 5 pts | 0.21 | 0.04 |
| A | 6 pts | 0.29 | 0.07 |
| B (40 x 3) | 3.5 pts | 0.14 | 0.03 |
| B | 5.7 pts | 0.31 | 0.10 |
| C (20 x 3) | 3 pts | 0.08 | 0.02 |
| C | 7.4 pts | 0.28 | 0.10 |

Reading: the planned tests have roughly 4 to 10% chance of declaring a true 5 to 6 point gain a "win" at the Holm first step, and about 20 to 30% unadjusted. Method-level (pipeline) claims with t on 2 df need 23 to 29 points before Holm (critical t 4.30 versus 1.96). The Holm-adjusted first-step values the paper reports (16, 21, 28) agree in order of magnitude.

TOST at margin 10 with true difference 0: power 0.50 (SE 4.3), 0.64 (SE 3.9), 0.16 (SE 5.4); the Holm family and the larger SE of CapEvolve-versus-competitor contrasts reduce this further. Equivalence claims at the stated margin are close to coin flips even when true.

Suite D (65 x 5, gpt-4.1-mini), my estimate with trial variance 0.148, task-effect spread 0.15: SE of a two-artifact contrast about 3.5 points, MDE about 10 before Holm and about 13 at the first step of the 10-contrast family. The Table 31 placeholder intervals (half-width about 12 to 14) are what a t distribution on 2 df gives with this SE; if Suite D really uses 2 df, the Holm-adjusted first-step critical value is 9.9, so a "supported" verdict would need a difference above about 30 points, and "equivalent" is unreachable (90% half-width about 9.6 points plus |difference| must stay under 10). State the df used for Suite D. Restricting to the sealed-40 tasks (problem 1) raises the SE to about 4.4 and the MDE to about 12.

Variance reconstruction: using the paper's within-task variance 0.088 on A, a task-effect spread of 0.15, and one trial per arm, I get an MDE of 14.9 for two arms, 13.2 for an arm against a twice-scored seed, and 14.0 as the noise-only floor, against the stated 12, 12 and 10. Either the paper averages over the three artifacts, or the stated MDE is optimistic by 10 to 25%. Please show the formula.

## Verdict on whether the design supports each headline claim

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| CapEvolve (some configuration) improves on the seed artifact, with an interval | Only descriptive. The registered configurations are mostly inert at search time; the shipped stock gate is post hoc and selection-optimistic. A "win" is likely only if the true gain exceeds about 12 to 16 points.
